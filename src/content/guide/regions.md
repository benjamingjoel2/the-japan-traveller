Japan is usually divided into eight traditional regions on the four main islands, with Okinawa often treated as a ninth for travel planning. The list below runs north to south. Rail times between the main cities are in the [rail-route table](/rail-routes); what is happening in each region month by month is on the [month-by-month page](/seasons).

## Hokkaido

The northern island. Main base: Sapporo. Known for national parks (Daisetsuzan, Shiretoko), deep powder snow in winter (Niseko, Furano), drift ice on the Okhotsk coast in February, and a cooler summer than the rest of the country. It has no formal rainy season. Distances are long; many visitors fly in to New Chitose Airport, though the Hokkaido Shinkansen reaches Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto from Tokyo.

## Tohoku

The north of Honshu: Aomori, Iwate, Akita, Miyagi, Yamagata and Fukushima. Main base: Sendai. Known for onsen towns, mountain scenery, late cherry blossom (Hirosaki, late April), and the big August festivals (Aomori Nebuta, Akita Kanto, Sendai Tanabata). Snowfall is heavy on the Sea of Japan side in winter. Served by the Tohoku Shinkansen.

## Kanto and Tokyo

Tokyo and the surrounding prefectures. Usual point of arrival (Narita and Haneda airports). Day and overnight trips include Nikko, Kamakura and Hakone (Hakone is just over the border in Kanagawa and is the classic Mount Fuji viewing and onsen stop). Winters are dry and clear; summers are hot and humid.

## Chubu and the Alps

Central Honshu, from Nagoya to the Japan Sea coast. Includes the Japan Alps (Kamikochi, Hakuba, the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route), the old post towns of the Nakasendo (Magome, Tsumago), Takayama, Shirakawa-go, Kanazawa, Matsumoto and Mount Fuji's northern side. Ski season in the Northern Alps; hiking season in summer; autumn colour in October.

## Kansai and Kyoto

Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, Kobe, Himeji and the Kii Peninsula. Kyoto is the most visited city for temples, gardens and crafts; Nara for its early temples; Osaka for food. The Kumano Kodo pilgrimage routes run through the Kii Peninsula in Wakayama. Arrival by Kansai International Airport, or by Shinkansen from Tokyo.

## Chugoku

Western Honshu: Hiroshima, Okayama, Tottori, Shimane and Yamaguchi. Main stops are Hiroshima (Peace Memorial Park) and Miyajima (Itsukushima Shrine), reached by the Sanyo Shinkansen. The Sea of Japan coast (Matsue, Izumo) is quieter and slower to reach.

## Shikoku

The smallest of the four main islands. Known for the 88-temple pilgrimage, the Iya Valley, Dogo Onsen in Matsuyama and, via Takamatsu, the Seto Inland Sea art islands such as Naoshima. Reached by rail from Okayama across the Seto Ohashi bridge.

## Kyushu

The southern main island. Bases: Fukuoka (Hakata), Nagasaki, Kumamoto and Kagoshima. Known for onsen towns (Beppu, Yufuin, Kurokawa), active volcanoes (Aso, Sakurajima) and its own food culture. Reached by Shinkansen from Osaka and Hiroshima; the Kyushu Shinkansen continues to Kagoshima.

## Okinawa

The subtropical island chain in the far south. Main base: Naha. Beaches, coral reefs and Ryukyu culture, with a climate distinct from the main islands: cherry blossom in January and February, rainy season in May and June, and the highest exposure to typhoons from summer into autumn. There is no JR line; most visitors fly.
