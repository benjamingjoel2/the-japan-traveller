Izumo and Matsue lie in Shimane Prefecture, on the Sea of Japan side of western Honshu. Izumo is home to the shrine Izumo Taisha. Matsue has a castle with an original keep and sits on the shore of Lake Shinji. This article covers the shrine, the castle, the lake and the trains that link them.

## Izumo Taisha

The Izumo Tourism Association states that the kami enshrined at Izumo Taisha is Okuninushi-no-Okami. JNTO describes Okuninushi as a deity associated with marriage and relationships.

### The four-clap prayer

The usual form of prayer at a shrine is two bows, two claps and one bow (see [temple and shrine etiquette](/articles/temple-and-shrine-etiquette)). Izumo Taisha has its own form. The Izumo Tourism Association gives it as "two bows, four claps, silent prayer, and one more bow (2-4-1)". JNTO's page describes the same sequence: two bows, four claps instead of the usual two, and one bow.

### The buildings

- **Main hall.** According to the tourism association, the present shrine was built in 1744 and is designated a National Treasure. It says the main hall stood 48 metres tall in the 10th century, and is about 24 metres, or half that height, today.
- **Kagura-den.** The tourism association says the Kagura-den hall carries the largest shimenawa, a twisted rope marking a sacred space, in Japan.

### Hours and festivals

The tourism association gives these times:

| Area | Hours |
|---|---|
| Front area of the shrine | About 6:00 to 19:00, every day |
| Formal prayers | 9:15 to 16:30 (reception 8:40 to 16:00) |
| Soga Shrine, behind the main hall | 6:00 to 16:30 |

JNTO says the highlight of the shrine's year is the Kamiari Festival, which lasts about a week and usually falls in November.

### Getting to the shrine

From the Izumo Tourism Association:

- **Bus:** about 25 minutes from JR Izumoshi Station to the Seimon Mae stop. Pay when you get off.
- **Ichibata Electric Railway:** about 25 minutes from Dentetsu Izumoshi Station, changing at Kawato for Izumo Taisha Mae, the final stop. The Japan Rail Pass is not valid on this line.
- **Taxi:** about 20 minutes.

## Matsue Castle

Visit Matsue, the city's tourism site, states that Matsue Castle was completed in 1611 and is one of only 12 remaining original castles in Japan. Its keep was designated a National Treasure in 2015. The castle's own site confirms the keep's National Treasure status. Matsue is one of the five castles with a National Treasure keep listed in [which Japanese castles are original](/questions/which-japanese-castles-are-original).

### Hours and admission

The castle's official fee page gives these hours for the keep (tenshu):

| Period | Hours | Last entry |
|---|---|---|
| 1 April to 30 September | 8:30 to 18:00 | 17:30 |
| 1 October to 31 March | 8:30 to 17:00 | 16:30 |

The surrounding Honmaru grounds open 8:00 to 18:30 from April to September and 8:00 to 17:30 from October to March. Entry to the grounds is free. The castle is open every day of the year, but hours may change for events.

Fees were revised from 1 July 2026 to pay for a large-scale repair of the keep:

| Ticket | Price |
|---|---|
| Adult | 1,200 yen |
| Group of 30 or more | 960 yen per person |
| Elementary and junior high school students | Free |

Holders of a disability certificate and one carer enter free. The ticket office accepts credit cards, transport IC cards and several e-money services. Visitors take off their shoes at the entrance and carry them in a bag provided.

### Getting there

Visit Matsue gives the distance from JR Matsue Station as 2 kilometres, or 10 minutes by Ichibata bus to the Kencho-mae stop, in front of the Shimane Prefectural Office.

## Lake Shinji

Shimane Prefecture's tourism site describes Lake Shinji as a brackish lake that contains some seawater, rich in fish and shellfish, and as Japan's largest source of shijimi clams. It says the sunset view over the lake has been selected as one of Japan's 100 famous landscapes. The lake is about a 10-minute walk from JR Matsue Station.

Visit Matsue recommends a sunset viewing spot south of the Shimane Art Museum, along the lakefront promenade, for the closest view of the small island of Yomegashima. It also suggests Shirakata Park and the museum itself. Visit Matsue publishes a daily sunset index that rates how visible the sunset is likely to be.

## Trains to Izumo and Matsue

### The Sunrise Izumo

The overnight Sunrise Izumo runs between Tokyo and Izumoshi. JR West lists its stops between Okayama and Izumoshi as Kurashiki, Bitchu-Takahashi, Niimi, Yonago, Yasugi, Matsue, Shinji and Izumoshi, noting that stops vary by train. For times, rooms and booking, see [the Sunrise Izumo and Sunrise Seto](/articles/sunrise-izumo-seto-night-train).

### The Limited Express Yakumo

JR West's Yakumo runs from Okayama, a Shinkansen station, to Yonago in Tottori Prefecture and Matsue and Izumoshi in Shimane Prefecture. JR West's page states a travel time of three hours and 30 trains a day. It uses 273 series tilting trains, which run as four or eight cars and have Green Car and ordinary reserved seats. JR West's page does not give a separate time to Matsue. Check JR West's timetable for the train you plan to take. For Okayama itself, see [Okayama and Kurashiki](/articles/okayama-and-kurashiki).

### The Ichibata Electric Railway

The Izumo Tourism Association says the Ichibata Electric Railway was founded in 1912 and its trains first ran in 1914. It links Matsue and Dentetsu Izumoshi, a short walk from JR Izumoshi Station, with Izumo Taisha Mae. Kawato is the transfer station, and most travellers to the shrine change trains there. Bicycles can be taken on board for a small fee. The Japan Rail Pass does not cover the line.

## Planning notes

- At Izumo Taisha the prayer is two bows, four claps, a silent prayer and one bow.
- Matsue Castle's fees were revised on 1 July 2026; adults now pay 1,200 yen.
- The Ichibata line is not covered by the Japan Rail Pass. Budget for the fare.
