Fireworks displays, called hanabi taikai, are a large part of summer in Japan. This article covers three of the best-known: the Sumida River festival in Tokyo, the Nagaoka festival in Niigata and the national competition at Omagari in Akita. It uses each organiser's site, plus JNTO and the Tokyo tourism bureau. All three were held in 2026. As of 7 November 2026, the Sumida and Omagari pages consulted gave only 2026 dates.

## The dates at a glance

| Festival | Place | Latest date (as officially stated) |
|---|---|---|
| Sumida River Fireworks Festival (49th) | Taito and Sumida, Tokyo | Saturday 25 July 2026, 7pm to 8.30pm |
| Nagaoka Fireworks Festival | Nagaoka, Niigata | 2 and 3 August every year (JNTO) |
| National Fireworks Competition (98th) | Daisen, Akita | Saturday 29 August 2026 |

The Nagaoka organiser's own site could not be reached when this article was written, so its details here come from JNTO. Other displays happen during the Tohoku festivals, for example the Aomori Fireworks Festival on the last night of Nebuta. See [Tohoku's summer festivals](/articles/tohoku-summer-festivals).

## Sumida River Fireworks Festival, Tokyo

**History.** GO TOKYO traces the festival to 1733, when a river-opening ceremony at Ryogoku was held for those who had died in the famine and disease of 1732. The organiser says it was renamed the Sumida River Fireworks Festival in 1978, when it was revived. GO TOKYO says the event includes a contest of new firework designs as well as the traditional display.

**2026 festival.** The 49th festival was held on Saturday 25 July 2026, from 7pm to 8.30pm. Fireworks are launched from two sites, the First Venue and the Second Venue, each by a different fireworks company. The organiser's rules are:

- If bad weather prevents the festival, it is cancelled, not postponed.
- The decision is normally made at 8am on the day.
- Sudden changes in the weather can also cause a cancellation. Notices go on the official site and its X account.

**Stations.** The organiser lists these stations:

| Venue | Nearest stations |
|---|---|
| First Venue | Asakusa, Oshiage, Tokyo Skytree, Hikifune |
| Second Venue | Asakusa, Kuramae, Ryogoku, Asakusabashi |

It expects Asakusa Station to be very crowded after the display and asks people to use other stations. GO TOKYO gives the main venue as about 15 minutes' walk from Asakusa Station and the second venue as about 5 minutes from Kuramae Station.

**Seating.** The only seats are the paid citizen sponsor seats (shimin kyosan seki). For 2026 they were sold out. The organiser says there is nowhere to sit and watch other than these seats.

**Rules for spectators.** The organiser asks that visitors:

- do not reserve a spot, or mark roads and parks with tape, chalk or spray (marked items are removed);
- follow one-way pedestrian routes and staff instructions, because of the crowds;
- do not fly drones;
- take rubbish home, or use the designated collection points;
- prepare for heatstroke and sudden heavy downpours.

## Nagaoka Fireworks Festival, Niigata

JNTO says the festival is held every year on 2 and 3 August, on the banks of the Shinano River near Ote Bridge. It began in 1946 as a reconstruction event to mourn those who died in the Second World War. JNTO says as many as a million people watch it each year.

The highlight is the Reconstruction Phoenix display. JNTO says it includes about five minutes of fireworks set to the song "Jupiter" by Ayaka Hirahara, which was written for the recovery of the Chuetsu area after a major earthquake.

**Seating and crowds.** According to JNTO:

- Paid "Grand Tickets" are sold, and JNTO recommends reserving them as early as May.
- The viewing area along the riverbank is free, but it becomes extremely crowded. JNTO advises arriving hours before the start to find a place.

**Getting there.** Take the Joetsu Shinkansen to Nagaoka Station. The venue is about 20 minutes' walk from the station. JNTO says parking is very limited, most festival car parks charge a fee, and traffic is heavy after the show.

## Omagari: the National Fireworks Competition, Akita

The National Fireworks Competition is held at Omagari Hanabi Park on the bank of the Omono River in Daisen, Akita Prefecture. The organiser says it was first held in 1910. Japan's top fireworks makers compete for the Prime Minister's Prize, which the organiser calls the highest honour in Japanese fireworks. In 2026, 28 companies took part.

The organiser says it is the only competition in Japan with daytime fireworks. These use smoke rather than light. The evening programme has three categories: regulated shells, freestyle shells and "creative hanabi".

**2026 schedule.** The 98th competition was held on Saturday 29 August 2026:

| | Time |
|---|---|
| Gates open | 3pm |
| Daytime fireworks | 5.10pm to 6pm |
| Evening fireworks | 7pm to 9.30pm |

If the weather is bad, the organiser decides a new date based on the forecast. The official site has a ticket section with several seating options, but the overview page does not give prices.

**Getting there.** According to the organiser:

- The nearest station is JR Omagari Station, about 30 minutes' walk (about 2 km) from the heart of the venue.
- Extra trains run on the day to reduce congestion.
- The area around the venue becomes a vehicle-restricted zone, and traffic jams often begin around 3pm and can last until late at night.

The organiser also holds other events in the year. In 2026 it listed an Autumn Chapter on 3 October.

## Planning notes

- Paid seats at Sumida sold out for 2026. Check the official sites in spring for 2027 sales.
- Mid-August is Obon, a busy domestic travel period. Nagaoka (2 and 3 August) falls just before it. See [peak holiday periods](/articles/peak-holiday-periods).
- Expect heat. Sumida's organiser asks visitors to prepare for heatstroke and sudden downpours. See also [Is Japan good in the rainy season?](/questions/is-japan-good-in-the-rainy-season)
