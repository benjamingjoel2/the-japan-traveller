Japan's parliament enacted a law on 29 May 2026 that creates JESTA, the Japan Electronic System for Travel Authorization. The Immigration Services Agency says the act was promulgated on 5 June 2026. Once JESTA starts, travellers who do not need a visa for a short stay will have to be approved online before they travel. It is not in operation yet, and nobody needs to apply for it today.

## What the new law sets up

The government decided to amend the immigration law to create JESTA on 10 March 2026, according to Nippon.com's timeline for that month. The Diet passed the bill on 29 May 2026, during its 221st special session.

The Immigration Services Agency's summary of the act describes how the system will work:

- **Who needs it.** Foreign nationals who can enter Japan without a visa and plan a short stay for tourism or similar purposes. It also covers passengers arriving on designated cruise ships who want to land for sightseeing, and some travellers entering Japan briefly to change ships or planes.
- **How often.** A new authorisation is needed for every new entry into Japan.
- **What it replaces.** Nothing. Travellers who are not visa-exempt still need a visa. Under the act, a foreign national with neither an authorisation nor a visa is not allowed to enter.
- **Airlines and shipping lines.** When they issue a ticket, carriers must report the booking name and other details to the agency by a set time. If the agency tells a carrier that a passenger should not be allowed into Japan, the carrier must not carry that person. Breaches can lead to an administrative fine.
- **Fee.** A fee will be charged, with the amount to be set later by Cabinet Order.

The agency gives two aims: to stop people who intend to overstay before they travel, and to speed up arrival checks. For approved visa-exempt visitors who meet the entry conditions, the landing stamp in the passport will be dropped. The summary says walk-through gates will be used in practice.

## When it starts

No start date has been set. The Immigration Services Agency says the JESTA provisions take effect on a date to be fixed by Cabinet Order, no later than 31 March 2029.

The Japan Tourism Agency describes the law as providing for JESTA to be introduced during fiscal 2028. Japan's fiscal year runs from April to March, so that means April 2028 to March 2029.

## What it means for travellers now

- JESTA is not running yet, and the government's stated target is fiscal 2028. For now, the current entry rules on our [entry basics page](/guide/entry-basics) apply.
- If you hold a passport from a visa-exempt country and plan a trip from 2028 onwards, expect to apply online before you travel. Expect to apply again for each separate trip.
- Your airline or shipping line will pass your booking details to the immigration agency. It must not carry you if the agency says you should not be allowed in.

The Japan Tourism Agency has also flagged a knock-on effect. Tax-free shops currently use the landing stamp in the passport to check that a buyer qualifies. JESTA will remove that stamp for some visitors, so the agency has asked for the tax-free checks to be reviewed. Our article on [tax-free shopping](/articles/tax-free-shopping-changes) explains how the current system works.

## What we don't know yet

The fee amount, the start date, the application website and the processing time have not been announced. The same act raises the caps on fees for residence permits. Those changes affect foreign residents, not short-term visitors. We will update this page when the government publishes JESTA's operating details.
