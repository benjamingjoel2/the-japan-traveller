Tokyo's accommodation tax will change from a flat charge to a percentage of the room price for stays from 1 April 2027. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government announced on 30 June 2026 that the Minister of Internal Affairs and Communications had consented to the change that day. The new tax is 3% of the room charge, and it will apply to more types of lodging than before.

## What changes on 1 April 2027

The Bureau of Taxation sets out the old and new rules. Both are charged per person per night.

| | Until 31 March 2027 | From 1 April 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | Guests at hotels and ryokan | Guests at hotels, ryokan, simple lodgings and minpaku (private lodging) |
| Exempt below | ¥10,000 per person per night | ¥13,000 per person per night |
| Tax | ¥100 for ¥10,000 to under ¥15,000; ¥200 for ¥15,000 or more | 3% of the room charge |

The bureau says the charge used for the tax is the room-only price, without meals. The accommodation operator collects the tax from the guest.

The new rule applies to stays from 1 April 2027. The press release does not say how bookings made before that date are treated, so check with your accommodation if you have already booked.

## What it means for travellers

- **Budget and mid-range stays.** If your room-only rate is under ¥13,000 per person per night, you will pay no accommodation tax. Today the exemption ends at ¥10,000.
- **Higher-priced stays.** The tax now rises with the price. Under the flat system, ¥200 was the most anyone paid. As an example from the 3% rate, a room-only charge of ¥20,000 per person per night would carry ¥600 of tax.
- **Minpaku and simple lodgings.** These have been outside Tokyo's accommodation tax until now. From April 2027 their guests pay it too. The bureau says new collectors could begin registering from 1 July 2026.

The bureau says the higher threshold is meant to keep school trips exempt. It says that, in its survey, more than 70% of school-trip rates fell below the new line. It also expects around 30% of private lodging facilities to be taxable.

## How the change came about

Tokyo was the first local government in Japan to introduce an accommodation tax, in October 2002. The press release gives the steps behind this revision. The Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly passed the amended ordinance in March 2026. The minister consented on 30 June 2026, and the ordinance was promulgated on 1 July 2026. The bureau says the tax pays for measures to strengthen Tokyo's appeal as an international city and to promote tourism.

According to The Japan News, Tokyo expects the switch to more than double its revenue from the tax, to ¥19 billion. The same report says some people in the hotel industry are concerned it could reduce hotel use.

## Other accommodation taxes

Tokyo is one of many places that now charge this kind of tax. The Japan News reported that, as of 1 June 2026, 45 local governments had introduced one: seven prefectures and 38 municipalities. Kyoto's revised rates from March 2026 are covered in [getting around Kyoto](/articles/getting-around-kyoto). Separately from local taxes, the national departure tax rose to ¥3,000 in July 2026; see [entry basics](/guide/entry-basics).
