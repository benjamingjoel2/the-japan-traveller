Nara was Japan's capital in the eighth century, and its main temples, shrine and park sit close together east of the city centre. This article covers the World Heritage listing, Todai-ji, Kasuga Taisha, Kofuku-ji, the deer of Nara Park, and the train options from Kyoto and Osaka.

## The World Heritage listing

Visit Nara, the prefecture's tourism site, says Nara was the capital of Japan from 710 to 784. Kasuga Taisha states that the "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara" were designated a joint UNESCO World Heritage Site in December 1998.

Visit Nara lists eight parts of the city in the listing:

- Former Site of the Imperial Palace at Heijokyo
- Todai-ji
- Kofuku-ji
- Yakushi-ji
- Toshodai-ji
- Gango-ji
- Kasuga Taisha
- Kasugayama Primeval Forest

Todai-ji, Kofuku-ji, Kasuga Taisha and the primeval forest lie in and around Nara Park. Yakushi-ji, Toshodai-ji and the palace site are further west and need a bus or train.

## Todai-ji

Todai-ji's Daibutsu-den (Great Buddha Hall) houses the Great Buddha. Visit Nara gives the statue's height as 15 metres and says it was completed in 752. You enter through the Nandaimon (Great Southern Gate), whose guardian figures Visit Nara credits to the carvers Unkei and Kaikei.

According to Todai-ji, the hall was destroyed by fire in 1180 and again in 1567. The present building dates from the Edo period. It is seven bays wide, against eleven for the original, but keeps the original height and depth. Todai-ji gives its dimensions as 57.012 m wide, 50.480 m long and 48.742 m high, and describes it as the largest wooden structure in the world. Both the hall and the statue are National Treasures.

## Kasuga Taisha and Kofuku-ji

Kasuga Taisha says its gods were enshrined on 9 November 768. The shrine mentions its 3,000 donated lanterns and gives its opening hours as 7:00 to 17:00. Visit Nara notes that it sits on the opposite side of the park from Todai-ji, linked to the Nigatsu-do hall by a path, and that its wisteria blooms in May.

Kofuku-ji was established with the new capital in 710, according to Visit Nara, and was the family temple of the Fujiwara clan. Its five-storey pagoda is a landmark of the city. JNTO gives its height as 50 metres and calls it the second tallest five-storey pagoda in Japan. Visit Nara lists the National Treasure Museum, which holds the six-armed Ashura figure, at ¥900 for adults, with hours of 9:00 to 17:00 (last admission 16:30). The temple is about 5 minutes on foot from Kintetsu-Nara Station.

## Nara Park and the deer

The deer are wild animals and are designated a national natural monument, according to the Nara Park Office of Nara Prefecture. Its July 2026 survey counted 1,687 deer: 411 males, 990 females and 286 young.

Feeding rules, as set out by the Nara Park Office:

- Feeding deer is prohibited in Nara Park. The one exception is shika senbei, the deer crackers sold in the park.
- Shika senbei are made of rice bran and wheat flour, with no sugar or salt.
- Never give the deer anything else. The office names sweets, bread, vegetables and leftovers, which can make the deer ill or cause accidents.

Safety guidance from the same office:

- Deer can charge, butt with their antlers, bite or kick, and people are injured each year in these ways.
- Take particular care in spring, when females with young are protective, and in autumn, the males' rutting season, when they are more aggressive.
- Do not let children approach the deer on their own.
- Keep dogs on a lead and away from the deer.
- Hold bags, maps and leaflets securely. Deer may eat plastic bags and paper, or catch bags on their antlers.
- There are no rubbish bins in the park. Take your rubbish with you, because deer that eat litter can die.
- Do not hit or chase the deer.

## Getting there from Kyoto and Osaka

Two railways serve Nara. JR trains arrive at JR Nara Station. Kintetsu trains arrive at Kintetsu-Nara Station, which is closer to the park. JNTO gives the walk to the park as 5 minutes from Kintetsu-Nara and 20 minutes from JR Nara.

Journey times as stated by the Nara City Tourism Association:

| From | Railway and service | Time |
|---|---|---|
| JR Kyoto Station | JR Miyakoji Rapid | about 45 minutes |
| Kintetsu Kyoto Station | Kintetsu limited express (surcharge) | 35 minutes |
| JR Osaka Station | JR Yamatoji Rapid (direct) | about 50 minutes |
| JR Tennoji Station | JR | 36 minutes |
| Osaka Namba Station | Kintetsu (direct) | about 40 minutes |

The JR route from Kyoto is in the site's [rail-route table](/rail-routes). Kintetsu is a private railway, not part of JR, so the Japan Rail Pass does not cover it. See [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass) for how the private railways fit in. ## Planning notes

- From Kyoto, Kintetsu gets you closer to the park. From JR Kyoto Station, the JR line is the option covered by the rail pass.
- Visit Nara notes that Yakushi-ji, Toshodai-ji and the Heijo palace site are outside the park, so a day limited to the park area will not cover all eight World Heritage parts.
- Buy shika senbei only if you plan to feed the deer, and keep them out of sight until you do.
