The walk between Magome and Tsumago is the best-known stretch of the Nakasendo, an old highway through the Kiso Valley in central Japan. This article covers the road's history, the walk itself, baggage delivery between the two towns and how to reach them by train and bus. The facts here come from the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO). The local tourist offices' own websites could not be checked for this article, so confirm bus times and baggage details locally.

## The Nakasendo

JNTO describes the Nakasendo as a road linking Kyoto and Edo (now Tokyo). It was an important trade route from the early 1600s until the late 1800s. The roadway dates from the seventh century. It was first called the Kisokaido and became the Nakasendo in the early 1600s. Locals call the Kiso Valley stretch the Kisoji.

The road had 69 post towns, spaced roughly every 10 km, which gave lodging, food and entertainment to merchants, samurai, monks and other travellers. Most of the highway has since been modernised, but parts of the Kiso Valley section remain largely unchanged.

- **Magome** is in Nakatsugawa, Gifu Prefecture, at the southern entrance to the Kiso Valley. JNTO describes Magome-juku as a living post town with about 400 years of history.
- **Tsumago** was the 42nd station on the Nakasendo. It is in Nagiso, Nagano Prefecture. In 1968 the local council chose to preserve its main street. Cars are banned from the street during the day, and telephone and power lines are hidden.

JNTO calls the two towns among the best-preserved stops on the route.

## The walk

| Detail | What JNTO states |
|---|---|
| Distance | About 9 km (one JNTO feature gives 8 to 9 km) |
| Walking time | Different JNTO pages give 2 to 2.5 hours, 2.5 to 3 hours and about 3 hours |
| Direction | Many walkers start in Magome and go north, as more of the route is downhill |
| Prefectures | The walk crosses from Gifu Prefecture into Nagano Prefecture |

JNTO describes the walk as relatively easy. It passes through cypress forest and farming communities, with shrines, waterfalls, bamboo groves, terraced rice fields and a teahouse rest stop along the way. One forest path uses large stones laid in the early 1600s. Signs are in English and Japanese.

The JNTO travellers' blog notes that bear bells hang along the trail and encourages walkers to carry one. JNTO also suggests staying a night in Tsumago to see the town after day visitors have left.

Beyond Tsumago, JNTO describes a 23 km continuation to Suhara through the post towns of Midono and Nojiri. Trains run along the valley, so you can skip ahead if you tire.

## Baggage delivery

JNTO states that baggage delivery is available between Magome and Tsumago, so you can send bags ahead and walk with a day pack. JNTO does not give the season, hours or price. Ask at the tourist information office in Magome or Tsumago before you set out.

For longer journeys, you can also send bags between hotels by courier (takkyubin). See the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass) for more on travelling with luggage.

## Getting there

Both towns are reached from the JR Chuo Main Line, which runs from Nagoya through the Kiso Valley. The JR Shinano limited express serves the line. From Tokyo, JNTO gives the route as the Tokaido Shinkansen to Nagoya, then the Shinano.

**To Magome (start of the walk)**

1. Take the Shinano limited express from JR Nagoya to JR Nakatsugawa. JNTO gives about 50 to 55 minutes.
2. Take a local bus from Nakatsugawa Station to Magome, about 25 minutes. The bus stops at the foot of the cobbled road that climbs through the town.

**From Tsumago (end of the walk)**

1. Take a local bus from Tsumago to JR Nagiso Station, about 10 minutes. JNTO notes that buses run infrequently; taxis are available.
2. From Nagiso, limited express trains reach JR Nagoya in about 1 hour.

JNTO also notes that you can take a local train from Nakatsugawa to Nagiso, where buses run to both Magome and Tsumago, and that buses link the two post towns. Highway buses to Magome are another option.

## Before you go

- **Check the buses.** JNTO gives no timetables. Bus services are infrequent, so plan your connections at both ends before the day.
- **Confirm baggage delivery** with a tourist information office, as the service details are not published by JNTO.
- **Book a night in Tsumago or Magome** if you want to see the towns when they are quiet. See [how far ahead to book a ryokan](/questions/how-far-ahead-should-i-book-a-ryokan).
- **Plan the wider route.** The walk fits into a central Japan itinerary between Tokyo, Nagoya and Kyoto; see [suggested routes](/guide/routes) and [regions](/guide/regions).
- **Seasons.** JNTO pages do not give seasonal closures for the walk. For general weather by month, see [seasons](/guide/seasons).
