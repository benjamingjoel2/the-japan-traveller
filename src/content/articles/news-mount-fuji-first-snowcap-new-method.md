The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) recorded the first snowcap of the season on Mount Fuji on 30 October 2026. It is the first time the date has been set by JMA's new analysis method rather than by staff at the Kofu Local Meteorological Office looking at the mountain. JMA announced the change on 31 July 2026. It also now publishes the current snow state at each mountain's summit, updated every hour.

## What changed

Until the 2025–26 winter, JMA staff recorded a first snowcap by eye for 44 mountains across Japan. Under the new system JMA uses what it calls "analysed snow depth". This combines data from its AMeDAS automatic weather stations, weather radar and the Himawari weather satellite. JMA says this lets it judge summit snow even when the top is in cloud or at night.

The new page on JMA's website lists all 44 mountains. For each it shows the first snowcap date of the season and the latest snow state at the summit. The page says it updates at about 25 minutes past each hour. The season runs from August to the following July.

According to Weathernews, the Kofu office could not see Mount Fuji on the morning of 30 October. Under the old method, a first snowcap could therefore have been recorded later.

## This year's dates

First snowcap dates on JMA's page for selected mountains, as of 7 November 2026:

| Mountain | First snowcap, 2026 |
|---|---|
| Asahidake | 2 October |
| Hakkoda | 22 October |
| Tateyama | 27 October |
| Zao | 28 October |
| Hakusan | 28 October |
| Mount Fuji | 30 October |
| Kai-Komagatake | 2 November |

Several mountains on the list, including Ibukiyama, Hira-san, Sefurisan and Yufudake, had no first snowcap recorded yet.

## Can it be compared with past years?

Not directly. The Kofu office says it ended its record series for Mount Fuji and Kai-Komagatake with the 2025–26 season because the method changed. Weathernews also noted that there is no normal date or year-on-year comparison under the new method.

For reference, the final records under the old method at Kofu were:

| Mount Fuji, old method (Kofu, by eye) | Date |
|---|---|
| Last season (2025) | 23 October |
| Normal | 2 October |
| Earliest on record | 9 August 2008 |
| Latest on record | 7 November 2024 |

## What this means for travellers

For travellers who want to see Mount Fuji with snow on its summit, JMA's page is now a free, hourly check on whether the top is snow-covered, whatever the weather at the time. It does not tell you whether the mountain will be visible from the ground; cloud can still hide the summit. JMA's winter outlook expects fewer sunny days than normal on the Pacific side this winter (see [JMA expects a mild winter for most of Japan in 2026–27](/articles/news-winter-2026-27-outlook)).
