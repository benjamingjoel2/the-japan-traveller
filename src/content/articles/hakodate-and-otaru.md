Hakodate and Otaru are two of Hokkaido's historic port towns. This article covers the main sights in each and how to reach them by rail, using the City of Hakodate, the Otaru Tourism Association, JR Hokkaido and the ropeway operator.

## Getting to Hakodate

The Hokkaido Shinkansen ends at Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto, not in central Hakodate. The City of Hakodate's transportation page says the Hakodate Liner shuttle runs to Hakodate Station in Hakodate city, in approximately 20 minutes, for 440 yen one way. It runs to match the Shinkansen timetable.

The city's access page gives about 4 hours 30 minutes from Tokyo Station to Hakodate Station, including the change at Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto. From Sapporo, it gives about 3 hours 30 minutes to Hakodate by JR limited express (Hokuto). For Shinkansen times and JR Pass coverage, see [rail routes](/rail-routes) and [Rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

**Getting around.** The city sells a 1-day tram pass (adult 800 yen, child 400 yen) and a 24-hour tram pass sold online only (adult 1,130 yen, child 570 yen). A combined tram and Hakodate Bus 1-day pass costs 1,600 yen for adults. The city notes that prices are subject to change.

## Mount Hakodate and the ropeway

The City of Hakodate notes that the night view from Mount Hakodate has a three-star rating in the Michelin Green Guide. The ropeway takes about three minutes from the base station to the summit. The city gives the gondola capacity as 125 people, with departures every 15 minutes, or every 5 to 10 minutes at busy times.

Hours and fares, as published by the operator:

| Period | First departure | Last up | Last down |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 April to 30 September | 10am | 9.30pm | 10pm |
| 1 October to 19 April | 10am | 8.30pm | 9pm |

| Ticket | Adult | Child |
|---|---|---|
| Round trip | 1,800 yen | 900 yen |
| One way | 1,200 yen | 600 yen |

Children up to two ride free. The operator says hours may change with the weather. The ropeway closes each year for legally required inspections. In 2026 the closure ran from 20 October to 8 November.

**Getting to the summit.** The city lists three ways from JR Hakodate Station:

- The Mt. Hakodate Climbing Bus (Route 1) to the summit, about 30 minutes, 700 yen one way. It ran from 17 April to 30 September and from 20 October to 8 November 2026, and does not run from 9 November 2026 to 12 April 2027.
- The ropeway shuttle bus to the base station, then the ropeway.
- The tram to Jujigai, then a steep walk of about 10 minutes to the base station.

The summit road is closed to private cars in the evenings in spring to autumn. It is closed to all traffic in winter, from 11am on 9 November 2026 to 11am on 12 April 2027.

## Goryokaku, the morning market and Motomachi

**Goryokaku.** JNTO describes Goryokaku as a five-sided star fort, the first Western-style fortification in Japan, completed in 1864. The Hakodate Magistrate's Office stands at its centre. The original was destroyed in 1871, and a reconstruction reopened in 2010. The Hokkaido Tourism Organization says Goryokaku was the site of the final battle of the Boshin War and that the park has around 1,500 cherry trees. It gives park hours of 5am to 7pm, or 5am to 6pm from November to March. The Hakodate Goryokaku Festival is held every May.

The observation deck of Goryokaku Tower shows the whole star shape. The City of Hakodate gives access as the tram from JR Hakodate Station to Goryokaku-koen-mae, then a 13-minute walk to the tower.

**Hakodate Morning Market.** The city says the market is about a minute's walk from JR Hakodate Station and opens early. At Ekini Market you can fish for live squid and eat it as sashimi. Nearby Donburi Yokocho Alley has about 20 shops.

**Motomachi.** The city describes Motomachi as an area of hilly streets and cobblestone roads at the foot of Mount Hakodate, looking over the bay area. Its mix of Japanese and Western influences dates from when Hakodate became the first port in Hokkaido to open and many foreign residents lived there. JNTO notes its foreign churches and former consulate buildings. The city's bay area, with the Kanemori Red Brick Warehouse on the quay, is reached from the Jujigai tram stop.

## Otaru Canal

The Otaru Tourism Association gives this description of the canal:

- It was completed in 1923. It was built by reclaiming land offshore rather than digging inland, so it curves gently.
- In 1986, after a dispute of more than ten years over filling it in, part of the canal was filled. Half its width became a road, and a walkway and small gardens were laid out.
- It is 1,140 m long. It is 20 m wide along the road and keeps its original 40 m width in the northern section, the North Canal.
- The walkway has 63 gas lamps. They are lit from sunset to midnight. During the Otaru Snow Light Path festival they are lit from 3pm.
- The canal is about a 10-minute walk from JR Otaru Station.

A 2022 association page also gives the warehouse light-up as sunset to 10.30pm all year.

**Winter 2026-27.** The association lists the Blue Canal illumination on the canal between Asakusabashi and Chuobashi from 1 November 2026 to 31 January 2027, lit from sunset to 10.30pm. Otaru Canal Candle Night runs from 18 to 27 December 2026 at Otaru Port Square, about 10 minutes' walk from JR Otaru Station, with candles lit from 4pm to 8pm. For the Otaru Snow Light Path in February, see [Hokkaido in winter](/articles/hokkaido-in-winter).

## Getting to Otaru

The Otaru Tourism Association gives the shortest time from Sapporo to Otaru as 32 minutes by the Rapid Airport train. That train stops at Otaru-Chikko, Minami-Otaru and Otaru. For 520 yen extra you can use a U-seat reserved seat.

JR Hokkaido gives a fastest time of 73 minutes from New Chitose Airport to Otaru, and says some Airport trains run through to Otaru without a change. Reserved seats are in car 4 only and need both a basic fare ticket and a reserved seat ticket.

## Planning notes

- Both towns can be reached by JR from Sapporo, so they can be combined on one trip. Check times with JR Hokkaido.
- If the ropeway is your reason for visiting Hakodate in late October or early November, check the inspection dates on the operator's site first.
- Snow and road closures change Mount Hakodate access in winter. See [Hokkaido in winter](/articles/hokkaido-in-winter) for advice on rail disruption.
