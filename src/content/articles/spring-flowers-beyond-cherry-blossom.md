Cherry blossom is only one of Japan's spring flower seasons. Plum blossom comes before it, wisteria follows it, and hydrangeas flower in early summer during the rainy season. This article covers one well-known place for each, using the dates and rules the organisers publish.

## The seasons at a glance

| Flower | Example | Timing (as stated by official sources) |
|---|---|---|
| Plum (ume) | Kairakuen and Kodokan, Mito | Mito Ume Matsuri, 11 February to 22 March 2027 |
| Wisteria (fuji) | Ashikaga Flower Park, Tochigi | Great Wisteria Festival, 11 April to 20 May in 2026 |
| Wisteria | Kawachi Fujien, Kitakyushu | Ticket period from about 20 April to about 6 May each year |
| Hydrangea (ajisai) | Meigetsu-in, Kamakura | Around June |
| Hydrangea | Hakone Tozan Railway | From around mid-June, moving uphill until mid-July |

Bloom timing moves with the weather each year. For cherry blossom itself, see [Seasons in Japan](/guide/seasons) and [the best week for cherry blossom in Kyoto](/questions/best-week-for-cherry-blossom-in-kyoto).

## Plum blossom in Mito

Mito, in Ibaraki Prefecture, holds the Mito Ume Matsuri (plum festival) each year at two sites: Kairakuen garden and the Kodokan.

- **Dates.** The Mito tourism association lists the 131st festival from 11 February to 22 March 2027. The Ibaraki prefectural tourism site gave the same calendar dates, 11 February to 22 March, for the 2026 festival.
- **The trees.** The Mito tourism association gives about 3,000 plum trees at Kairakuen and about 800 at the Kodokan. The Ibaraki site says the trees flower in late winter and early spring, earlier and for longer than cherry blossom.
- **Admission.** The festival page lists Kairakuen at 500 yen for adults and 250 yen for children and people aged 70 and over. The Kodokan is 420 yen for adults and 210 yen for children and people aged 70 and over. Fees may differ during special events.
- **Opening.** Neither site closes on any day during the festival.

The Ibaraki site recommends buying tickets online, because advance and fast-pass tickets let visitors skip the queue. It also mentions the Japan Umeshu Festival, a plum liqueur event usually held for three days in early March on the grounds of Tokiwa Shrine, next to Kairakuen.

## Wisteria at Ashikaga Flower Park

Ashikaga Flower Park in Tochigi Prefecture holds "A Tale of the Wisteria", its Great Wisteria Festival, each spring. The 2026 festival ran from 11 April to 20 May, with the wisteria lit up in the evening from 15 April. The park says it has over 350 wisteria trees, along with about 5,000 azaleas, and a Great Wisteria over 160 years old on a trellis covering 600 tatami mats.

The park gives this order of flowering:

| Variety | When it flowers |
|---|---|
| Pale pink wisteria | Mid to late April |
| Great Wisteria and double-flowered purple (Yae-kokuryu) | Late April to early May |
| White wisteria (80 m tunnel) | Early May |
| Laburnum (yellow) | Early to mid May |

**Admission changes with the flowers.** In 2026, adults paid between 1,200 and 2,300 yen and children between 600 and 1,200 yen, depending on the state of the bloom. Evening tickets went on sale from 5.30pm. Opening hours also changed through the festival. They were longest from 18 April to 6 May, at 7am to 9pm.

**Getting there.** The park's own station is Ashikaga Flower Park on the JR Ryomo Line. The park advises arriving early because the station is very crowded at departure times, and topping up IC cards in advance.

## Wisteria at Kawachi Fujien

Kawachi Fujien is a wisteria garden in Yahatahigashi-ku, Kitakyushu, in Fukuoka Prefecture. Its site lists an 80 m wisteria tunnel (completed 1977), a wisteria dome (1990) and a 110 m tunnel (1990, improved 1997).

**Advance tickets only in wisteria season.** The garden has used a reservation ticket system since 2016 to reduce traffic jams. During the wisteria season no tickets are sold at the gate. Visitors buy a ticket in advance on the official site or at convenience stores in Japan (Seven-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson and Ministop). The rules on the site are:

- An adult ticket costs 600 yen and counts towards an entrance fee of 1,600 yen.
- The entrance fee changes with the flowering, so the amount due at the gate can vary.
- Tickets are for a time slot between 8am and 6pm. The last slot must be used by 5.20pm. There is no night lighting.
- Tickets cannot be cancelled, and the date or time cannot be changed after purchase.
- One adult (junior high school age and over) can bring up to two primary-school-age or younger children free.

The ticket period runs from about 20 April to about 6 May each year. For 2026 it was provisionally set at 18 April to 6 May. After the wisteria season the garden closes for pruning and disinfection. As of November 2026, the 2027 sales schedule had not been announced.

## Hydrangeas in Kamakura and Hakone

Hydrangeas flower during the rainy season. For what that season means for travel, see [Is Japan good in the rainy season?](/questions/is-japan-good-in-the-rainy-season)

**Kamakura.** The Kanagawa prefectural tourism site says Meigetsu-in has about 2,500 hydrangeas, all of one blue colour, known as "Meigetsu-in Blue". It calls the temple "The Temple of Hydrangeas". Many visitors come around June, when the temple opens 8.30am to 5pm. In other months it opens 9am to 4pm. The nearest station is Kita-Kamakura on the JR Yokosuka Line. Hasedera lists hydrangeas among its June flowers and opens 8am to 5.30pm from April to June, with last entry at 5pm. Admission, walking times and Kamakura's visitor guidelines are in the [Kamakura day trip](/articles/kamakura-day-trip) article.

**Hakone Tozan Railway.** The Hakone Navi travel site, which covers the railway, says hydrangeas along the line begin flowering around mid-June. Because the line climbs, the best viewing moves gradually uphill with the altitude until mid-July. The railway is part of the [Hakone loop](/articles/hakone-loop).
