On 26 July 2026 the "Ancient Capitals of Asuka and Fujiwara" in southern Nara Prefecture were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. In a message issued the same day, Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae welcomed the listing as Japan's 27th World Heritage property.

## What was inscribed

According to the Prime Minister's Office, the property is an archaeological ensemble of the capitals of the early Japanese state, which took shape between the 6th and 8th centuries. It has 19 component parts, among them the Asuka Palace Site and the Fujiwara Palace Site. The statement says the sites show how a centralised state formed through the move between two successive capitals.

The joint promotion site run by Nara Prefecture, Kashihara City, Sakurai City and Asuka Village lists all 19 parts. They fall into three groups:

- **Palaces and related sites:** Asuka Palace Site, Asuka Pond Garden Site, Asuka Water Clock Site, Sakafuneishi Ritual Site and Fujiwara Palace Site.
- **Temple sites:** Asuka-dera, Tachibana-dera, Yamada-dera, Kawara-dera, Hinokuma-dera, Daikandaiji and Motoyakushiji.
- **Mounded tombs:** Ishibutai, Shobuike, Kengoshizuka, the tomb of Emperor Temmu and Empress Jito, Nakaoyama, Kitora and Takamatsuzuka.

Most parts are in Asuka Village. Fujiwara Palace Site, Motoyakushiji and Shobuike are in Kashihara City, and Yamada-dera is in Sakurai City. Daikandaiji is listed with an address in both Asuka and Kashihara.

This is a separate listing from the "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara" in Nara city, which our [Nara day trip](/articles/nara-day-trip) article covers.

## What visitors will find

Much of the property is archaeological. Many parts are excavated sites, foundations or mounded tombs rather than standing buildings. The promotion site notes, for example, that some remains at the Asuka-dera site are exposed above ground, and that the Tachibana-dera site is still used as a monastery.

Two of the tombs have painted interiors that visitors cannot see in place. The promotion site says the Kitora murals have been removed for conservation and are kept under strict safekeeping. Nippon.com, in a feature published on 28 July 2026, reports that the Takamatsuzuka murals are kept at a nearby conservation facility. The same feature says visitors can look into the stone chamber of the Shobuike tomb, and that Kengoshizuka was restored in 2022.

The promotion site lists ten museums and visitor facilities around the area, including:

- Nara Prefectural Manyo Culture Museum (Asuka)
- Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, Asuka Museum (Asuka)
- Takamatsuzuka Mural Museum (Asuka)
- Kitora Tumulus Mural Experience Hall "Shijin no Yakata" (Asuka)
- Nara Prefectural Kashihara Archaeological Institute Museum (Kashihara)
- Fujiwara-kyu Site Materials Room, Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties (Kashihara)

The page does not give opening hours or admission fees for these facilities. Check each one before you go.

## Getting there and around

The promotion site's sample route names three stations: Kintetsu Asuka Station, Sakurai Station (Kintetsu and JR) and Kintetsu Yamato-Yagi Station. It gives these walking times between central Asuka sites:

| Leg | Walking time | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| Manyo Culture Museum to Sakafuneishi site | about 2 minutes | 190 m |
| Sakafuneishi site to Asuka Palace Site | about 5 minutes | 450 m |
| Asuka Palace Site to Asuka Pond Garden site | about 4 minutes | 350 m |
| Asuka Pond Garden site to Asuka-dera site | about 8 minutes | 700 m |
| Asuka-dera site to Asuka Water Clock site | about 5 minutes | 450 m |
| Asuka Water Clock site to Fujiwara Palace Site | about 30 minutes | 2.5 km |

The same route uses a bus of about 22 minutes from the station end, and a bus of about 20 minutes from the Fujiwara Palace Site to Yamato-Yagi. The parts are spread across three municipalities, so check bus times before you set out.

## What we don't know yet

Most visitor pages on the promotion site are in Japanese, and the English pages do not list hours, fees or seasonal closures for individual sites. The site's news list shows new pamphlets and maps published in October 2026, but we could not confirm whether English versions exist. We found no official figures on visitor numbers since the listing, and no new reservation or entry rules at any of the 19 sites.
