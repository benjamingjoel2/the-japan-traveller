Hiroshima and the island of Miyajima are usually visited together, and both hold World Heritage sites. This article covers the Peace Memorial Park and Museum, the Genbaku Dome, Itsukushima Shrine, the two ferries from Miyajimaguchi, and the tax charged on entering Miyajima.

## Peace Memorial Museum

The Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum is at 1-2 Nakajima-cho, Naka-ku, Hiroshima. The museum says it "conveys to the world the horrors and the inhumane nature of nuclear weapons."

Opening hours, as stated by the museum:

| Months | Hours |
|---|---|
| March to July | 7:30 to 19:00 |
| August | 7:30 to 20:00 (until 21:00 on 5 and 6 August) |
| September to November | 7:30 to 19:00 |
| December to February | 7:30 to 18:00 |

The museum is closed on 30 and 31 December and for a period in mid-February.

Admission is ¥200 for adults (including college students) and ¥100 for high school students. Junior high school students and younger enter free.

Entry rules stated by the museum:

- An online reservation is required to enter between 7:30 and 8:30, and during an evening slot that varies by month. From March to July and from September to November the evening slot is 17:30 to 18:30. From December to February it is 16:30 to 17:30.
- Web tickets can sell out before the last entry time.
- The museum may temporarily restrict admission when it is crowded.

From Hiroshima Station, the museum lists streetcar Line 1 (for Hiroshima Port) to Fukuro-machi, or Line 6 (for Eba) or Line 2 (for Miyajima) to Genbaku Domu Mae, taking about 25 minutes. Bus 24 to Heiwa kinen koen takes about 20 minutes.

## The Genbaku Dome

The dome is the ruin of the Hiroshima Prefectural Commercial Exhibition Hall, completed in April 1915 to a design by the Czech architect Jan Letzel, according to the City of Hiroshima. The city says the UNESCO World Heritage Committee, meeting in Merida, Mexico, decided on 5 December 1996 to add the dome to the World Heritage List. The inscribed name is "Hiroshima Peace Memorial (Genbaku Dome)". The Agency for Cultural Affairs designated it a national historic site in June 1995.

The city gives the streetcar from the South Exit of JR Hiroshima Station as about 20 minutes, on Line 2 or Line 6, to the Genbaku Domu-mae stop.

## Itsukushima Shrine

Itsukushima Shrine says it was registered as a World Heritage Site in 1996. The Miyajima Tourist Association gives the shrine's traditional founding date as 593. Taira no Kiyomori began construction in 1168, and the present main sanctuary dates from 1571.

Shrine opening hours, as stated by the shrine:

| Period | Hours |
|---|---|
| 1 January | midnight to 18:30 |
| 2 to 3 January | 6:30 to 18:30 |
| 4 January to end of February | 6:30 to 17:30 |
| 1 March to 14 October | 6:30 to 18:00 |
| 15 October to 30 November | 6:30 to 17:30 |
| December | 6:30 to 17:00 |

Admission to the shrine is ¥300 for adults, ¥200 for high school students and ¥100 for elementary and junior high students. A combined ticket with the Treasure Hall is ¥500 for adults. The shrine notes that its corridors have small gaps between the floorboards to relieve pressure from the sea at high tide, so heels can catch in them.

### The tide and the torii

The Miyajima Tourist Association publishes daily tide information. For each day it lists the times when you can walk out to the Great Torii and the times when the shrine appears to float on the sea. Times change every day, so check the page for your date.

## Getting to Miyajima

The Miyajima Tourist Association says two ferry companies sail between Miyajimaguchi and the island: the JR ferry and Miyajima Matsudai Kisen. The crossing takes about 10 minutes.

**JR Miyajima Ferry.** The ferry says you can use the Japan Rail Pass and the JR-West Rail Pass, and that it accepts IC cards. The adult fare is ¥180 one way, children (6 to 12) ¥90, excluding the visit tax. Sailings from Miyajimaguchi between 9:10 and 16:10 run as "Great Torii" services that pass closer to the torii, at no extra time.

**Miyajima Matsudai Kisen.** Its terminal is about a 1-minute walk from Hiroden Miyajima-guchi streetcar station and about 6 minutes from JR Miyajima-guchi Station, according to the operator.

For the JR train from Hiroshima to Miyajimaguchi, see the [rail-route table](/rail-routes). The City of Hiroshima also lists streetcar Line 2 as running toward Miyajima-guchi.

## The Miyajima visit tax

Hatsukaichi City has charged the Miyajima visit tax since 1 October 2023. It applies to anyone entering Miyajima by boat. The rate is ¥100 per person per visit, or ¥500 per person for a year paid in advance. On passenger ferries the tax is added to the fare and collected by the operator. The JR ferry states that the tax is charged separately from its fares. Exemptions include preschool children and certain school trips. See [Is there a fee to visit Miyajima?](/questions/is-there-a-tax-to-visit-miyajima) for the details.

## Planning notes

- Check the museum's reservation rules before an early-morning or evening visit.
- Check the Miyajima Tourist Association's tide page for the day if you want to walk to the torii or see it at high water.
