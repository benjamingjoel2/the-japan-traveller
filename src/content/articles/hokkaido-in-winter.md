Winter in Hokkaido centres on three things: the Sapporo Snow Festival, drift ice on the Sea of Okhotsk and skiing. This article covers when each happens, according to official sources, and what JR Hokkaido advises about travelling when snow disrupts services.

## The season

The Hokkaido Tourism Organization gives the snow season as running from late November to early May. JNTO says Hokkaido's ski resorts open from November to May. JR Hokkaido's winter model route covers December to March, and lists these seasonal events in and around Sapporo:

| Event | Timing given by JR Hokkaido |
|---|---|
| Sapporo White Illumination | Late November to mid March |
| German Christmas Market in Sapporo | Late November to 25 December |
| Sapporo Snow Festival | Early to mid February |
| Otaru Snow Light Path | Early to mid February |

For 2027, the Sapporo Tourist Association lists the Otaru Snow Light Path from 6 to 13 February. Its venues include the Otaru Canal and the former Temiya railway line.

## Sapporo Snow Festival

The festival's official site lists the 2027 festival from 4 to 11 February 2027 at three sites, all open on the same dates:

- **Odori Site**, in Odori Park in central Sapporo.
- **Tsudome Site**, at the Sapporo Community Dome (Tsudome) in Higashi-ku.
- **Susukino Site**, on the main street in Susukino, from Minami 4-jo to Minami 7-jo.

As of 7 November 2026, the official site said more information about the 2027 festival would be added over time. It had not yet given opening hours. For comparison, the 2025 festival also ran from 4 to 11 February.

The Sapporo Tourist Association describes each site:

- At Odori, snow sculptures line a 1.5 km stretch of the park from 1-chome to 12-chome. They are lit from sundown to 10pm. The International Snow Sculpture Contest is held at 11-chome.
- Tsudome has a giant slide and indoor and outdoor snow activities.
- Susukino has an ice sculpture contest, illuminated ice sculptures and an ice bar.

According to the same page, the festival began in 1950 with six snow sculptures made by local junior and high school students. It now draws more than two million visitors a year. For a short answer on dates, see [When is the Sapporo Snow Festival?](/questions/when-is-the-sapporo-snow-festival)

## Drift ice on the Okhotsk coast

Each winter, drift ice (ryuhyo) moves in from the Sea of Okhotsk towards Hokkaido's northern coast and the Shiretoko Peninsula. JNTO says Hokkaido is the southernmost point in the Northern Hemisphere where drift ice can be seen.

Official sources give slightly different windows, because the ice depends on the year:

- JNTO says the ice can be seen from mid January to late March at points along the Okhotsk Sea. It often spills into the Nemuro Strait in February and March.
- The Hokkaido Tourism Organization's Abashiri itinerary gives the drift ice season as mid February to early March. It gives the average temperature in that period as about minus 6C.
- The Aurora icebreaker at Abashiri lists its operating period as 20 January to 31 March.

**Ways to see it**, as listed by JNTO and the Hokkaido Tourism Organization:

- **Icebreaker cruises** from Abashiri and Monbetsu, and sightseeing cruises from Rausu into the Nemuro Strait.
- **Drift ice walks** near Utoro on the Shiretoko Peninsula. Local guides lead the walks and participants wear a dry suit over their clothes. JNTO lists the activity season as December to February.
- **The Okhotsk Ryu-hyo Museum** in Abashiri, where you can touch real drift ice kept at minus 15C.

At Abashiri, the Aurora sails from the Roadside Station Abashiri (Ryuhyo Kaido). Its operator says the large ship's cruise lasts about one hour and that it recommends booking ahead on its website. When there is no drift ice, the route changes to a cruise to Cape Notoro. Cancellations are not announced individually, so check the operator's website on the day.

**Getting there.** JNTO lists flights from Tokyo and Sapporo to Memanbetsu (for Abashiri), Monbetsu and Nakashibetsu (for Rausu). The Hokkaido Tourism Organization says the bus from Memanbetsu Airport to JR Abashiri Station takes about 35 minutes and that winter bus services are limited.

## Skiing

JNTO names Niseko United, Hoshino Resorts Tomamu, Sahoro Resort, Kiroro Snow World, Rusutsu Resort and Furano among Hokkaido's ski areas. It says the island receives around 15 metres of snow a year, carried by winds from Siberia. From New Chitose Airport, JNTO says airport buses serve the western resorts in two to three hours depending on the destination.

The Hokkaido Tourism Organization also lists other winter activities: backcountry skiing, dog sledding, winter canoeing on the Kushiro Wetland and Lake Shikotsu, and ice fishing for pond smelt.

## Getting around in snow

JR Hokkaido's Rapid Airport train links New Chitose Airport and Sapporo in about 37 minutes. Snow can delay or cancel trains, and JR Hokkaido's advice for disruptions is:

- Check the Train Operation Information page on its website. It marks sections where trains are cancelled, delayed by 30 minutes or more, or replaced by buses.
- Check station boards and announcements, and consider other transport such as buses or taxis.
- Expect alternative buses to become extremely crowded when trains are cancelled. Travel time may increase, or services may stop, depending on weather and road conditions.
- Ask at the station if you need a certificate showing that your train was delayed or cancelled.
