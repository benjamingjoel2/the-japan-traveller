Okinawa Prefecture will start charging an accommodation tax on 1 February 2027. The prefecture's rules for the tax were promulgated on 25 February 2026, after the national government consented to it on 13 February 2026. Guests will pay 2% of the room-only price per person per night, up to a maximum of ¥2,000. The prefecture last updated its information page on 5 November 2026.

## How the tax works

The Okinawa Prefectural Government sets out the rules:

- **Who pays.** Guests at hotels, ryokan, simple lodgings and minpaku (private lodging), including special-zone minpaku.
- **What is taxed.** The room-only price per person per night, without meals. The price is rounded down to the nearest ¥1,000 before the rate is applied. The price counted is capped at ¥100,000.
- **Rate.** 2%, with a maximum of ¥2,000 per person per night.
- **Longer stays.** The tax is charged for each night you stay.
- **Collection.** The accommodation operator collects the tax and pays it to the prefecture.

The prefecture gives a worked example. A room-only price of ¥8,500 per person per night is rounded down to ¥8,000, and the tax is ¥160.

## Towns that share the rate

Six municipalities are introducing their own accommodation tax as well: Motobu, Onna, Chatan, Miyakojima, Ishigaki and Nago. The prefecture says the combined rate stays at 2% and the combined cap at ¥2,000 in these places. The share is 0.8% for the prefecture, capped at ¥800, and 1.2% for the municipality, capped at ¥1,200. In the ¥8,500 example, that is ¥64 to the prefecture and ¥96 to the municipality.

For a traveller, the total is the same wherever you stay in Okinawa. What changes is who receives the money. The prefecture does not give the start dates of the municipal taxes on its page; local operators are told to check with their municipality.

## Exemptions

The prefecture exempts stays by school pupils and the staff with them on designated school activities, such as school trips and club activities. Universities are not included. It also exempts stays to take part in competitions run by designated bodies, such as the Japan Junior High School Athletic Federation. The guest has to show a certificate from the school head or similar at the front desk.

## What it means for travellers

- Stays on or after 1 February 2027 are taxed. If you are booking a winter or spring trip, expect an extra charge per person per night.
- The tax is based on the room-only price per person, not the whole room or a meal-inclusive rate.
- The prefecture says the money will go to tourism infrastructure and visitor support, protecting Okinawa's scenery and natural environment, passing on culture and history, safe tourism and sustainable tourism.
- The prefecture plans to review the tax three years after it starts, in fiscal 2029.

Okinawa joins a growing list of places with an accommodation tax.
