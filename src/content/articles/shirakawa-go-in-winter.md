Shirakawa-go is a village of thatched farmhouses in Gifu Prefecture, in one of Japan's heavy snowfall areas. This article covers the village, the winter light-up and its strict reservation system for 2027, and the buses from Takayama and Kanazawa.

## The village

The village visitors usually mean is Ogimachi, in Shirakawa Village, Gifu Prefecture. According to the Shirakawa-go Tourist Association, it was selected as an important preservation district for groups of traditional buildings in 1976. In 1995 Shirakawa-go, together with Gokayama in Toyama Prefecture, was registered as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The tourist association says more than 100 large and small gassho-style buildings still stand, and people still live in them.

Gassho-zukuri is the local building style: a steep thatched gable roof on a truss-like frame. "Gassho" means hands pressed together in prayer, which the roof shape resembles. The association says the style suits the area's natural conditions and heavy snowfall. Roofs are still re-thatched with help from the whole village, a tradition of shared labour called yui.

## Snow

Shirakawa-go is in one of Japan's heavy snowfall areas. The tourist association says snow on the ground can reach 170 cm or more in February, and the highest recorded snowfall is 4.5 metres. In the past, snow cut the village off from surrounding areas.

## The winter light-up

The 2027 event is the 41st. It will be held in Ogimachi from 17:30 to 19:30 on four evenings:

| Evening | Date |
|---|---|
| 1st | Monday 11 January 2027 (public holiday) |
| 2nd | Sunday 17 January 2027 |
| 3rd | Sunday 24 January 2027 |
| 4th | Sunday 31 January 2027 |

**Reservation only.** The tourist association says the event runs under a strict advance reservation system. No tickets are sold on the day, and visitors without a ticket are refused entry or asked to leave. Tickets are worn around the neck, are colour-coded by date and are checked at a gate at the village entrance, which is set up from 15:30. Daytime visitors arriving by regular bus must reach the village by the 15:20 bus.

**Ways to attend in 2027**, as listed by the tourist association:

1. **Staying overnight in the village.** Rooms were allocated by lottery. Applications closed on 31 October 2026, with results due on 10 November 2026. This option includes an observation deck ticket.
2. **Reserved parking for a private car** at Midashima Parking Lot. This is for ordinary cars only, with a maximum of 10 people per vehicle. The first round went on sale on 10 September 2026 at 6,000 yen for 1 to 5 people and 9,000 yen for 6 to 10 people. A second round goes on sale at 13:00 on 1 December 2026 at 7,000 yen and 10,000 yen. Changes and cancellations are not accepted, and refunds are made only if the event is cancelled for weather. All vehicles must have studless winter tyres. No observation deck ticket is included.
3. **Reserved parking plus a taxi**, with the same rules as the parking option.
4. **Highway bus tours** run by Nohi Bus, Kaetsuno Bus, Toyama Chihou Railway Bus, Hokutetsu Bus and Iruka Kotsu, booked directly with each operator. Some include an observation deck ticket. Sales opened between 13 October and 2 November 2026, depending on the date.
5. **Travel agency bus tours**, published from 13:00 on 2 December 2026. These do not include an observation deck ticket.

The tourist association handles only the first three options. It warns that new bus reservations are "very difficult to secure".

**Observation deck.** Only holders of an observation deck ticket may view the village from the Ogimachi Castle Ruins Observation Deck during the light-up. These tickets are not sold separately and cannot be transferred. Viewing is in three sessions and you cannot choose your slot. Tripods are banned on the deck. Access may be suspended in heavy snow.

**Other rules.** Drones are banned throughout the village, which is a residential area. Violating the rules can lead to a permanent ban from the event. The event may be cancelled if a severe cold wave or other extreme winter weather is expected to cause major transport disruption.

**Daytime visits on light-up days.** Ordinary cars do not need a parking reservation for daytime sightseeing. The car park opens at 8:00, the last entry is 15:00 and all vehicles must leave by 17:00. The tourist association strongly recommends arriving by 12:00, and suggests allowing about two to three hours to see the village.

## Getting there by bus

The tourist association's access page lists express buses and car as the ways to reach the village. Buses stop at the Shirakawago Bus Terminal, about a minute's walk from the village. The tourist association gives these approximate times by express bus:

| From | Time to Shirakawa-go |
|---|---|
| Takayama Station | About 50 minutes |
| Kanazawa Station | About 1 hour 20 minutes |
| Toyama Station | About 1 hour 20 minutes |
| Nagoya (Meitetsu Bus Center) | About 2 hours 30 minutes |

Nohi Bus runs the line from Takayama to Shirakawa-go and on to Kanazawa, Toyama and Takaoka, with partner operators including Hokuriku Tetsudo on the Kanazawa section. Some departures require a reservation; they are marked "R" in the timetable. Reserved seats can be booked by phone with each operator or online through Japan Bus Online.

Fares and timetables change on 1 December 2026. Nohi Bus lists these one-way fares:

| Route | Until 30 November 2026 | From 1 December 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Takayama Bus Terminal to Shirakawa-go | 2,800 yen | 2,900 yen |
| Shirakawa-go to Kanazawa Station | 2,800 yen | 2,900 yen |

Nohi Bus warns that delays of two hours or more can occur because of snowfall, queues for car parking at Shirakawa-go or expressway closures.

## Before you go

- Dress for the cold. For the light-up, the tourist association expects temperatures of about minus 10C to 0C and icy roads, and advises bringing a torch.
- On light-up days, book transport or parking well before the date; there is no ticket sale on the day.
- Build slack into bus connections in winter, given the delays Nohi Bus warns of.
