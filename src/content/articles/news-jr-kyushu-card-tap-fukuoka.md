JR Kyushu announced on 8 September 2026 that its tap-to-ride service for contactless bank cards would cover more lines in the Fukuoka area from 18 September 2026. Since the first train that day, the service has worked at 92 stations. You pay the fare by tapping a contactless credit, debit or prepaid card, or a phone set up with one, at the ticket gate. You do not need to buy a ticket or an IC card first.

## Where it works

JR Kyushu began trials in July 2022, the first in the JR group, and started the full service on 1 April 2026. It then covered the Kagoshima Main Line between Mojiko and Kurume and one station on the Kashii Line, Uminonakamichi. The September expansion adds three lines. The full list of covered sections is:

| Line | Section | Stations |
|---|---|---|
| Kagoshima Main Line | Mojiko to Kurume | 49 |
| Fukuhoku Yutaka Line | Orio to Hakata | 27 |
| Kashii Line | Saitozaki to Umi | 16 |
| Wakamatsu Line | Wakamatsu to Orio | 6 |

Stations that appear on more than one line are counted once, which gives the total of 92. The Kagoshima Main Line section includes Hakata and Kokura stations. JR Kyushu's English page calls the coverage the Fukuoka area only.

These are **not** covered:

- the Shinkansen, and the Shinkansen transfer gates at Kokura and Hakata;
- the Sanyo Main Line and the Sanin Main Line, including Shimonoseki Station;
- any station outside the sections listed above.

## How to use it

- Accepted brands are Visa, Mastercard, JCB, American Express, Diners Club, Discover and UnionPay, when the card supports contactless payment. All 92 stations accept all seven.
- Credit, debit and prepaid cards all work. So do phones and wearables using Apple Pay or Google Pay.
- Tap the same card or device at the start and end of the journey. Depending on the station, the reader is on the ticket gate or on a separate post-style terminal.
- No registration, app or top-up is needed. The fare is charged to your card later, on your card issuer's billing cycle.
- Only adult fares are charged this way. Each person needs their own card or device, so this does not work for children.
- The card must be in the user's own name.
- It cannot be combined with other tickets, such as commuter passes.

## Before you travel

If you leave the train at a station that does not support the service, you cannot exit by tapping your card. Ask station staff for help. Plan to stay within the covered sections when you pay this way. The Shinkansen still needs a normal ticket or a Japan Rail Pass; see [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass). IC cards are another way to pay local fares; see [IC cards: Suica, PASMO and ICOCA](/articles/ic-cards-suica-pasmo-icoca). For travel further south in Kyushu, see [Kyushu onsen towns](/articles/kyushu-onsen-towns).

JR Kyushu says the expansion is meant to make travel around the Fukuoka urban area smoother, including for overseas visitors.

## What we don't know yet

JR Kyushu has not said on these pages whether or when the service will reach other parts of Kyushu.
