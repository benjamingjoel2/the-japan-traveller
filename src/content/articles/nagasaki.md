This article covers the Atomic Bomb Museum and Peace Park, the restored Dutch trading post at Dejima, Glover Garden, Oura Church and its World Heritage listing, the rules for landing on Gunkanjima, and the rail route from Hakata.

## Getting there from Hakata

There is no through train from Hakata. JR Kyushu runs the **Relay KAMOME** limited express from Hakata to Takeo-Onsen. There you change to the **Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen KAMOME**, which arrives on the opposite side of the same platform.

JR Kyushu says the Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen opened on 23 September 2022. It runs 66 km from Takeo-Onsen to Nagasaki and has five stations: Takeo-Onsen, Ureshino-Onsen, Shin-Omura, Isahaya and Nagasaki. When it announced the opening, JR Kyushu gave Hakata to Nagasaki as approximately 80 minutes.

Notes from JR Kyushu's pages:

- Buy a ticket covering the whole distance before boarding.
- Neither train can be booked through JR Kyushu's online booking. Rail Pass holders who want a reserved seat should go to a JR station ticket counter after arriving in Japan.
- The KAMOME has no Green Car and no on-board sales.
- Timetables may only be valid for a few months.

For pass options, see [regional rail passes](/articles/regional-rail-passes) and the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

## Atomic Bomb Museum and Peace Park

The Nagasaki Atomic Bomb Museum is at 7-8 Hirano-machi. The museum's own site gives:

| | |
|---|---|
| Opening hours | 8:30 to 17:30 |
| Closed | 29 to 31 December |
| Admission, age 15 and over | ¥200 |
| High school students and younger | free (student ID needed) |

Group rates have been abolished. Entry to special exhibitions only is free. The museum says its permanent exhibition is being renewed in phases, with partial closures from September 2026 and completion planned for March 2027. Most of the section holding the atomic bomb artefacts stays open during the work.

The Peace Park is on a small hill north of the Atomic Bomb Hypocenter Park. Nagasaki City's tourism site describes it as a space of prayer for the victims and for lasting peace. Its bronze Peace Statue, by the Nagasaki-born sculptor Kitamura Seibo, is 9.7 m tall. From Nagasaki Station, take tram Line 1 or 3 (for Akasako) for about 15 minutes to the Peace Park stop, then walk about 3 minutes.

For Hiroshima's peace sites, see [Hiroshima and Miyajima](/articles/hiroshima-and-miyajima).

## Dejima

Dejima was a fan-shaped artificial island built for foreign trade. Its official site says it was completed in 1636. In 1641 the shogunate ordered the Dutch to move their trading post there from Hirado. Dejima's trade monopoly with Europe ended when Japan opened its ports in 1859.

The restoration project aims to recreate Dejima as it was around 1820. Sixteen buildings, including the Chief Factor's Quarters, have been restored so far.

- Exhibition area: 8:00 to 21:00, last entry 20:40. Open all year, though some facilities may close for maintenance.
- Admission: adults ¥1,100; senior high, junior high and elementary students ¥550. No group discounts and no re-entry.
- Access: the Dejima tram stop is about 5 minutes by tram from Nagasaki Station.

## Glover Garden and Oura Church

**Glover Garden** is on the Minami-Yamate hill above the port. The garden says the Former Glover House is a component of the World Heritage site "Sites of Japan's Meiji Industrial Revolution". Nagasaki City's tourism site calls it Japan's oldest wooden Western-style building.

The garden's opening hours are 8:00 to 18:00, with evening opening to 20:00 from 1 November to 31 December 2026. Last entry is 20 minutes before closing. From 1 April 2026, admission is ¥1,300 for visitors aged 15 and over and ¥650 for elementary, junior high and high school students. Viewing of the Former Alt House has been suspended for repairs since December 2022, and the garden plans to reopen it in March 2027.

**Oura Church** (Oura Cathedral). Nagasaki Prefecture's tourism site says it was completed in November 1864 and dedicated on 19 February 1865. The prefecture dates the discovery of the Hidden Christians at the church to 17 March, about a month after the dedication. The church was designated a National Treasure in 1933.

The prefecture says "The Hidden Christian Sites in the Nagasaki Region" were inscribed on the World Heritage List on 1 July 2018. Nagasaki City's tourism site lists church admission as ¥1,000 for adults, ¥400 for junior high and high school students and ¥300 for elementary pupils, including entry to the church's Christian museum.

## Gunkanjima (Hashima)

Hashima, known as Gunkanjima, is an artificial island about 40 minutes by boat from Nagasaki Port. Nagasaki City's tourism site says it was listed in July 2015 as part of the "Sites of Japan's Meiji Industrial Revolution: Iron and Steel, Shipbuilding and Coal Mining".

You can only land by joining a landing tour run by one of several boat operators, booked directly with the operator. A facility fee applies on top of the boat fare: ¥650 for visitors aged 15 and over and ¥320 for children. The city warns that landing may not be possible because of the weather.

Landing is governed by the city's rules on when the Hashima viewing facilities may not be used. Since 22 April 2023, the city has run a trial to review those rules. Under the trial, landing is allowed only when:

- wave height at the Iojima offshore gauge is no more than 0.6 m
- the wind reading from the boat's anemometer is 5 m or less
- visibility is at least 500 m
- the wind direction is within the permitted range
- the captain judges that passengers can get on and off safely

The city's answer is dated 18 May 2023, and the page was last updated on 5 February 2025. It notes that the underlying rules had not been revised at that time.

## Planning notes

- From Hakata, allow for the change at Takeo-Onsen and reserve seats at a station counter if you hold a rail pass.
- The Atomic Bomb Museum is under partial renovation until March 2027.
- Glover Garden's evening hours change through the year, so check the garden's guide page for your dates.
- Gunkanjima landings depend on the sea. A tour can sail without landing.
