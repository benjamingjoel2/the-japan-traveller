On 4 November 2026 the Japan Meteorological Corporation (JMC), a private forecaster, issued its third and final autumn foliage forecast of the year. It puts maple colouring later than average in most of the large cities it covers. The Japan Weather Association (JWA), whose forecasts appear on tenki.jp, said in its third forecast on 28 October that peak viewing would be around normal or later than normal across Japan.

## Two forecasters, two kinds of date

The two forecasts measure different things, which explains why their dates look far apart.

- **JMC** forecasts the date on which most of the leaves on a sample tree have turned red (maple) or yellow (ginkgo). It says the method follows the Japan Meteorological Agency's standards for observing sample trees.
- **JWA** forecasts when named viewing spots reach their best colour.

For Kyoto, for example, JMC forecasts maple colouring on 14 December. JWA expects Arashiyama to reach its peak from around 13 November. The two dates measure different things, so they do not contradict each other. Our [Kyoto autumn leaves guide](/articles/kyoto-autumn-leaves) explains the official observation in detail.

JMC says this is its last forecast for the season. Of the 14 cities in its table, Aomori and Kagoshima are the only two where the maple date is forecast earlier than average.

## What JWA says

JWA's forecast of 28 October gave this picture:

- **Northern Japan:** around the usual time. Mountain spots peaked early, and some lowland spots may peak later.
- **Eastern and western Japan:** around the usual time or later. In western Japan, colouring was progressing slowly.
- **Why:** cool spells in early September and early October brought early colour to some mountain areas. Since then temperatures have been above average, slowing colour in the lowlands. JWA expected a cooler early November, especially in the north, and a somewhat warmer late November.

JWA also compared progress with earlier years. As of 28 October, 118 of its locations had reached peak, against about 180 at the same point in 2022 and 2023.

## What this means for travellers

In JMC's table, the forecast maple date is 22 November or later for 12 of the 14 cities, and later than average for 11 of them. JWA said it would update its forecast three to four times through November on tenki.jp, with daily notes from its forecasters.
