Nikko, in Tochigi Prefecture, is one of the standard trips out of Tokyo. This article covers the World Heritage shrines and temples, the two rail routes from Tokyo, the pass Tobu Railway sells for foreign visitors, and the bus up to Lake Chuzenji and Kegon Falls. For the wider region, see [/guide/regions](/guide/regions).

## The Shrines and Temples of Nikko

The World Heritage site is called the "Shrines and Temples of Nikko". According to Nikko City, it was registered at the 23rd session of the World Heritage Committee, held in Marrakesh in December 1999. It is made up of two Shinto shrines and one Buddhist temple:

- Toshogu Shrine
- Futarasan Shrine (Futarasan-jinja)
- Rinnoji Temple

Nikko City gives the total as 103 buildings: nine National Treasures and 94 Important Cultural Properties. The registered site covers 50.8 hectares, with a 373.2-hectare buffer zone. The city's summary says most of the buildings are the work of some of Japan's greatest 17th-century artists.

The Visit Nikko site lists Shinkyo Bridge and Taiyuin Temple among the World Heritage spots. JNTO says Shinkyo belongs to Futarasan-jinja and is now commonly regarded as the gateway to the main shrines and temples. It also says the 103 structures are spread over three complexes, all within easy walking distance of each other.

## Toshogu and the main sites

**Toshogu** enshrines Tokugawa Ieyasu, whom JNTO calls the most famous samurai leader. He was enshrined a year after his death. JNTO's page highlights:

- the Yomeimon Gate, covered with 508 detailed carvings;
- the "see no evil, speak no evil, hear no evil" monkeys, one of eight carvings on the Sacred Horse's stable;
- the sleeping cat carving, with two sparrows on its reverse side.

**Futarasan-jinja** was founded in the 8th century and is dedicated to Nikko's three sacred mountains. JNTO says the main grounds are free to enter and the inner garden has a small fee. The shrine also has a branch at Lake Chuzenji.

**Rinnoji** was also founded in the 8th century. It houses three Buddha images said to represent the three sacred mountains, and JNTO notes its garden is popular for autumn colour.

**Taiyuin** is the mausoleum of Tokugawa Iemitsu, Ieyasu's grandson.

Admission fees and opening hours are set by each shrine and temple. None of the official tourism pages used here state current figures, so check on arrival or on each site's own website.

## Getting there from Tokyo

There are two main ways in. Tobu Railway runs to **Tobu-Nikko Station**; the JR Nikko Line runs to **Nikko Station**. Buses into the shrine area and up to Lake Chuzenji start from both stations.

| Route | Time as stated by the operator or tourism body | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tobu Limited Express SPACIA, Asakusa to Tobu-Nikko (direct) | About 1 hour 50 minutes | Tobu |
| Tobu Limited Express SPACIA, Asakusa to Shimo-imaichi, then Tobu Nikko Line | About 1 hour 40 minutes, then about 10 minutes | Tobu |
| Through limited express SPACIA from JR Shinjuku | About 2 hours (direct) | Tobu; not covered by the NIKKO PASS |
| Through limited express SPACIA from JR Ikebukuro | About 1 hour 55 minutes (direct) | Tobu |
| Tobu local trains from Asakusa | About 60 minutes longer than the limited express | Visit Nikko; about half the limited express fare |
| Shinkansen to Utsunomiya, then JR Nikko Line | No time given by Visit Nikko | Fully covered by the Japan Rail Pass, according to Visit Nikko |

Visit Nikko says the JR route costs more than Tobu and needs a change at Utsunomiya. It describes Tobu limited express trains as leaving Asakusa about twice per hour. Tobu is a private railway, so its trains are not covered by the Japan Rail Pass (see [/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass)). The site's [/rail-routes](/rail-routes) table also lists Tokyo to Nikko.

## The NIKKO PASS

Tobu sells the **NIKKO PASS ALL AREA** to foreign visitors. Japanese nationals travelling with them may also buy it. As described on Tobu's page:

- Price: 8,000 yen adult, 4,000 yen child. Valid for 4 days.
- One round trip between Asakusa (or TOKYO SKYTREE) and Shimo-Imaichi.
- All Tobu Railway lines between Shimo-Imaichi, Tobu-Nikko and Shin-Fujiwara.
- All Tobu Bus lines in Nikko, which include the buses to Lake Chuzenji and Yumoto Onsen.
- The Lake Chuzenji sightseeing cruise and the hybrid bus to the Odashirogahara area, both from mid-April to 30 November.
- Discounts at some facilities in the Asakusa, Nikko and Kinugawa areas.

The pass does not include limited express charges. Tobu says a limited express ticket must be bought separately to use an express train. Shrine and temple admission fees are not included. Tobu's page also mentions a cheaper "world heritage area" version, but its main price list gives only the All Area price.

Tobu also notes that the Akechidaira Ropeway is suspended from 16 January 2026 to 31 August 2027, and that the closure may be extended.

## Okunikko: Lake Chuzenji and Kegon Falls

Okunikko is the mountain area above the town. You reach it by bus up the **Irohazaka** road, which Visit Nikko describes as a road with 48 sharp curves linking Nikko city to Okunikko.

- **Lake Chuzenji** lies at an altitude of 1,269 metres. Visit Nikko calls it Japan's highest natural lake and says it was formed about 20,000 years ago by an eruption of Mount Nantai. Its page gives about 50 minutes by bus from Nikko or Tobu-Nikko Station on buses bound for Chuzenji Onsen or Yumoto Onsen.
- **Kegon Falls** drops 97 metres from Lake Chuzenji. Visit Nikko's Kegon Falls page gives about 40 minutes from Nikko or Tobu-Nikko Station on buses bound for Chuzenji Onsen, to the last stop. A paid observation platform at the base gives close-up views.

In town, the World Heritage Tour bus takes 13 minutes from Nikko or Tobu-Nikko Station to the shrine area, according to Visit Nikko. JNTO says the walk from Nikko Station to Toshogu takes about 45 minutes and passes Shinkyo Bridge.

## Autumn colour

Visit Nikko publishes these typical windows:

| Area | Autumn foliage |
|---|---|
| Okunikko (Lake Chuzenji, Kegon Falls) | Late September to late October |
| Central Nikko (shrines and temples) | Late October to mid-November |
| Irohazaka | Peaks from mid-October to early November |

Visit Nikko warns of traffic jams on Irohazaka in the foliage season. Its access page says the drive from Nikko Station to Lake Chuzenji normally takes about 45 minutes, but can take over 4 hours at peak congestion, from mid-October to mid-November. Buses use the same road. For other regions, see [/guide/seasons](/guide/seasons) and [/articles/kyoto-autumn-leaves](/articles/kyoto-autumn-leaves).

## Planning notes

- In October and November, allow extra time for the road up to Okunikko (see above).
- If you use the NIKKO PASS, budget for limited express tickets on top.
- If you travel on a Japan Rail Pass, the JR route via Utsunomiya is the one covered.
- Check bus timetables before going up to Lake Chuzenji. Tobu warns that delays are possible due to traffic and snow.
- For carrying bags between hotels, see [/articles/luggage-forwarding-takkyubin](/articles/luggage-forwarding-takkyubin).
