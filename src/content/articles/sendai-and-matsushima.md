Sendai is the largest city in Tohoku and the usual starting point for trips around northern Honshu. This article covers getting there, two Date-era sites in the city, and the short rail trip to Matsushima Bay. Opening hours and fees below are as published by Sendai's and Matsushima's tourism bodies on 7 November 2026.

## Sendai as a base

Sendai's official tourism site, run by the Sendai Tourism, Convention and International Association, describes Sendai Station as the gateway to the Tohoku region. It presents the city as a base for trips to neighbouring prefectures such as Yamagata, Iwate and Fukushima.

The same site gives these shortest rail times to Sendai:

| From | Line | Shortest time (Sentabi) |
|---|---|---|
| Tokyo | Tohoku Shinkansen | about 1 hr 32 min |
| Morioka | Tohoku Shinkansen | about 39 min |
| Shin-Aomori | Tohoku Shinkansen | about 1 hr 28 min |
| Akita | Akita Shinkansen | about 2 hr 18 min |
| Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto | Hokkaido Shinkansen | about 2 hr 35 min |

It also lists direct flights to Sendai from Sapporo (New Chitose), Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe, Hiroshima, Fukuoka and Okinawa, and highway buses from Tokyo of about 5 to 5 hours 30 minutes from Tokyo Station. For the main Shinkansen routes and JR Pass coverage, see [rail routes](/rail-routes) and [Rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

## Zuihoden

Zuihoden is the mausoleum of Date Masamune, the founding lord of Sendai. According to Sentabi, it was built in 1637 in an ornate style that carries on the Momoyama tradition. It was designated a National Treasure in 1931 but was destroyed in the war. The present building is a reconstruction from 1979.

| Item | Detail (Sentabi) |
|---|---|
| Hours, 1 February to 30 November | 9am to 4.50pm, last entry 4.30pm |
| Hours, 1 December to 31 January | 9am to 4.20pm, last entry 4pm |
| Closed | 31 December |
| Admission | Adults 570 yen, high school students 410 yen, elementary and junior high students 210 yen |

**Getting there.** Take the Loople Sendai sightseeing bus from stop 16 at the Sendai Station West Exit bus terminal to Zuihoden-mae, then walk about five minutes. There are stairs from the bus stop. Alternatively, walk about 15 minutes from Omachi-Nishikoen Station on the Sendai Subway Tozai Line.

## Osaki Hachimangu

Sentabi says Osaki Hachimangu was founded by Date Masamune in 1607 and designated a National Treasure in 1952. It lists no closing days and does not give opening hours or an admission fee.

The shrine holds the Matsutaki festival, also called Dontosai, on 14 January. Sentabi notes that it is a Sendai City Intangible Folk Cultural Property.

**Getting there.** City buses run from stops 10 and 15 at the Sendai Station West Exit bus terminal, towards Oridate or Sakunami, Kunimigaoka, or Minamiyoshinari. Sentabi gives the fare as 230 yen. The Loople Sendai bus from stop 16 also stops at Osaki Hachimangu-mae. Stairs lead up from the bus stop to the shrine buildings.

## Matsushima Bay

The Matsushima Tourism Association titles its sightseeing page "Three Most Scenic Spots of Japan" and describes the bay's "260 small pine-covered islands". Its other main points:

- **Zuiganji** is a National Treasure and Important Cultural Property. The association says it was first opened in 828 by the Tendai priest Ennin. The present buildings were completed by Date Masamune in 1609 after five years of work.
- **Godaido** is an Important Cultural Property. The association says the current hall was built by Date Masamune in 1604. Admission is free. It is closed in the evenings.
- **Kanrantei** is a tea house of two rooms, named by Date Yoshimura, the fifth-generation lord.
- **Oshima** and **Fukuurajima** are islands that can be reached on foot over red bridges. The association says legend gives Oshima 108 caves, of which about 50 remain.
- The Fukuurabashi bridge and Godaido are lit up at night.

Zuiganji's published hours and fees:

| Months | Zuiganji hours (last entry 30 minutes before closing) |
|---|---|
| April to September | 8.30am to 5pm |
| October and March | 8.30am to 4.30pm |
| November and February | 8.30am to 4pm |
| December and January | 8.30am to 3.30pm |

Admission is 700 yen for adults and 400 yen for children. Kanrantei is open 8.30am to 5pm from April to October and 8.30am to 4.30pm from November to March. Its admission is 200 yen for adults, 150 yen for high school and university students and 100 yen for elementary and junior high students.

The association lists the Zuiganji Osegakie, a procession of Buddhist priests and ceremony, on 16 August.

## Bay cruises

The association lists three regular sightseeing boat routes:

- a round trip around the bay from Matsushima;
- a route between Matsushima Bay and Shiogama Bay;
- a round trip between Matsushima and the Sagakei cliffs at Oku-Matsushima.

Its FAQ says the round trip of the bay and the Matsushima to Shiogama cruise each take 50 minutes. Each operator runs several cruises, so check timetables and fares with the operator or at the tourist office. The association also mentions oyster lunch cruises in winter. It does not publish departure times.

## Getting to Matsushima

The Matsushima Tourism Association gives three rail options from Sendai Station:

| Route | Time | Fare |
|---|---|---|
| JR Senseki Line to Matsushimakaigan | about 40 min | 410 yen |
| JR Tohoku Line to Matsushima, then 15 to 20 min walk to the waterfront | about 25 min | 410 yen |
| Senseki-Tohoku Line express to Takagimachi, then about 20 min walk | from about 25 min | not stated |

For the Senseki Line, trains leave from platform 10 at Sendai Station. Take a train terminating at Ishinomaki or Takagimachi. Trains bound for Shiogama or Tagajo require a change. From Sendai Airport, the association gives 17 minutes on the Sendai Airport Access Line to Sendai, then the Senseki Line.
