Jigokudani Yaen-koen is a wild monkey park in the Yokoyu River valley in northern Nagano, where Japanese macaques bathe in hot spring pools. It is reached by train or bus from Nagano and a walk through the forest. This article covers the park's hours, fees and rules, the season for bathing monkeys, and the route via Yudanaka, using the park's own site and official tourism sources.

## The park and the season

Nagano Prefecture's tourism site says the park is home to over 160 Japanese macaques. It sits upriver from Yudanaka and Shibu Onsen, and the area is under snow for about a third of the year. The name means "Hell's Valley", after the steep cliffs and the hot springs that burst from the ground.

The park's visitor guide says the monkeys use the hot spring baths from December to March, the coldest months. Baby monkeys can sometimes be seen in spring. In autumn, food is plentiful in the mountains, so the monkeys may not come to the park every day. The park does not guarantee sightings. Staff post daily updates on the monkeys on Facebook and X, and the site carries monkey appearance forecasts.

## Hours and admission

| Season | Hours |
|---|---|
| Summer (April to October) | 8:30 to 17:00 |
| Winter (November to March) | 9:00 to 16:00 |

Winter hours began on 1 November 2026. The park has no fixed closing days. It may shorten hours or close without notice because of weather, the monkeys' behaviour or natural disasters. The park asks visitors to arrive by 30 minutes before closing time.

Admission listed on the official site in November 2026 (yen, tax included):

| Ticket | Adult (18 and over) | Child (6 to 17) |
|---|---|---|
| Single ticket | 800 | 400 |
| Group (20 or more) | 680 | 340 |
| Annual pass | 5,000 | 2,500 |

The site says children under 5 are free and must be with a guardian. There is no senior discount. Credit cards and e-money are accepted, but QR code and barcode payments are not. **The park says admission fees will be revised from 1 December 2026.** The new fees were not available in text form on the site when this article was checked, so confirm them before you go.

From December to April, the park says priority entry goes to visitors who bought tickets online.

## Park rules

The park's rules page says:

- **No feeding.** Do not show or give the monkeys any food.
- **No touching.** Do not touch or shout at the monkeys. Never touch baby monkeys, even if they come close. If curious young monkeys approach, step away.
- **No staring.** Staring into a monkey's eyes and opening your mouth is seen as hostile.
- **Photography:** cameras, smartphones and flash are allowed. Selfie sticks, drones and cameras in the bath are not. Do not hold a camera too close to the monkeys.
- **No pets,** including guide dogs, hearing dogs and other assistance dogs.
- **No bathing** with the monkeys.
- Do not catch wild animals or birds, or collect insects or plants.

Visitors who break the rules may be asked to leave.

## The walk in

There are no paved paths inside or outside the park, and the park says wheelchairs and carts cannot use the approaches. The park gives these walking times:

- from the Snow Monkey Park bus stop: about 35 minutes;
- from Kanbayashi Onsen along the Yumichi nature trail: about 2 km, roughly 30 minutes, mostly flat with some hilly sections;
- from Jigokudani Parking (500 yen): about 15 minutes.

The road from Shibu Onsen to Jigokudani Parking is a narrow logging road that is closed completely in winter. The park recommends trainers or hiking shoes in the warmer months, and rubber or snow boots in winter, with simple crampons bought in advance.

## Getting there

- **Tokyo to Nagano:** JR Hokuriku Shinkansen, fastest about 79 minutes, according to the park.
- **Nagano to Yudanaka:** Nagano Electric Railway (Nagaden) limited express, about 38 minutes. Nagaden's 2026 leaflet names the limited express trains as the Snow Monkey and the Yukemuri. Some other trains need a change at Shinshu-Nakano or Suzaka.
- **Yudanaka to the park:** local bus, about 15 minutes to the Snow Monkey Park stop, then the walk.
- **Nagano Station to the park direct:** the express bus to Shiga Kogen, about 41 minutes, from stops 23 and 24 near Nagano Station's east exit, according to Nagaden's leaflet.

Timetables and fares change by season. Check nagaden-net.co.jp before you travel. Nagano's tourism site also describes a Snow Monkey Pass that combines transport and admission; check current terms with Nagaden.

## Shibu and Yudanaka onsen

Yudanaka is the last stop on the Nagaden line. JNTO describes Shibu Onsen, next to Yudanaka, as a hot spring town with a history said to go back over 1,300 years. It was a rest stop for pilgrims travelling to Zenkoji in Nagano. Shibu has nine public baths, called soto-yu, run by the town's inns. Guests at the inns can use all nine free. Non-guests can use the largest, the Oyu, for a small fee paid at the information centre. JNTO puts Shibu about a half-hour walk or a short taxi ride from Yudanaka Station, and about 10 minutes by bus from the entrance to the monkey park.

## Before you go

- Check the park's site, Facebook or X on the day for monkey sightings and any closure.
- Confirm the fees from 1 December 2026 and buy online in winter if you want priority entry.
- Wear proper footwear; the path is snow and ice in winter.
