Yakushima is a mountainous island south of Kagoshima, known for its old cedar forests and heavy rain. This article covers the World Heritage listing, the day hike to the Jomon Sugi cedar, the seasonal bus-only access to the Arakawa trailhead, the Shiratani Unsuikyo ravine, the trekking donations, and how to reach the island by sea and air.

## A World Heritage island with a lot of rain

JNTO says Yakushima was registered as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1993, and that it was Japan's first inscription on the list. JNTO describes microclimates ranging from subtropical on the beaches to subarctic in the high mountains.

Rain is part of any visit. The Kagoshima prefectural tourism site says the island receives almost three times Tokyo's annual rainfall and is often described as "rainy 35 days a month". The Forestry Agency uses the same saying. For general advice on wet-season travel, see [Is Japan good in the rainy season?](/questions/is-japan-good-in-the-rainy-season).

## The Jomon Sugi cedar

According to JNTO, the Jomon Sugi was officially discovered in 1966. It is 25.3 m high with a circumference of 16.4 m, and its age is estimated at between 2,000 and 7,200 years. Visitors see it from an observation deck about 15 m from the tree.

The usual route starts at the Arakawa trailhead. The Ministry of the Environment's Yakushima World Heritage Conservation Center gives the round trip from the Arakawa trail entrance to the Jomon Sugi as 9 to 10 hours, and asks hikers to set out between 5:00 and 7:00. JNTO gives about 10 hours and advises leaving before dawn to return by sunset. Neither page gives the trail distance.

Points from the conservation center's trekking advice:

- Toilets are at the Arakawa trail entrance, Sandai-sugi cedar, the Okabu trail entrance and Takatsuka Hut.
- There are no public phones at the Arakawa trail entrance. Mobile coverage depends on the provider.
- Part of the route follows an old forest railway track. Walk in single file, rest at passing places rather than on the track, and move aside when a railcar approaches. The ends of the sleepers are slippery and a frequent cause of accidents.
- At the Jomon Sugi, stay on the viewing deck. Tents and overnight stays there are generally prohibited. Eat at the nearby rest area or Takatsuka Hut.
- At the Wilson-kabu stump, do not climb on or damage the trunks, and keep to trails and rest areas.

The center also publishes a calendar of less busy trekking days.

## Getting to the Arakawa trailhead

The conservation center says ordinary vehicles are restricted from the Arakawa trail entrance all day from 1 March to 30 November. During that period, take the Arakawa hiking bus from in front of the Yakusugi Museum. JNTO also says a shuttle bus from the Yakusugi Museum is the recommended option from March to November, and that the trailhead is a 30-minute bus ride from the town of Anbo. Outside the restricted period, the center asks drivers not to park in prohibited places because this blocks large trucks.

Neither page gives the bus timetable or fare. Check them locally before the day of your hike.

## Shiratani Unsuikyo

Shiratani Unsuikyo is a recreation forest managed by the Forestry Agency. It covers 426.52 hectares, mostly between 600 and 800 m above sea level, with a highest point of 1,050 m. The Kagoshima prefectural tourism site describes its deep forest, mosses and ferns.

The Forestry Agency lists three walking trails:

| Trail | Time given |
|---|---|
| Taiko-iwa Rock trail (the most popular) | about 4 hours |
| Bugyo-sugi trail | about 3 hours |
| Yayoi-sugi trail | about 1 hour |

The forest is open every day of the year, but may close when a severe weather warning is issued. The agency warns that mountain leeches are present all year, that streams can rise quickly after heavy rain, and that sandals and pumps are unsuitable. By bus, it gives about 35 minutes from Miyanoura Port. Parking for 50 cars is free.

## Trekking donations

Two separate contributions apply.

**Mountain environment conservation donation.** The Ministry of the Environment's conservation center describes a donation scheme set up in March 2017. Day trekkers in the mountains are asked for ¥1,000 and those staying overnight for ¥2,000. It applies to junior high school students and older, and elementary pupils and younger are exempt. Pay before starting and keep the certificate. If you start from the Arakawa trail entrance, you pay when buying the shuttle bus ticket. The center says the money goes to mountain toilets and to inspecting and repairing trails.

**Shiratani Unsuikyo entry donation.** The Forestry Agency asks high school students and older for ¥500 per person, or ¥400 per person in groups of 15 or more. A ¥200 discount ticket issued at Yakusugi Land can be used. Between 8:30 and 16:30 you pay at the administration building. At other times there is a collection box.

## Getting there

JNTO gives these options:

- **Speed ferry** from Kagoshima Port to Miyanoura or Anbo port: two and a half hours.
- **Car ferry** on the same route: four hours, and cheaper than the speed ferry.
- **Flights** from Kagoshima to Yakushima: 40 minutes. The Forestry Agency gives about 30 minutes, and also lists about 1 hour from Fukuoka and about 1 hour 30 minutes from Itami (Osaka).

For reaching Kagoshima itself, see [Kagoshima and Sakurajima](/articles/kagoshima-and-sakurajima) and the [rail-route table](/rail-routes). The Kagoshima prefectural tourism site says two companies run local and sightseeing buses on the island.

## Before you go

- From 1 March to 30 November you cannot drive to the Arakawa trailhead. Plan around the bus from the Yakusugi Museum, and buy the conservation donation with your bus ticket.
- Allow 9 to 10 hours for the Jomon Sugi round trip and start early.
- Expect rain at any time of year, and check weather warnings before walking at Shiratani Unsuikyo.
- Sea and air times differ between sources, so check the timetable for your day. For how this fits into a Kyushu trip, see [the regions guide](/guide/regions).
