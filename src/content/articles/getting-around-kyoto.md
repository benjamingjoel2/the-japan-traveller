This article covers fares and payment on the buses and subway, the day pass, the sightseeing express buses and the city's seasonal crowd measures, rail routes to three busy areas, the official guidance for Gion, and Kyoto's accommodation tax.

## City buses and subway: fares and payment

The Kyoto City Transportation Bureau runs the city buses and the two subway lines, the Karasuma Line (north to south, through Kyoto Station) and the Tozai Line (east to west). The lines cross at Karasuma Oike.

| Service | Adult | Child (6 to 11) |
|---|---|---|
| City bus, flat-fare zone | 230 yen | 120 yen |
| Sightseeing Limited Express Bus (EX100, EX101) | 500 yen | 250 yen |
| Subway (by distance) | 220 to 360 yen | 110 to 180 yen |

The subway fares are marked as effective from 1 October 2025.

**How to ride a bus.** On standard routes you board at the rear door, pay when you get off, and leave by the front door. On the EX100 and EX101 sightseeing express buses it is the other way round: board at the front, pay as you get on, leave at the rear.

**Paying.** The bureau accepts cash, IC cards, one-day tickets and other valid tickets. Its bus guide states that credit, debit and cash cards cannot be used, and that the Japan Rail Pass is not accepted. IC cards listed for the buses and subway are PiTaPa, ICOCA, Kitaca, PASMO, Suica, manaca, TOICA, HAYAKAKEN, nimoca, SUGOCA and KANSAI ONE PASS (for foreign tourists only). Load the card at a station or a convenience store before you ride. See [IC cards: Suica, PASMO, ICOCA](/articles/ic-cards-suica-pasmo-icoca) for how the cards work.

**Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass.** This costs 1,100 yen for adults and 550 yen for children. It covers all subway lines and all city bus routes, plus listed Kyoto Bus and Keihan Bus routes and the West Japan JR Bus Takao-Keihoku line (Kyoto Station to Toganoo). Some seasonal Kyoto Bus lines are excluded. It is valid only on the day it is first used, not for 24 hours. It is sold at subway stations, on city buses (subject to stock) and at some convenience stores and tourist information centres. It can be used on the sightseeing express buses with no extra fare.

## Sightseeing express buses and crowd measures

The bureau's spring 2026 press release says the sightseeing express buses have run since June 2024 to shorten waits and ease crowding on the parallel routes between Kyoto Station and the Higashiyama area. It describes them as the first in Japan to charge a different fare from ordinary buses, using a new national scheme for overtourism. They run on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays.

- **EX100:** Kyoto Station (stop D1), Gojo-zaka (for Kiyomizu-dera), Gion, Okazaki Koen Bijutsukan / Heian Jingu-mae, and Ginkakuji-mae.
- **EX101:** Kyoto Station to Gojo-zaka and back.

The bureau's English page says they leave Kyoto Station for Kiyomizu-dera every 7 to 8 minutes in the morning on operating days.

For autumn 2026, the bureau's 23 October press release lists a campaign on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays from 31 October to 29 November 2026, with 21 to 23, 28 and 29 November as the focus days. The measures include:

- Extra runs on route 106 (Kyoto Station, Gojo-zaka, Gion, Sanjo Keihan-mae) and Kyoto-bound route 206.
- Route 109 between Kitaoji Bus Terminal, Kinkakuji-michi and Arashiyama on the focus days, hourly from the 8:00 hour to the 16:00 hour.
- Extra Karasuma Line trains from about 10:00 to 19:00, running about every 5 to 6.5 minutes instead of about every 7.5 minutes.
- "Pokeloke plus+", which since 20 March 2026 shows how crowded each approaching bus is.

**Planned fare change.** A March 2026 bureau paper sets out a planned "resident-priority" bus fare. Subject to national approval, the standard flat fare would rise to about 350 to 400 yen, with Kyoto residents paying 200 yen. It is to start in the flat-fare zone during fiscal 2027 (April 2027 to March 2028). This was a plan, not a confirmed fare, when the sources were checked.

## Rail to Arashiyama, Fushimi Inari and Uji

The Transportation Bureau recommends combining trains and buses in the busy seasons, because buses take longer at peak times.

**Arashiyama.** The JR Sagano Line from Kyoto Station takes about 11 minutes by rapid service to Saga-Arashiyama, then about 8 minutes on foot to Togetsu-kyo Bridge. The association says it is crowded and suggests boarding from the back of the platform. A calmer option is the subway to Uzumasa Tenjingawa, then the Randen tram. From Kyoto-Kawaramachi, take Hankyu and change at Katsura for the Arashiyama Line, or change at Shijo-Omiya for the Randen. The association warns that city bus 11 along Shijo-dori "tends to be constantly congested".

**Fushimi Inari Taisha.** The shrine says it is right next to Inari Station on the JR Nara Line, two stops from Kyoto Station. The association gives about 9 minutes and says local and rapid services stop there. From Gion-Shijo, the Keihan Line runs direct to Fushimi-Inari in about 22 minutes, but limited express trains do not stop there. The shrine is a 5-minute walk east of Keihan Fushimi-Inari Station.

**Uji.** JNTO gives about 17 minutes on the JR Nara Line from Kyoto Station to JR Uji. Keihan trains take about 40 minutes from Demachiyanagi to Keihan Uji, changing to the Uji Line at Chushojima.

## Gion: the official guidance

The Kyoto City Tourism Association's guidelines (11 March 2026) say "Gion is not just a tourist destination; it is a place where people live." They ask visitors not to touch, chase or photograph geiko and maiko without permission, not to enter private property, and not to block streets, and warn that violations may result in fines. A notice from the residents and associations of southern Gionmachi, published on the same site in January 2024, says obstructing someone's path can bring a fine of under 10,000 yen and obstructing traffic a fine of up to 50,000 yen. For general conduct at temples and shrines, see [temple and shrine etiquette](/articles/temple-and-shrine-etiquette).

## Kyoto's accommodation tax

Kyoto City revised its accommodation tax from 1 March 2026. The tax is charged per person per night, based on the room charge without meals or consumption tax:

| Room charge per person per night | Tax |
|---|---|
| Under 6,000 yen | 200 yen |
| 6,000 to under 20,000 yen | 400 yen |
| 20,000 to under 50,000 yen | 1,000 yen |
| 50,000 to under 100,000 yen | 4,000 yen |
| 100,000 yen and over | 10,000 yen |

School pupils on school trips, and their supervisors, are exempt. The exemption does not cover university students.

## Planning notes

- Carry an IC card or a 1-Day Pass. City buses do not take credit cards.
- On autumn and spring weekends, the bureau's advice is to combine the subway or trains with the bus rather than ride a bus the whole way.
- The sightseeing express buses run only on weekends and holidays. Check the bureau's site before you rely on them.
- For when the leaves turn and where to see them, see [autumn leaves in Kyoto](/articles/kyoto-autumn-leaves). For rail passes, see [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).
