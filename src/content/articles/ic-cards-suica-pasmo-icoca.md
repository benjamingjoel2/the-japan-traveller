IC cards are rechargeable contactless cards used to pay fares and make small purchases across Japan. This article explains what they do, where they work, which versions are sold only to visitors, and how the 2023 to 2025 card shortage affected what you can buy. JR East's own English pages could not be retrieved for this article, so details of Welcome Suica come from joint JR East and PASMO announcements and from JNTO.

## What an IC card does

You load money onto the card and touch it on the reader at a ticket gate or on a bus. JNTO says IC cards let you transfer between train lines and board buses and boats "with a minimum of fuss", and that they also work at some vending machines, convenience stores and taxis. JNTO's credit card page suggests Suica or PASMO "as an alternative to carrying cash", while noting that they cannot be used everywhere.

PASMO's rules say a card cannot be topped up at station machines if the balance would go over 20,000 yen.

## Where they work

JNTO says most regions have their own card. Suica is issued by JR East and PASMO by the Tokyo-area private railways and bus companies. ICOCA is JR West's card. PASMO lists ten interoperable transport IC cards:

- Kitaca, Suica, PASMO, TOICA, manaca, ICOCA, PiTaPa, HAYAKAKEN, nimoca and SUGOCA.

PASMO notes that PiTaPa does not support nationwide use as electronic money. JR West says ICOCA can be used on railways and buses across Japan and in shops displaying the IC or ICOCA mark. PASMO's own page notes that the nationwide network does not cover every line.

## Deposits and refunds on the standard cards

JNTO says most IC cards need a 500 yen deposit when you buy them. For PASMO, the 500 yen deposit cannot be spent on fares or shopping. When you return the card, PASMO refunds the stored balance and the deposit. The balance cannot be refunded on its own.

JR West sells a special-design ICOCA for foreign visitors at the JR ticket office at Kansai Airport Station. It costs 2,000 yen, including a 500 yen deposit. Buyers must hold a passport issued by a government other than Japan and be visiting as a temporary visitor. It is limited to one per person.

## Cards for visitors

**Welcome Suica.** JR East's card for short-term visitors. JNTO says it is available without a deposit. A joint JR East and PASMO notice of August 2024 said that from 1 September 2024 Welcome Suica would be sold at travel service centres at Tokyo, Shinagawa, Shibuya, Shinjuku, Ikebukuro and Ueno stations and at Narita Airport, Airport Terminal 2 and Haneda Airport Terminal 3 stations, at JAPAN RAIL CAFE TOKYO, and from dedicated machines at the three airport stations, with a limit of one card per person. Check JR East for current sales points and validity before you travel.

**Welcome Suica Mobile.** A smartphone app for visitors. The joint JR East and PASMO notice of 18 February 2025 said it was due to launch on 6 March 2025 and would support iOS only.

**TOURIST PASMO.** PASMO announced this card on 17 April 2026, with a launch in May 2026. PASMO says it:

- is valid for 28 days from the date of purchase;
- has no deposit, and no refunds, including of unused value;
- is sold at Haneda Airport and Narita Airport;
- costs 2,000 yen at Narita (including 2,000 yen of value), and comes in 1,000, 2,000, 3,000, 4,000, 5,000 and 10,000 yen denominations at Haneda;
- can be used on trains and buses that accept IC cards across Japan and at participating shops.

**PASMO PASSPORT.** This earlier visitor card is no longer available. PASMO says sales had already ended, all handling ended on 11 October 2024, and its rules were abolished from 12 October 2024.

## Mobile cards

PASMO's site refers to topping up Suica, PASMO and ICOCA in the Apple Wallet app. PASMO says its card can be added in the Wallet app on iPhone 8 or later running iOS 14.0, or on Apple Watch Series 3 or later. JR West says ICOCA can be used on iPhone through Apple Pay. A joint JR East and PASMO notice of August 2024 said that Mobile Suica and mobile PASMO need no 500 yen deposit.

PASMO states that Android devices bought outside Japan are not compatible with Mobile PASMO.

## The 2023 to 2025 sales suspensions

A global semiconductor shortage disrupted the supply of IC chips. JR East and PASMO announced the following:

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| 8 June 2023 | Sales of new non-personalised Suica and PASMO cards suspended |
| 2 August 2023 | Sales of new personalised Suica and PASMO cards suspended; Welcome Suica limited to airport stations, one per customer |
| 1 September 2024 | Personalised Suica and PASMO cards back on sale |
| 1 March 2025 | Non-personalised Suica and PASMO cards back on sale |

During the suspension, cards already issued could still be used and topped up as normal, and Mobile Suica and Mobile PASMO were unaffected.

## Before you go

- If you have an iPhone, a mobile card avoids the deposit. Check your phone model before relying on it.
- If you arrive at Narita or Haneda, TOURIST PASMO and Welcome Suica are both sold at the airport stations. Neither needs a deposit.
- A standard card has a 500 yen deposit, which is refunded with the balance when you return a PASMO.
- IC cards cover everyday fares. For Shinkansen tickets and passes, see /guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass.
- For paying for other things, see /questions/do-i-need-cash-in-japan.
