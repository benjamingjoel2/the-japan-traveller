Japan's door-to-door parcel services, known as takkyubin, let visitors send suitcases ahead to their next hotel or to the airport. This article covers how it works, what the main operator says about sizes, times and prices, and how it fits with the Shinkansen luggage rules.

## What takkyubin is

Yamato Transport's takkyubin service is called TA-Q-BIN in English. Its English site describes TA-Q-BIN as offering "next-day delivery to most areas in Japan all year round", with a choice of delivery date and time range.

JNTO's luggage page names Yamato Transport as one of the carriers visitors can use, and lists the services travellers usually want:

- delivery from Narita, Haneda and Kansai International airports to your hotel;
- delivery between hotels, which JNTO says "most major hotels can arrange";
- sending from a designated convenience store if you are not staying in a large hotel;
- same-day delivery or delivery on a set date and time, depending on the service.

Yamato's visitor page says its service covers all 47 prefectures. It notes that delivery may be unavailable or delayed on remote islands and in areas affected by disasters.

## Hands-Free Travel counters

JNTO describes a network of counters certified as "Hands-Free Travel", marked with a logo. At these counters you hand over your bags and fill in a delivery form. JNTO says the counters offer multilingual help for international visitors. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism publishes a list of certified counters, linked from the JNTO page.

Yamato's visitor page says no reservation is needed. You can drop luggage at an airport counter, a city sales office, a station counter or a hotel front desk. If you are sending to a hotel, the hotel must confirm in advance that it will accept luggage. To collect, you show ID or your receipt at the destination.

## Sending from a hotel or convenience store

Yamato lists several ways to send a parcel:

- ask your hotel's front desk;
- take it to a Yamato Transport sales office;
- take it to a TA-Q-BIN agency or a convenience store showing official TA-Q-BIN signs and flags;
- request a pickup.

Some restrictions apply at convenience stores. Yamato says parcels larger than Size 180 cannot be sent from some stores, and that payment methods there vary by store. At Yamato's own sales offices and agencies, accepted payment includes cash, credit cards from the main international brands, and transport IC cards such as Suica, PASMO and ICOCA.

On timing, Yamato's visitor page says luggage dropped at a sales office by around 6.00pm (Japan time) is delivered the next day, and that office hours vary.

## Sizes, weights and prices

TA-Q-BIN prices are set by size band and by payment method. Size is the total of length, width and height in centimetres. Yamato's published bands are:

| Size | Total of three sides | Maximum weight |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | up to 60 cm | 2 kg |
| 80 | up to 80 cm | 5 kg |
| 100 | up to 100 cm | 10 kg |
| 120 | up to 120 cm | 15 kg |
| 140 | up to 140 cm | 20 kg |
| 160 | up to 160 cm | 25 kg |
| 180 | up to 180 cm | 30 kg |
| 200 | up to 200 cm | 30 kg |

If a parcel is over the limit for its size or its weight, the higher band applies. The longest side may be up to 170 cm. Yamato's visitor page says prices depend on size and distance. As an example, it gives a Size 160 suitcase sent from the Tokyo Station sales office to a hotel in Kyoto as "from 3,160 JPY" (standard fare, tax included, before discounts, as shown in November 2026). Yamato lists a 100 yen discount per parcel for dropping it at a sales office, agency or convenience store.

JNTO's general guidance for luggage delivery services is items "not exceeding 160 linear cm, within 25 to 30 kg in weight", with contents worth under 300,000 yen. It lists valuables, fragile items, fresh food and liquids, and dangerous goods as not accepted, and says fees vary by airport, destination and luggage size.

## Airport TA-Q-BIN

Yamato's Airport TA-Q-BIN carries suitcases to or from the airport. For departures, Yamato says to drop off your bags "by two days prior to your departure date (three days in some areas)". It delivers to the airport by the day before departure, and you collect the bags at the airport counter on the day you fly, bringing the sender receipt from the waybill. Airport rates include a 660 yen airport handling fee. Each parcel is insured up to 300,000 yen. Parcels over 30 kg are not accepted.

Yamato also offers a return service, which can be used up to one month from the day you drop off your luggage.

## Why it helps on the Shinkansen

JR Central's rules for the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen are a common reason to send bags ahead:

- Bags with total dimensions up to 160 cm can go on the overhead racks without a reservation.
- Bags from 161 to 250 cm need a "seat with an oversized baggage area", booked when you buy your ticket. JR Central says there is no extra charge for this seat.
- If you bring an oversized bag without that reservation, JR Central charges a 1,000 yen fee (tax included).
- Bags over 250 cm in total cannot be taken on board.

For more on Shinkansen tickets and seat reservations, see /guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass.

## Planning notes

- Allow at least a day. Next-day delivery is the standard, and Airport TA-Q-BIN needs your bags two days before your flight (three in some areas).
- Keep an overnight bag with you, since the suitcase arrives after you do.
- Check with your next hotel that it will accept a parcel before you send it.
- Keep medicines, valuables and documents in your hand luggage. JNTO lists valuables and fragile items as not accepted.
- Yamato runs an English helpline, open 8.00am to 9.00pm every day; contact details are on its English site.
- Coin lockers are an alternative for a few hours. JNTO says to expect to pay 300 to 600 yen depending on size, and that lockers in busy places fill quickly.
