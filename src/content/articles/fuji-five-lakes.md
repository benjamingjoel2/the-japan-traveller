The Fuji Five Lakes, or Fujigoko, lie on the Yamanashi side of Mount Fuji. Kawaguchiko is the easiest of the five to reach from Tokyo and the usual base. This article covers the lakes, the train and bus links from Tokyo, the Chureito Pagoda viewpoint in Fujiyoshida, and official guidance on seeing the mountain. For the summit climb, see [Climbing Mount Fuji](/articles/climbing-mount-fuji).

## The five lakes

The descriptions below come from JNTO and the Yamanashi prefectural tourism guide.

| Lake | What the official guides say |
|---|---|
| Kawaguchiko | The most visited and easiest to reach from Tokyo; the main entry point, with lakeside hotels, hot springs and excursion boats. Autumn leaves along the northern shore in early November. |
| Yamanakako | The largest of the five, and the closest to Mount Fuji. It has the third-highest elevation of any lake in Japan. Oshino Hakkai, a set of springs fed by Fuji's groundwater, is nearby. |
| Saiko | The second smallest but second deepest, with a maximum depth of 71.7 m. Saiko Bat Cave, the Narusawa Ice Cave and the thatched village of Saiko Iyashi no Sato Nenba are here. |
| Shojiko | The smallest and least developed. The Eboshi-dake viewpoint looks over the reflected "upside-down Fuji". |
| Motosuko | The westernmost and deepest. The view of Fuji from its north shore appears on the 1,000 yen note. |

JNTO says buses run from Kawaguchiko Station to all five lakes. Yamanashi's guide notes that Fujikawaguchiko town and Fuji Kyuko started a sightseeing "Retro Bus" around Lake Kawaguchiko in spring 2005.

## By train via Otsuki

The Fujikyu Railway (run by Fuji Kyuko) is a private line of 26.6 km. It starts at Otsuki, where it meets the JR Chuo Line, and climbs about 500 m to Kawaguchiko. Fujikyu gives Otsuki at 358 m and Kawaguchiko at 857 m. Stops near the end of the line include Shimoyoshida (for Arakurayama Sengen Park), Mt. Fuji, Fujikyu Highland and Kawaguchiko.

JNTO's route is a JR Chuo Line express to Otsuki, then a change to the Fujikyu line. There are also through trains:

- **Fuji Excursion** (also called Fuji Kaiyu): a limited express from Shinjuku, with some trains starting at Chiba. It runs four round trips a day, with one more in some seasons. The fastest timetabled run from Shinjuku to Kawaguchiko is 1 hour 53 minutes. All seats are reserved, and tickets go on sale one month before travel. The one-way fare from Shinjuku to Kawaguchiko is 4,200 yen (2,580 yen basic fare plus 1,620 yen limited express). JR and Fujikyu fares are charged for their own sections.
- **JR direct rapid trains:** under the timetable in force from 14 March 2026, two trains a day run each way between Tokyo and Kawaguchiko.

Fujikyu also runs its own express trains on the line, the Fujisan View Express and the Fujisan Express, which can be booked online. Fujikyu notes that services may be suspended without notice for bad weather or inspections.

## By highway bus from Shinjuku

Fuji Kyuko and Keio jointly run highway buses from Busta Shinjuku, the bus terminal on the 4th floor directly connected to Shinjuku Station's south exit.

- **Journey time:** about 1 hour 45 minutes to Kawaguchiko Station, depending on traffic.
- **Fare:** adults from 2,200 yen one way, or 2,000 yen when paying online to Kawaguchiko Station or Fuji-Q Highland. Children pay half.
- **Seats:** all reserved. Booking opens one month before the travel date.
- **Booking sites:** from 1 October 2026, Fuji Kyuko's own services are sold online through its Fujiyama Connect site. Keio services stay on Highwaybus.com. Changes and cancellations must be made on the site where you booked.
- **Luggage:** luggage reservations are not accepted, and space may run out.

The bus also stops at Shimoyoshida and Fuji-Q Highland on the way.

## Chureito Pagoda and Arakurayama Sengen Park

The classic view of a five-storey pagoda in front of Mount Fuji is from Arakurayama Sengen Park in Fujiyoshida. The city's tourism site gives these details:

- **Steps:** 398 steps up to the observation deck, which reopened on 1 February 2022.
- **Hours:** open 24 hours. The park is lit in the evening, with times that vary by month.
- **Cherry blossom:** about 650 cherry trees bloom in spring. The city says the park is also worth seeing for fresh green leaves, autumn colour and snow.
- **Access:** about 25 minutes on foot from Shimoyoshida Station on the Fujikyu line to the pagoda.
- **Parking:** four paid car parks, at 1,000 to 1,500 yen per 6 hours. Tour buses cannot reach them because the roads are narrow.

The city also asks visitors:

- not to use the toilets inside Shimoyoshida Station, and not to make calls or ask for directions there;
- not to drive to the plaza in front of the memorial tower, which it calls extremely dangerous because of the crowds;
- not to park or stop in front of Shimoyoshida Station except to drop off or pick up.

On nearby Shimoyoshida Honmachi Street, where Fuji appears behind the shop signs, the city asks people to take photographs safely and quietly.

## Seeing the mountain

Fuji is often hidden by cloud. JNTO's viewing guide says:

- crisp, clear winter days give the best views from a distance;
- the mountain is often hidden during the rainy season, from early June to mid-July;
- early morning or late afternoon is best for close views.

## Before you go

- Book the Fuji Excursion or a highway bus seat early for weekends and holidays; both are fully reserved.
- From Kawaguchiko Station, buses run to the Fuji Subaru Line 5th Station. In the JNTO schedule for January 2026, the one-way fare was 1,950 yen, and services could be suspended in heavy snow. Climbing the summit is a separate matter: see [Climbing Mount Fuji](/articles/climbing-mount-fuji).
