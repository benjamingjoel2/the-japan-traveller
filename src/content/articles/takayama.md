Takayama is a town in the mountains of Gifu Prefecture, in the Hida region. This article covers the Sanmachi old town, the two morning markets, Takayama Jinya, the spring and autumn festivals, Hida beef, and how to get there by train and bus. Hours and dates are given as the town's official tourism site and JR Central publish them.

## Sanmachi old town

JNTO describes the Sanmachi district as retaining the atmosphere of the Edo period (1603 to 1867) in its three main streets. It is one of Takayama's Preservation Districts for Groups of Traditional Buildings. JNTO says Sanmachi is a 15-minute walk east of JR Takayama Station. JR Central's travel guide describes "dark-wood merchant houses and small water canals".

The town's official tourism site says the area is known for sake brewing, with breweries marked by balls of cedar leaves (sugidama) hanging at their entrances. JNTO says seven sake breweries remain in the area, each with a history of over 100 years, and that some offer tours and tastings.

## The morning markets

Takayama has two morning markets. Both are open all year, and the official tourism site gives the same hours for each:

| Period | Hours |
|---|---|
| April to November | 7:00 to 12:00 |
| December to March | 8:00 to 12:00 |

- **Miyagawa morning market** runs along the Miyagawa River in the centre of town, between the Kaji-bashi and Yayoi-bashi bridges. Stalls on the riverside sell vegetables, fruit, pickles and spices, and shops on the other side sell Japanese sweets and crafts, including sarubobo dolls and Ichii Itto-bori wood carvings.
- **Jinya-mae morning market** is held in front of Takayama Jinya, about a 10-minute walk from JR Takayama Station. The tourism site says only farmers may open stalls, selling vegetables, dried foods and homemade pickles.

## Takayama Jinya

Takayama Jinya was a local governor's office during the Edo period and was in official use from 1692 to 1969. The official tourism site says there were once more than 60 similar buildings in Japan and Takayama Jinya is the only one left.

| | Details |
|---|---|
| March to October | 8:45 to 17:00 (August: 8:45 to 18:00) |
| November to February | 8:45 to 16:30 |
| Closed | 29 December, 31 December and 1 January |
| Adults | 430 yen (380 yen each in groups of 30 or more) |
| Senior high school students and younger | Free |

## The Takayama Festival

The festival is held twice a year, by two different shrines in two parts of the old town.

- **Spring Takayama Festival (Sanno Matsuri), 14 and 15 April.** The annual festival of Hie Shrine. The official tourism site lists 12 festival floats (yatai), three of which carry marionette (karakuri) performances, held at Takayama Jinya. The floats are lit with lanterns for the night festival on 14 April. The parade area is south of Yasugawa Street.
- **Autumn Takayama Festival (Hachiman Matsuri), 9 and 10 October.** The annual festival of Sakurayama Hachimangu Shrine, celebrating the harvest. The parade area is in the Shimomachi area north of Yasugawa Street. The tourism site says there are 11 floats, but for its current listing only 10 will be displayed because one is under repair. A marionette performance is held on the Hotei-tai float in the shrine precinct, and the night festival is on 9 October.

The spring page states that the festival is not postponed for rain; rain cancels the procession, display, marionette shows and night festival. Procession and performance times are published each year on the tourism site. Accommodation for these dates is in high demand; see [how far ahead to book a ryokan](/questions/how-far-ahead-should-i-book-a-ryokan) and the [April](/seasons/april) and [October](/seasons/october) season pages.

## Hida beef

The official tourism site describes Hida beef (Hida-gyu) as beef from a black-haired Japanese cattle breed raised in Gifu Prefecture for at least 14 months and certified to set yield and quality grades. Beef that does not meet the criteria is sold as "Hida Wagyu". The site traces the brand to a bull named Yasufuku in 1981, and lists sukiyaki, shabu-shabu and yakiniku as ways it is eaten, as well as steak.

## Getting there

**By train from Nagoya.** JR Central's Limited Express Hida runs from Nagoya to Takayama in about 2 hours 20 minutes. From Tokyo, take the Tokaido Shinkansen to Nagoya and change; the town's tourism site gives about 100 minutes for the Nozomi leg. From Osaka, change from the Shinkansen at Nagoya, or take the Hida that the tourism site lists from JR Osaka Station (255 minutes one way). The Hida also runs from Toyama (90 minutes). See the [rail-route table](/rail-routes) and [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

**By highway bus.** The tourism site lists buses to Takayama from:

| From | Journey time (one way) |
|---|---|
| Nagoya (Meitetsu Bus Center) | 165 minutes |
| Shinjuku, Tokyo (Shinjuku Bus Terminal) | 5.5 hours |
| Matsumoto (Matsumoto Bus Terminal) | 150 minutes |
| Toyama (JR Toyama Station) | 140 minutes |
| Osaka (Namba) | 325 minutes |

Buses also run between Takayama and Kanazawa via Shirakawa-go (JR West lists the Takayama to Shirakawa-go to Kanazawa line from the Takayama Nohi Bus Center). For that route, see [Shirakawa-go in winter](/articles/shirakawa-go-in-winter) and [Kanazawa](/articles/kanazawa).
