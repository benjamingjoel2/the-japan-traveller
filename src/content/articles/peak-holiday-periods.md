Japan has three periods each year when domestic travel peaks: New Year, Golden Week and Obon. This article explains what each one is, which national holidays fall in them in 2027 according to the Cabinet Office, and what official tourism sources say visitors should expect.

## Three peak seasons

The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) describes three major holiday seasons when domestic travel peaks and many businesses may close or reduce hours:

| Period | When (per JNTO) | What JNTO says |
|---|---|---|
| New Year (Oshogatsu) | Late December to early January; typically 29 December to 3 January, depending on how the days fall | Many businesses close for several days; a busy and expensive time to travel |
| Golden Week | Late April to early May | A cluster of national holidays; expect crowds and fully booked transport |
| Obon | Mid-August | A traditional family holiday when many people travel back to their hometowns |

JNTO also notes that during these periods some services may be reservation-only because of demand, and advises checking the operator's website.

## New Year

Only 1 January (New Year's Day, Ganjitsu) is a national holiday in the Cabinet Office list. The wider holiday is the custom of Oshogatsu, which JNTO says typically runs from 29 December to 3 January depending on how the days fall. JNTO notes that many shrines hold events on New Year's Eve, 31 December.

In 2027, New Year's Day falls on a Friday. The next national holiday is Coming of Age Day on Monday 11 January 2027.

## Golden Week

Golden Week is a run of four national holidays in late April and early May. The Cabinet Office lists them as:

- **Showa Day** (Showa no Hi), 29 April
- **Constitution Memorial Day** (Kenpo Kinenbi), 3 May
- **Greenery Day** (Midori no Hi), 4 May
- **Children's Day** (Kodomo no Hi), 5 May

Under the national holidays law, a holiday that falls on a Sunday is followed by a substitute holiday on the next non-holiday day. In 2026, 3 May was a Sunday, so 6 May 2026 was a substitute holiday.

**Golden Week 2027, as listed by the Cabinet Office:**

| Date | Day | Holiday |
|---|---|---|
| 29 April 2027 | Thursday | Showa Day |
| 3 May 2027 | Monday | Constitution Memorial Day |
| 4 May 2027 | Tuesday | Greenery Day |
| 5 May 2027 | Wednesday | Children's Day |

No substitute holiday falls in Golden Week 2027. 30 April 2027 (Friday) is not a national holiday. With the weekend of 1 and 2 May, the days from Saturday 1 May to Wednesday 5 May 2027 are five consecutive non-working days for most people with a Monday-to-Friday week.

A JNTO article on Golden Week (written for 2019) says that, apart from the New Year period, it is the busiest time of year for tourism; that travel peaks around the beginning and end of the holiday; that stations are packed and trains run at full capacity; and that Shinkansen bookings are essential and accommodation should be booked in advance.

## Obon

Obon is a traditional summer holiday in mid-August. JNTO describes it as a family holiday when many people travel back to their hometowns. Obon itself is not a national holiday in the Cabinet Office list. The nearby national holiday is Mountain Day (Yama no Hi), which falls on Wednesday 11 August in 2027.

Rail operators sometimes change seating rules for the peak. For example, JNTO reported that from 9 to 18 August 2024 all Nozomi trains on the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen ran with reserved seats only. Check the operator's announcements for the year you travel.

## What to expect as a visitor

Based on what JNTO and the rail operators state:

- **Trains are full.** JNTO says to expect fully booked transport in Golden Week, with travel peaking around its beginning and end. Reserve seats as early as you can in all three periods.
- **Some trains become reserved-only.** Besides the 2024 Obon Nozomi example above, the Takayama-Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass page notes that from 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027 all seats on the Limited Express Hida (Nagoya to Takayama to Toyama) are reserved only. Rail pass holders need a seat reservation on such trains.
- **Accommodation fills early.** JNTO advises booking accommodation in advance for Golden Week, and its reservations guide suggests travelling outside major domestic holidays, such as Golden Week or Obon, for better prices and availability. See [How far ahead should I book a ryokan?](/questions/how-far-ahead-should-i-book-a-ryokan)
- **Some businesses close.** JNTO says many businesses close for several days at New Year.

## Planning notes

- If your dates are flexible, avoid the start and end days of Golden Week, when JNTO says travel peaks.
- If you are using a rail pass, book seat reservations as soon as you can. See [Regional JR passes](/articles/regional-rail-passes) and the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).
