Kumamoto Castle and the Aso caldera are the two main sights of central Kumamoto Prefecture, and both were hit by the Kumamoto earthquakes of April 2016. This article covers what has been restored at the castle and what is still closed, current access to Aso's Nakadake crater, and how to get between Kumamoto and Aso by JR and bus. For hot-spring towns nearby, including Kurokawa, see [Kyushu's onsen towns](/articles/kyushu-onsen-towns).

## Kumamoto Castle: the 2016 damage

Kumamoto Prefecture's tourism site says two earthquakes struck in April 2016, each measuring shindo 7 on the Japanese intensity scale. The castle's official site lists the damage building by building. At the main tower (Tenshukaku), roof tiles and a shachihoko ornament fell and parts of the stone wall crumbled. An 80 m section of the eastern Nagabei Long Wall collapsed. The foundation stones of the Nishi-otemon Gate collapsed and its turret became slanted. In the Honmaru-goten Palace, a hall floor sank and walls in every hall broke. The Uto-Yagura turret's neighbouring Tsuduki-Yagura partly collapsed.

The main tower itself is a 1960 reconstruction. The castle's site says the exteriors of the large and small towers were rebuilt then in steel-reinforced concrete.

## Restoration: what is open and what is not

**Main tower.** The castle's official site says full restoration of the main tower was completed in March 2021. Kumamoto Prefecture's tourism site gave 26 April 2021 as the day visitors could enter it for the first time since the earthquakes. The tower has six floors and a basement, with exhibits on each floor and a viewing floor at the top. The castle's site says three elevators are reserved for wheelchair and pushchair users and people who find stairs difficult.

**Completed repairs.** According to the castle's site, the Nagabei Long Wall was restored in 2021, the Kenmotsu-Yagura in 2023 and the stone wall at Iidamaru Gokai-Yagura in 2024.

**Still closed or under work.** The castle's site lists the Honmaru-goten Palace as no entry, with restoration scheduled. Uto-Yagura, Nishi-otemon Gate and Akazu-no-mon Gate are listed as demolished, with restoration scheduled. In a July 2025 article, Kumamoto City's official guide said dismantling of Uto-Yagura began in 2022, with repair and reassembly projected to finish by 2032. It gave 2027 as the start of major reconstruction at the Honmaru Goten Palace, with completion by 2032.

**Completion date.** The same Kumamoto City guide article says overall completion of the restoration is "currently projected for 2052". A January 2021 prefectural tourism page gave 2037, which has since been superseded.

**The elevated walkway.** JNTO says the Special Observation Path opened on 1 June 2020. It is 350 m long. JNTO describes it as about 6 m high, and the prefectural tourism site gives 5 to 7 m above ground. It lets visitors look over the damage and the repair work. The July 2025 city guide article says the Tago Yagura turret complex can be seen from it.

## Mount Aso and the Nakadake crater

JNTO describes the Aso caldera as one of the largest in the world, 18 km from east to west and 25 km from north to south. Five peaks rise from the caldera floor. One of them, Nakadake, is still active. Its crater holds an acidic pale turquoise pool from which steam rises.

**Current restrictions.** The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) raised Asosan to Volcanic Alert Level 3 (entry restriction) on 14 August 2026 after a sharp rise in sulphur dioxide emissions and volcanic tremor. It lowered the level to 2 (restriction around the crater) on 1 September 2026. As of JMA's update of 6 November 2026, Level 2 remains in force. JMA says people should be alert to large ballistic rocks and pyroclastic flows within about 1 km of the Nakadake No. 1 crater, watch for ash and small rocks downwind, and be aware of volcanic gas. It says Aso City and Minamiaso Village should keep crater-area entry restrictions in place.

In short, the crater rim cannot be visited at present. JR Kyushu says crater access depends on the alert level and the weather. When the crater is closed, JNTO says the Aso Volcano Museum shows a 15-minute video. Kusasenri, the grassland between Mt Kishima and Mt Eboshi, is opposite the museum.

**Aso Shrine.** JNTO says the shrine's tower gate (a National Treasure) and worship hall were badly damaged in 2016. The shrine is under reconstruction but remains open.

## Getting to Aso

**JR Hohi Line.** The 2016 earthquakes cut the line between Higo-Ozu and Aso, about 27 km. The Ministry of the Environment's Kyushu office says a slope in the Tateno gorge collapsed, cutting National Route 57 and the railway. The section reopened on 8 August 2020, after 4 years and 4 months, so trains again run from Kumamoto to Aso. JNTO gives Kumamoto to Higo-Ozu as about 40 minutes and Higo-Ozu to Aso as about 45 minutes.

**ASO BOY!** JR Kyushu's sightseeing train runs Kumamoto to Miyaji by way of Shin-Suizenji, Higo-Ozu, Tateno and Aso. On the timetable dated January 2026 there are 2 round trips on operating days, and Kumamoto to Miyaji takes 1 hour 35 minutes. All seats are reserved. Check JR Kyushu's operating calendar; on some dates another train runs in its place.

**Buses.** JNTO gives about 50 minutes from Kumamoto Airport to Aso Station on the Kyushu Odan Bus, and 2 hours 20 minutes from Beppu to Aso Station by train, changing at Oita. From Aso Station, JNTO gives about 35 minutes by bus to Aso Sanjo Terminal. From 1 July 2026, the Asochiho bus between Aso and Takachiho runs via Kusasenri and Aso Sanjo Terminal, and Aso City's tourism site says reservations take priority. See [Takachiho Gorge and its shrines](/articles/takachiho-gorge).

## Before you go

- Check JMA's volcanic warnings on the day. The crater area can close at short notice, and the alert level was changed twice in August and September 2026.
- At the castle, the Honmaru-goten Palace and several turrets remain closed. Allow time for the elevated walkway as well as the main tower.
- JNTO says public buses around Aso are limited and many sights are far from stations. Plan connections in advance. For JR passes covering Kyushu, see [regional rail passes](/articles/regional-rail-passes) and the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).
