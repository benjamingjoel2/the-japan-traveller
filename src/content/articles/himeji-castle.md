Himeji Castle stands in the centre of Himeji, in Hyogo Prefecture. It is a National Treasure and was one of Japan's first two World Heritage sites. This article covers what survives, the opening hours and the higher admission prices that took effect on 1 March 2026, the neighbouring Koko-en garden, and how to get there by Shinkansen.

## Status and history

Himeji City states that the castle was inscribed on the World Heritage List on 11 December 1993, together with Horyu-ji in Nara, as Japan's first World Cultural Heritage sites. The Agency for Cultural Affairs' list of World Heritage properties in Japan gives the same year. Himeji City's history page dates the National Treasure designation to 1931.

The site's history goes back to 1333, when Akamatsu Norimura built a fort on Himeyama hill. A full castle followed in 1346. According to Himeji City, Ikeda Terumasa began a major rebuilding in 1601. The linked main keep group was completed in 1609, and the castle as seen today was complete by 1617. The city's history page says the castle has come down to the present without being damaged in war.

Two large repair projects have taken place since. The keep was taken apart and repaired from 1956 in the Showa repairs, finished in 1964. The Heisei preservation repairs of the main keep began in 2009, and Himeji City records the castle's grand reopening in 2015.

## What survives

Himeji City describes the castle as a well-preserved example of castle building at its height in the early 17th century. It lists the keep group, turrets, gates, earthen walls, stone walls and moats among what survives. The World Heritage property covers about 107 hectares, the same area as the Special Historic Site of Himeji Castle at the time of inscription.

The buildings designated as National Treasures, as listed by Himeji City, are:

- the main keep (dai-tenshu)
- three smaller keeps: the west, inui (north-west) and east small keeps
- four connecting turrets (watari-yagura), known as i, ro, ha and ni, with one attached kitchen building

JR West describes the main keep as having five tiers and six storeys above a basement. JNTO notes that only one of Himeji's three original moats survives intact.

## Opening hours and admission

Himeji City gives the opening hours as 9:00 to 17:00, with last entry at 16:00. Windows in the main keep, the Nishi-no-maru and the Hyakken-roka corridor start closing from about 16:30. The castle is closed on 29 and 30 December.

### Admission from 1 March 2026

Himeji City revised admission fees from 1 March 2026. The new prices do not separate foreign visitors from Japanese visitors. They charge visitors who live in Himeji less than everyone else. The fee table is:

| Ticket | Price |
|---|---|
| General, age 18 and over | 2,500 yen |
| Himeji residents, age 18 and over | 1,000 yen |
| Groups of 30 or more, general | 2,000 yen per person |
| Groups of 30 or more, Himeji residents | 800 yen per person |
| Himeji Castle and Koko-en combined ticket | 2,600 yen |
| Annual pass (one year from purchase, same holder) | 5,000 yen |
| Under 18 | Free |

Before the change, the adult (18 and over) fee was 1,000 yen. Residents must show official photo ID with their address, such as a My Number Card, driving licence or residence card. Under-18s may be asked for ID or a student card. Some English-language pages, including JNTO's Himeji Castle page, still give the old 1,000 yen price.

With the fee change, Himeji City also:

- gives each visitor a free shoulder bag to carry shoes in the main keep and the Hyakken-roka corridor, and to keep afterwards
- moved digital tickets into full use
- stopped allowing pets in the paid area, even in carriers. Guide dogs and hearing dogs are still allowed.

### Tickets and crowds

The city recommends the timed digital ticket, which lets you choose a date and time in advance. The official FAQ says digital tickets can be bought up to 90 days ahead. A ticket is valid only on the day it is issued, and re-entry is not allowed. Himeji City notes that a digital ticket does not give priority when the castle is crowded. The main keep admits up to 1,000 people an hour, so there can be a queue at its entrance on busy days.

The FAQ suggests allowing about 1.5 to 2 hours, plus up to about an hour of waiting in busy seasons. Inside the buildings, tripods, monopods and selfie sticks are not allowed. Wheeled bags must go in paid coin lockers. Visit Himeji warns that reaching the top of the main keep means climbing steep stairs under low ceilings. According to the FAQ, wheelchairs cannot enter the buildings.

## Koko-en garden

Koko-en is a garden next to the castle. JNTO describes it as nine separate walled gardens, opened in 1992 to mark Himeji City's centenary. The garden's official site gives these details:

- Hours: 9:00 to 17:00, with last entry 30 minutes before closing
- Closed: 29 and 30 December
- Adult (18 and over): 400 yen. Groups of 30 or more: 320 yen per person.
- Under 18: free
- Combined ticket with Himeji Castle: 2,600 yen

The garden's fees were also revised from 1 March 2026. Hours may differ during events.

## Getting there

Himeji Station is on the Shinkansen. JR West's Himeji Castle page gives the walk from Himeji Station to the castle as about 20 minutes. The alternative is a Shinki Bus to the Himeji-jo Otemon-mae stop, which is a 5-minute walk from the castle.

JR West's page does not give journey times. The Himeji Convention and Visitors Bureau (Visit Himeji) gives these Shinkansen times to Himeji:

| From | Time |
|---|---|
| Shin-Osaka | 28 minutes |
| Okayama | 20 minutes |
| Kyoto | 44 minutes |
| Tokyo | 3 hours 10 minutes |
| Hakata | 1 hour 58 minutes |

Visit Himeji gives 1 hour 1 minute by JR from Osaka Station without the Shinkansen. It also warns that not every Shinkansen stops at Himeji, so check the train before you board. For passes and fares on this line, see [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass) and [rail routes](/rail-routes).

## Before you go

- Buy a timed digital ticket for busy periods, but expect to queue for the main keep anyway.
- Under-18s enter free. Adults pay 2,500 yen unless they live in Himeji.
- Wear shoes you can take off easily, and be ready for steep stairs.
- Allow extra time if you add Koko-en; the combined ticket is 2,600 yen.
- Himeji is covered in the [regions guide](/guide/regions) under Kansai and Kyoto.
