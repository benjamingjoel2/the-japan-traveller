Japan changed how visitors get consumption tax back on 1 November 2026. Before that date, eligible visitors paid no consumption tax at the till. Purchases made on or after that date work on a refund method: you pay the full price and get the tax back after customs confirms you are taking the goods out of Japan.

## What changed on 1 November 2026

The National Tax Agency states that, under the fiscal 2025 tax reform, the refund method was implemented from 1 November 2026. The Japan Tourism Agency describes it this way. Travellers pay the tax-inclusive price at tax-free shops. "An amount equivalent to the consumption tax will be refunded after the export of the goods is confirmed upon departure."

The date that matters is the date of purchase:

| Purchase date | System | What you pay at the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 31 October 2026 | Previous system | No consumption tax charged on eligible goods |
| On or after 1 November 2026 | Refund method | Full tax-inclusive price; refund after customs confirmation |

So on today's date, 7 November 2026, the refund method is in force for anything you buy.

## Who can use it

The Japan Tourism Agency's page on tax-free shops sets out who counts as an eligible purchaser:

- **Foreign nationals who are non-residents.** It must be possible to confirm that less than six months have passed since you entered Japan. The agency excludes diplomatic, official and US military status from this six-month check.
- **Japanese nationals who are non-residents.** The same six-month check applies. Proof must be a valid My Number Card, or a residence certificate or copy of the family register attachment issued on or after the date six months before your last entry.

The agency's traveller FAQ adds that, for Japanese nationals who have lived abroad for two years or more, a residence certificate, a copy of the family register attachment or a My Number Card showing the move abroad can be used.

The online Visit Japan Web procedure is limited to people with a status of residence of "Temporary Visitor", "Diplomat" or "Official". It cannot be used by Japanese nationals.

## The rules for goods

- **Minimum spend.** Purchases must total ¥5,000 or more, excluding tax, per day at the same store. Where shops share a tax-free procedure counter, purchases at several stores on the same day can be combined.
- **No set maximum.** The Japan Tourism Agency page gives no maximum amount. Goods are limited to quantities you can personally carry out of Japan, in their entirety, when you leave.
- **One set of goods.** The old split between general goods and consumables has been abolished, and so has the special sealed packaging for consumables.
- **Do not use consumables in Japan.** If you consume food, drink or cosmetics in Japan, you cannot get the refund for them.
- **Excluded items.** Gold and platinum bullion, and items that are already exempt from consumption tax, are excluded.
- **High-value items.** For items of ¥1 million or more (excluding tax), customs may ask for certificates of authenticity or warranties.

## At departure

You must complete the customs check at the airport or seaport you leave Japan from, within 90 days of the purchase date.

1. Before you check in any baggage, go to a tax-free procedure terminal in the international departure lobby and present your passport. Have all the tax-free goods with you. Checked baggage cannot be retrieved for this.
2. A green result completes the procedure. A red result means customs will inspect your goods at the inspection area.
3. Customs confirms each purchase transaction as a whole. If even one item from a receipt is missing, nothing on that receipt is refunded.
4. Export the goods without delay. If goods that passed the check stay in Japan, the tax is collected and penalties apply.

At Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, New Chitose and Naha airports, you can also do the first step online through Visit Japan Web, using the designated wireless network in the international departure lobby before security. If you transfer from a domestic to an international flight, complete the procedure at the airport where you leave Japan. The same principles apply at seaports and for cruises.

Customs confirmation must be finished before you board. The Japan Tourism Agency says there is no compensation if you miss a flight because you did not allow enough time.

## How the money comes back

The refund is paid by the tax-free shop, or its refund service provider, after customs has confirmed the sale. The National Tax Agency and the Japan Tourism Agency list bank transfer, credit card, app-based transfer and cash at the departure port as possible methods. Which one you get depends on the shop. The shop tells you how to register your refund details when you buy.

The government pages checked for this article do not set a time frame for the refund to arrive, and they do not describe fees. Ask the shop or its refund provider.

## Before you go

- Keep your passport with you when shopping and keep every receipt.
- Pack tax-free goods in your carry-on or in a bag you can show before check-in.
- Allow time at the airport for the terminal step and a possible customs inspection.
- Shipping goods overseas depends on the individual shop. The Japan Tourism Agency notes that the separate shipment method by mail or parcel was abolished on 31 March 2025.
