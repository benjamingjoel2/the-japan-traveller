Kyoto's maples turn later than most of Japan, from mid November into December. This article sets out the official observation dates, how the forecasts work, the viewing areas named by Kyoto's official tourism site and what official sources say about crowds and evening light-ups.

## When the leaves turn

JNTO says autumn leaves "come to Kyoto late, with the season starting in mid-November". Its November guide adds that Kyoto "can be particularly busy at the end of November when the leaves are at their peak". For Japan as a whole, JNTO's autumn guide gives mid November to early December as the time the trees turn.

The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) keeps a longer record. At its Kyoto station it watches a specimen tree, normally an iroha maple (iroha kaede). The colouring date is the first day on which most of the tree's leaves have turned red, looking at the tree as a whole.

| Kyoto maple colouring date (JMA) | Date |
|---|---|
| Normal (average for 1991–2020) | 5 December |
| Earliest on record | 10 November (1976) |
| Latest on record | 20 December (2024) |
| 2025 | 10 December, 5 days later than normal |

Recent years have been late. The observed dates for 2016 to 2025 were 6 December, 1 December, 12 December, 10 December, 7 December, none recorded in 2021, 12 December, 13 December, 20 December and 10 December.

The JMA date marks one tree at one site being mostly red. Gardens and hillsides around the city colour over several weeks, so they are not the same as the JMA date. The Kyoto City Tourism Association's calendar shows peak viewing dates from previous years at its listed spots ranging from 30 October to 11 December.

## How the forecasts work

Two kinds of information are published each autumn.

- **Peak forecasts.** The Japan Weather Association (JWA) publishes autumn leaves forecasts on tenki.jp. Its forecasts are for iroha maple. JWA notes that peak colour tends to come earlier when autumn (September to November) is cool and later when it is warm. In its third 2026 forecast, issued on 28 October 2026, JWA predicted peak colour at Arashiyama from around 13 November, the same as in 2025. For western Japan it expected peak timing to be normal or later than normal.
- **Live reports.** tenki.jp rates spots in five stages: green leaves, colouring begins, peak, fading begins and leaves fallen. Reports come in daily from viewing spots across Japan. On 7 November 2026, a JWA forecaster wrote that colouring had begun in Kansai.

The Kyoto City Tourism Association runs its own Autumn Leaves Calendar for 2026. It began updating on 30 October 2026 and uses five stages: Green, Color Appearing, Partially Colored, Peak and Past Peak. On 6 November 2026 it reported that trees were "beginning to change colors at many spots throughout Kyoto City" and that the leaves at Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei had already reached their peak.

## Where to see them

The official calendar groups its spots into these areas:

- Central Kyoto City and the area around Kyoto Station
- Gion and Kiyomizu
- Philosopher's Path and Okazaki
- Kinugasa and Kitano
- Saga and Arashiyama
- Takao and Nishikyo
- Kurama and Kibune, and Ohara
- Yamashina, Fushimi and Keihoku
- Places outside Kyoto City

Some places named by official sources:

- **Tofuku-ji.** The Kyoto City Tourism Association calls it "one of the most popular spots" for autumn leaves. The maples beside its Tsutenkyo Bridge are the centrepiece. JNTO says it is a 10-minute walk from Tofukuji Station, reached from Kyoto Station on the JR Nara Line or the Keihan Line.
- **Eikando (Zenrinji).** JNTO describes it as "famous for brilliant foliage in the fall", with evening illumination. It lies just south of the Philosopher's Path. JNTO notes that the season "draws huge crowds".
- **Kiyomizu-dera.** JNTO names it as a viewing spot and says it holds a night light-up for the leaves.
- **Mount Hiei Enryaku-ji.** Its Momiji (Autumn Leaves) Festival runs from 31 October to 23 November 2026, according to the Kyoto City Tourism Association.
- **Others pictured in the official calendar update** for 6 November 2026: Daikaku-ji, Obai-in (a sub-temple of Daitoku-ji), Hozukyo Gorge and Ninna-ji.

The tourism association suggests the quieter countryside "just a stretch away" as an alternative to the famous gardens. Its autumn tips page also highlights the Yamashina, Daigo and Okazaki areas.

## Crowds and getting around

The official advice is practical rather than prescriptive. The Kyoto City Tourism Association's autumn tips page points to Kyoto Smart Navi, which shows real-time crowding at tourist sites and needs no app or registration. It also says that at Kyoto Station you can store luggage or have it sent to your accommodation the same day.

For buses, the tips page mentions the Subway and Bus 1-Day Pass. It covers all subway and city bus lines and Kyoto Bus, Keihan Bus and West Japan JR Bus for one day.

The official sources consulted do not give advice on visiting early in the morning.

## Evening light-ups

The Kyoto City Tourism Association says that "many temples, shrines and gardens organize special illumination and opening events between October and December". It publishes a list of the 2026 events with links to each venue. Eikando and Kiyomizu-dera are among the temples JNTO names for evening illumination. JNTO describes the Kiyomizu-dera light-up as running from mid to late November. Dates, hours and any booking rules are set by each temple, so check the venue's own page for the current year.

## Planning notes

- Kyoto's leaves come late. Mid November to early December covers the usual season; the JMA normal for its specimen tree is 5 December.
- The last week of November is busy, according to JNTO. For booking a stay over this period, see [How far ahead should I book a ryokan?](/questions/how-far-ahead-should-i-book-a-ryokan)
- Watch the forecasts from October and the official calendar once it starts updating.
