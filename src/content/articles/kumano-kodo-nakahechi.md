The Nakahechi is the best-known route of the Kumano Kodo, the old pilgrimage roads of the Kii Peninsula in Wakayama. This article covers the route's main stages, how to reach the trailheads by train and bus, luggage shuttles and lodging, the seasons, and the stamp credential. All figures come from the Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau, the official tourism body for the area.

## What the Kumano Kodo is

The Kumano Kodo is a network of pilgrimage routes leading to the three Grand Shrines of Kumano, known together as Kumano Sanzan: Kumano Hongu Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha and Kumano Nachi Taisha. The routes were inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage on 7 July 2004, as part of "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range". That listing also includes Koyasan and Yoshino and Omine.

The tourism bureau describes four main routes:

- **Nakahechi** (Imperial Route): from Tanabe on the west coast, east into the mountains to the Grand Shrines.
- **Kohechi**: a north-south crossing from Koyasan to Kumano, about 70 km long, with three passes above 1,000 m.
- **Ohechi**: the coastal route south from Tanabe.
- **Iseji**: the east-coast route between Ise-jingu and Kumano Sanzan.

The Nakahechi was used by the imperial family on pilgrimages from Kyoto from the 10th century. The bureau calls it the main route linking the three Grand Shrines.

## The Nakahechi stages

Most multi-day walkers start at Takijiri-oji, near the Tonda River, and finish at Kumano Hongu Taisha. The full walk from Takijiri-oji to Hongu is about 38 km. The bureau splits it into two long days, with an overnight in or near Chikatsuyu:

| Stage | Distance | Walking time | Climb / descent | Bureau difficulty rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Takijiri-oji to Chikatsuyu-oji | about 13.3 km | 6 to 8 hours | about 1,010 m up, 840 m down | 3 |
| Chikatsuyu-oji to Kumano Hongu Taisha | about 24.5 km | 9 to 12 hours | about 1,100 m up, 1,380 m down | 4 |
| Hosshinmon-oji to Kumano Hongu Taisha (final section) | about 7.5 km | 2 to 3 hours | about 190 m up, 460 m down | 2 |

The first day passes through Takahara village. The Kumano Kodo Kan centre at Takijiri-oji has route exhibitions, sells walking sticks and has a water tap. The second day follows paved road through Nonaka to Kobiro-toge Pass, then mountain trail to Hosshinmon-oji. The bureau notes there are no restaurants along this stage, so bring lunch. Many walkers shorten it by bus.

The Hosshinmon-oji to Hongu section works as a half-day walk. Hosshinmon-oji marks the outer entrance to the shrine's sacred precincts. There is no lodging at Hosshinmon-oji, so plan your bus back.

Daimon-zaka, the stone staircase to Kumano Nachi Taisha, Seiganto-ji Temple and Nachi Falls, is a separate short walk. The slope is about 600 m long with 267 steps and takes about 30 minutes. Buses run from Kii-Katsuura Station to its base in about 20 minutes.

## Getting there

The gateway is JR Kii-Tanabe Station. JR West's Kuroshio limited express runs from Osaka and Kyoto. The tourism bureau gives these times to Kii-Tanabe:

| From | Time |
|---|---|
| Tennoji | 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Shin-Osaka | 2 hours |
| Kyoto | 2 hours 35 minutes (not all trains run from Kyoto; many start at Shin-Osaka) |

From Nagoya, JR Central's Wideview Nanki runs to Kii-Katsuura via Shingu in about 4 hours. Nanki-Shirahama Airport has daily flights to and from Tokyo.

Local buses leave from Bus Stop 2 at Kii-Tanabe Station for Takijiri and for Kumano Hongu Taisha, with stops at Yunomine and Kawayu Onsen. Buses also run from Shirahama. Several companies run them, not JR, so the Japan Rail Pass does not cover them. Timetables change by season; download the current ones from the bureau's bus page. See the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass) for the wider network.

## Luggage and lodging

**Luggage shuttle.** A daily luggage shuttle moves bags between accommodations on the Nakahechi. It is booked only through KUMANO TRAVEL, the reservation system linked from the bureau's site. Online booking is possible only if your accommodation is also booked through KUMANO TRAVEL. Book at least 20 days ahead. Last-minute shuttles and on-site changes are not possible. Pack everything inside the bag; items tied to the outside may lead to extra fees or refusal.

**Next-day shipping.** Yamato Transport (TA-Q-BIN) and Japan Post (Yu-Pack) ship bags without advance booking. The maximum is often 160 cm (length plus width plus height) and 25 kg. A 7-Eleven inside Kii-Tanabe Station (6:00 to 22:00) accepts parcels.

**Storage.** The TANABE Tourist Information Center, next to Kii-Tanabe Station, stores bags daily from 9:00 to 18:00 for 500 yen per bag per day, for up to one month, with no reservation needed. Station coin lockers cost 400 to 700 yen per day, for a maximum of 3 days.

**Lodging.** The bureau directs walkers to book accommodation through the KUMANO TRAVEL community reservation system. Its tips page strongly recommends booking ahead: walk-in lodging is uncommon and rooms are limited in small settlements. Evening buses are limited too. For general advice on inns, see [ryokan and onsen](/guide/ryokan-and-onsen) and [how far ahead to book a ryokan](/questions/how-far-ahead-should-i-book-a-ryokan).

## Seasons and trail conditions

The Nakahechi is open all year. The bureau describes the Kii Peninsula as mild but one of the wettest areas of Japan. Typhoons can bring flash floods and landslides, and JR trains may be cancelled or delayed.

- **Spring and autumn** are the most popular seasons. Buses can be crowded or full.
- **Summer** is hot, humid and wet.
- **Winter** can bring freezing temperatures. Snow at higher levels is rare and usually melts quickly. Days are shortest.

From late autumn to early spring the bureau advises reaching your destination between 15:00 and 17:00, as valley villages get dark early. Carry a light.

The trail mixes uneven paths, old cobblestones and stairs, and paved village roads. The bureau says it is not technical but is physically demanding, with steep climbs. Most injuries are slips on stones and roots, and cobbled steps are slippery when wet. Natural water sources are untested; carry water and refill at your lodging. Wildlife to watch for includes the mamushi pit viper, hornets (most aggressive in autumn), leeches and ticks. Asiatic bears are rare. ## Before you go

- **Stamps.** A free stamp credential has a Kumano Kodo side and a Way of St. James side. Stamps sit in wooden stands at most sites. The completion stamp is at Kumano Hongu Taisha. Credentials are available at the TANABE Tourist Information Center, the Kumano Hongu Heritage Center and the Kumano Kodo Kan at Takijiri-oji, among others.
- **Dual Pilgrim.** Walkers who complete both the Way of St. James and a qualifying Kumano Kodo walk (for example, Takijiri-oji to Hongu on foot) can register in person in Tanabe or Santiago de Compostela.
- **Cash.** The bureau notes that many places do not accept cards. Post office ATMs are the most reliable for withdrawals.
- **Emergencies.** How to reach emergency services and the JNTO Japan Visitor Hotline is covered in [earthquakes, tsunami and safety alerts](/articles/earthquakes-and-safety).
- **Closures.** Check the bureau's tips page for current closures and detours before you set out.
