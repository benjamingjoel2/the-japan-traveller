Kinosaki Onsen is a hot-spring town in Hyogo Prefecture. The Tottori Sand Dunes are in neighbouring Tottori Prefecture, on the Sea of Japan coast. This article covers Kinosaki's seven public baths and day pass, yukata etiquette, trains to Kinosaki, and how to reach and visit the dunes.

## Kinosaki Onsen

Visit Hyogo, the prefecture's official tourism site, says Kinosaki has a history of about 1,300 years and has long drawn writers and artists, among them the novelist Naoya Shiga. JNTO repeats a legend that a priest named Douchi Shonin came to Kinosaki in 717. Visit Hyogo describes the town as designed to work like a single ryokan: "the inns are the guest rooms, and the public baths are the grand bath." The willow-lined Otani River runs through the centre.

## The seven public baths

Visit Kinosaki lists seven public bathhouses. Its hours and closing days in November 2026 were:

| Bathhouse | Hours | Closed |
|---|---|---|
| Kono-yu | 7:00 to 23:00 | Tuesday |
| Mandara-yu | 15:00 to 23:00 | Wednesday |
| Goshono-yu | 7:00 to 23:00 | Thursday |
| Ichino-yu | 7:00 to 23:00 | Wednesday |
| Yanagi-yu | 15:00 to 23:00 | Thursday |
| Jizo-yu | 7:00 to 23:00 | Monday |
| Satono-yu | No hours listed | Closed for renovation since 1 April 2024 |

Visit Kinosaki also listed Kono-yu as closed for renovation from 11 May to 30 October 2026. Check that it has reopened before you go.

According to Visit Hyogo, Ichino-yu was known as Arayu in the mid-Edo period and was renamed after the physician Kagawa Shutoku praised it as "the best in the land".

### Tattoos

Visit Kinosaki states that all of Kinosaki's public bathhouses allow tattoos of all shapes and sizes. Visit Hyogo adds that every inn and bath in the town is tattoo-friendly. For bathing etiquette in general, see [ryokan and onsen](/guide/ryokan-and-onsen).

## The Yumepa pass

The day pass to the public baths is called Yumepa. Visit Kinosaki gives the price as 1,500 yen and says day visitors can buy it at any of the bathhouses. Visit Hyogo gives 1,500 yen for adults and 750 yen for children, and notes that the pass does not include Satono-yu while it is closed. JNTO's Kinosaki page still quotes 1,300 yen "as of November 2021", which is out of date.

If you stay overnight, Visit Kinosaki says accommodation in the town usually gives guests a Yumepa pass at check-in for all the public baths. It advises checking with your accommodation that this is offered. JNTO describes ryokan guests as using the public baths free of charge from check-in to check-out.

## Strolling in yukata

Visit Kinosaki says guests are given a yukata by their accommodation to wear during their stay. They are encouraged to walk around town in it, and shops, restaurants, bars and attractions welcome visitors in yukata. The site notes that yukata were first designed as an alternative to towels when bathing. Visitors not staying overnight can rent a yukata for the day in town.

## Getting to Kinosaki

Kinosaki Onsen Station is on the San'in Main Line. Visit Kinosaki says it can be reached by JR limited express trains from Kyoto, Osaka, Sannomiya and Himeji. JR West runs two limited expresses to the town:

| Train | Route as listed by JR West |
|---|---|
| Limited Express Kinosaki | Kyoto to Fukuchiyama, Toyooka and Kinosaki Onsen |
| Limited Express Konotori | Shin-Osaka to Fukuchiyama, Toyooka and Kinosaki Onsen |

JR West's train pages do not give journey times. JNTO gives about 2.5 hours from Kyoto or Osaka to Kinosaki Onsen Station by direct express train. Visit Hyogo gives about two and a half hours from Kobe, Osaka or Kyoto by limited express. Not every train runs the full route, so check the destination when you book.

Visit Kinosaki states that all limited express trains require a seat reservation in advance. It also says a Japan Rail Pass or a JR West Rail Pass can be used to reach Kinosaki Onsen. See [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass) and [regional rail passes](/articles/regional-rail-passes).

## The Tottori Sand Dunes

Tottori Prefecture's tourism site describes the dunes as stretching 2.4 km north to south and 16 km east to west. JR West gives 2 km by 16 km. JR West states that a section of the dunes is part of San'in Kaigan National Park. The prefectural site says the dunes lie in the heart of the San'in Kaigan Geopark.

The small hill called Umanose, "the horse's back", gives a view over the sea. The prefecture lists camel rides, paragliding, a lift called the Dune Lift, cycling and sandboarding among the activities on the dunes.

The Tottori Sand Dunes Visitor Center is open all year from 9:00 to 17:00 and has free maps of the dunes. The visitor center states that sand graffiti and writing messages on the dunes are prohibited by ordinance.

### Getting there

Visit Tottori City, the city's official tourism site, gives these buses from the Tottori Bus Terminal at the north exit of JR Tottori Station:

| Bus | Days | Time to the dunes | Fare |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local bus 39 (Tottori Sakyu Line) or 32 and 33 (Iwami Iwai Line) | Daily | About 22 minutes | 380 yen each way |
| Loop Kirinjishi Bus | Weekends and holidays only | About 20 minutes | 400 yen a ride, or 700 yen for a one-day pass |

The closest stop to the dunes is Sakyu-kaikan. The site gives the last bus from Sakyu-kaikan to Tottori Station as 18:30. IC cards are not accepted on local buses in Tottori Prefecture, so carry cash; the site notes there are no convenience stores or ATMs near the dunes.

To reach Tottori Station from Tokyo, Visit Tottori City gives the Nozomi Shinkansen to Himeji (about 3 hours), then the Limited Express Super Hakuto to Tottori (about 1 hour 35 minutes).

## Planning notes

- Each open Kinosaki bathhouse closes on one day a week, and Satono-yu has been closed since April 2024.
- Day visitors pay 1,500 yen for the Yumepa pass; overnight guests usually receive one at check-in.
- Reserve seats on limited expresses to Kinosaki in advance.
- At the dunes, take cash for the bus and check the time of the last bus back.
- Tottori Prefecture is part of the Chugoku region; see the [regions guide](/guide/regions).
