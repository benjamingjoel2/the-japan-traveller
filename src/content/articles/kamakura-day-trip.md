Kamakura is a city in Kanagawa Prefecture. The Kamakura City Tourist Association puts it about an hour from Tokyo and about 30 minutes from Yokohama. This article covers the Great Buddha, Tsurugaoka Hachimangu, the June hydrangea temples, the Enoden line and the city's visitor guidelines.

## Getting there from Tokyo

JR trains run directly from central Tokyo to Kamakura Station. The tourist association gives these times on the JR Yokosuka Line:

| From | Line | Time to Kamakura (Visit Kamakura) |
|---|---|---|
| Tokyo Station | JR Yokosuka Line | About 60 minutes |
| Shinagawa Station | JR Yokosuka Line | About 50 minutes |
| Yokohama Station | JR Yokosuka Line | About 25 minutes |

JNTO gives 55 minutes from Tokyo Station to Kamakura on the same line. From Shinjuku, JNTO says to take the **Shonan-Shinjuku Line** to Kamakura Station. Neither source gives a time for that route.

**Kita-Kamakura** is the stop before Kamakura on the Yokosuka Line. JNTO's itinerary gives 55 minutes from Tokyo Station to Kita-Kamakura, then 3 minutes on to Kamakura. JNTO puts Engakuji a 1-minute walk from Kita-Kamakura Station, and the tourist association puts Meigetsuin 10 minutes' walk from it.

These are all JR lines. For pass coverage, see [/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass). For paying with a Suica or PASMO card, see [/articles/ic-cards-suica-pasmo-icoca](/articles/ic-cards-suica-pasmo-icoca).

## The Great Buddha at Kotoku-in

The Great Buddha is a bronze statue of Amida Buddha in the grounds of Kotoku-in, a temple of the Jodo sect. JNTO gives its height as 11.3 metres and its weight as 121 tons, and dates it to the 13th century. It was first housed in a wooden hall. According to JNTO, the hall was repeatedly destroyed by typhoons, tsunami and earthquakes in the 14th and 15th centuries. In 1498 the statue was left exposed to the elements, and it remains in the open today.

Visitor information from Kotoku-in:

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Hours, April to September | 8:00 to 17:30 |
| Hours, October to March | 8:00 to 17:00 |
| Last entry | 15 minutes before closing |
| Admission | 300 yen; children aged 6 to 12, 150 yen |
| Inside the statue | 50 yen extra (free for children under six); 8:00 to 16:30, last entry 10 minutes before closing |
| Access | Seven-minute walk from Hase Station on the Enoden |

Kotoku-in's rules: no smoking anywhere in the grounds, no drones and no selfie sticks inside the statue. Pets must be in a cage or carrier; dogs on leads are not allowed, except service dogs. Commercial photography needs the temple's permission in advance.

## Tsurugaoka Hachimangu

Tsurugaoka Hachimangu is a 10-minute walk from the East Exit of JR Kamakura Station. The tourist association says Minamoto no Yoriyoshi first built it as a branch of Iwashimizu Hachimangu. A hundred years later, Minamoto no Yoritomo moved it to its present site. JNTO dates the move to 1180, from Yuigahama Beach, and notes that Yoritomo established the Kamakura Shogunate.

According to the tourist association:

- The main gate opens at 6:00 and closes at 20:00.
- The Treasure Hall charges 200 yen for adults and 100 yen for elementary school pupils.
- The shrine's flower report lists peonies and wisteria in spring, and red spider lilies and autumn leaves in autumn.
- Yabusame (horseback archery) is held at the shrine's annual festival in September.

## Hydrangea season

The tourist association's seasons page says the hydrangeas are in full bloom in June. It also notes that the rainy season spans June and July, so an umbrella is a must. For what that means for a trip, see [/questions/is-japan-good-in-the-rainy-season](/questions/is-japan-good-in-the-rainy-season).

- **Meigetsuin** is "also known as the Hydrangea Temple". It is a 10-minute walk from JR Kita-Kamakura Station. Admission is 500 yen (high school age and over) or 300 yen (elementary and junior high). The garden behind the main hall costs 500 yen extra. Normal hours are 9:00 to 16:00, but the temple notes these may change in June when the hydrangeas bloom.
- **Hasedera** lists hydrangeas among its summer flowers. It is a 5-minute walk from Hase Station on the Enoden. Admission is 400 yen for adults (junior high and over) and 200 yen for elementary pupils. Hours are 8:00 to 16:30 (gates close 17:00) from July to March, and 8:00 to 17:00 (gates close 17:30) from April to June.

The same seasons page gives late November to early December as the best time for autumn leaves.

## The Enoden

The Enoshima Electric Railway, known as the Enoden, links Fujisawa, Enoshima and Kamakura. It has 15 stations. Hase, for the Great Buddha and Hasedera, is the third stop from Kamakura (after Wadazuka and Yuigahama).

- **Noriorikun one-day pass:** 800 yen adult, 400 yen child. Unlimited rides on the whole Enoden line on the day of issue. Sold from ticket machines at all stations. It also gives discounts at places including Hasedera, Enoshima Sea Candle and Enoshima Aquarium.
- **IC cards:** Enoden asks passengers to touch their IC card on the gate reader each time they get on and off. The simple card readers are used at all stations except Fujisawa, Enoshima, Hase and Kamakura. An error occurs if your card balance is below the basic fare.
- **Near the tracks:** Do not enter the track area. On platforms, stay behind the yellow line and do not use flash or other lighting. Enoden asks photographers not to inconvenience other passengers or local residents.

## Visitor guidelines

Kamakura City Tourist Association publishes "Respect Kamakura Guidelines". The main points:

- Be quiet and calm at temples and shrines.
- Mind the people around you when eating and drinking in the street.
- Take your rubbish with you.
- Be careful eating outdoors in parks and on beaches: hawks will go for your food.
- Do not take photos in dangerous places, and do not obstruct vehicles.
- Watch for traffic and other pedestrians in crowded areas, and do not ride bicycles, scooters or motorcycles in narrow or crowded streets.
- Take care not to disturb local residents.

## Before you go

- The seasons page says many people visit for hatsumode, the first shrine or temple visit of the year, in January. In summer it suggests sightseeing in the morning, when it is cooler.
- Beaches and beach huts open from 1 July to 31 August, according to the seasons page.
- Check temple hours in June: Meigetsuin says its times may change when the hydrangeas bloom.
