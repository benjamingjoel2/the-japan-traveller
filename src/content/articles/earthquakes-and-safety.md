Earthquakes can happen anywhere in Japan, and JNTO notes they can strike with little or no warning. This article explains the warnings the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) issues, the official tools for visitors, and what official guidance says to do when the ground shakes.

## Earthquake Early Warnings

JMA's Earthquake Early Warning (in Japanese, Kinkyu Jishin Sokuho) gives advance notice of the estimated shaking and the expected arrival time of strong tremors. It is based on rapid analysis of data from seismographs near the epicentre. JMA launched the public service on 1 October 2007. Warnings reach the public through media such as TV and radio.

JMA states the limits plainly:

- The time between the warning and strong shaking is a matter of seconds, or between several seconds and a few tens of seconds.
- Near the epicentre, the warning may arrive too late, or strong tremors may arrive at the same time as the warning.
- False warnings can occur, for example from noise caused by accidents, lightning or device failure.
- Estimates of magnitude and shaking are less reliable for very large earthquakes and for earthquakes that happen close together.

## What to do if you feel shaking or receive a warning

JMA's leaflet on Earthquake Early Warnings says to remain calm and secure your personal safety, and to alert the people around you. Its advice by setting:

| Where you are | JMA advice |
|---|---|
| At home or in your room | Protect your head and shelter under a table. Do not rush outside. Do not worry about turning off the gas in the kitchen. |
| In public buildings | Follow the attendant's instructions. Do not rush to the exit. |
| Outdoors | Look out for collapsing concrete-block walls. Be careful of falling signs and broken glass. |
| On buses or trains | Hold on tight to a strap or a handrail. |
| In a lift | Stop the lift at the nearest floor and get off immediately. |
| Driving | Do not slow down suddenly. Turn on hazard lights, slow down smoothly, and pull over safely to the left and stop. |

JNTO's guidance for visitors adds:

- When an earthquake occurs, protect your head and ensure your safety. After the tremor stops, follow staff instructions.
- Fire is the most dangerous secondary disaster, so put out any naked flames as quickly as possible.
- Once safety is confirmed, watch for aftershocks and secondary disasters before returning to your accommodation.
- When you check in to a hotel, find out where the evacuation routes are.
- Keep your phone and devices fully charged.

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government advises people to act calmly on officially announced information. If you cannot get back to where you are staying, it advises against moving around unnecessarily: stay in a safe place, and if you are out with nowhere to go, wait at a temporary stay facility. It also notes that phones may be hard to use after a disaster.

## Tsunami warnings

JMA issues tsunami warnings and advisories for each coastal region, about three minutes after an earthquake and in some cases as early as two minutes.

| Level | Expected height | What JMA says to do |
|---|---|---|
| Major Tsunami Warning | Over 3 m ("Huge") | Evacuate from coastal or river areas immediately to high ground or a tsunami evacuation building |
| Tsunami Warning | Up to 3 m ("High") | Evacuate from coastal or river areas immediately to high ground or a tsunami evacuation building |
| Tsunami Advisory | Up to 1 m | Get out of the water and leave coastal areas immediately |

The words "Huge" and "High" are used in the first warning for very large earthquakes (magnitude 8 or more); JMA then updates the warning with figures. A Major Tsunami Warning is classed as an Emergency Warning, which JMA uses for risks seen only once every few decades.

Other points from JMA:

- If the source is near the coast, a tsunami may arrive before a warning. Evacuate when you feel strong shaking, or weak shaking that lasts a long time.
- Heights can exceed estimates because of local coastline shape, so keep moving to higher ground where you can.
- Waves keep arriving and later waves may be higher. Do not leave the evacuation location until warnings are cleared.

JNTO's advice to visitors is the same in short form: if you are by the coast when a large earthquake strikes, head for higher ground.

## The Safety tips app

Safety tips is a free app developed under the supervision of the Japan Tourism Agency, for iPhone and Android. JNTO lists it as a source of information during a major natural disaster. According to the developer's page it:

- sends Earthquake Early Warnings for earthquakes of seismic intensity 4 or higher, plus tsunami, volcanic and weather warnings, tropical cyclone information, heat illness warnings, civil protection information (including ballistic missile alerts) and evacuation information;
- lets you choose up to five locations in Japan;
- lists medical facilities that accept foreigners;
- includes a disaster evacuation flowchart and communication cards with questions to ask people around you;
- works in 15 languages: English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Korean, Spanish, Portuguese, Tagalog, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Nepali, Thai, Khmer, Burmese, Mongolian and Japanese.

Install it and set your locations before you travel.

## Japan Visitor Hotline and other information

The **Japan Visitor Hotline** is run by JNTO. It operates 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, in English, Chinese and Korean. It gives general tourist information and help with accidents, illness and other emergencies, and support during natural disasters. Find its details on the [JNTO hotline page](https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/hotline/).

JNTO also runs a Japan Safe Travel Information service, which posts updates on X as @JapanSafeTravel, and points visitors to the JMA website for earthquake information.

## Before you go

- Install the Safety tips app and add the places you will stay.
- Save the Japan Visitor Hotline page and the JMA English site on your phone.
- At each hotel or ryokan, check the evacuation routes. If you are staying near the coast, find out where the nearest high ground is.
- Keep a charged phone or a power bank with you.
- For seasonal hazards such as typhoons, see the [seasons guide](/guide/seasons).
