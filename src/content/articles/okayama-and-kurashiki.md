Okayama Station is on the Sanyo Shinkansen. The city's two main sights are Korakuen garden and Okayama Castle. Kurashiki, a short JR ride west, has a canal-side merchant quarter and the Ohara Museum of Art. This article covers all four, with hours and fees as the official sites stated them in November 2026.

## Okayama Korakuen

Okayama Korakuen describes itself as "One of the Three Great Gardens of Japan." According to the garden's history page, the daimyo Ikeda Tsunamasa ordered his retainer Tsuda Nagatada to begin a new garden in 1687. The groundwork took 13 years, until 1700, with a few more years for the buildings.

Other dates from the official history:

- **1884:** ownership passed to Okayama Prefecture and the garden opened to the general public.
- **1934 and 1945:** a typhoon and then a wartime air raid caused serious damage. The garden was restored using historical plans and photographs.
- **1952:** designated a Special Place of Scenic Beauty under the Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties.

The garden states that students up to age 18, including international students, are temporarily free of charge on showing proof of attendance. Combined tickets are sold with Okayama Castle (800 yen), with the Okayama Prefectural Museum (610 yen), and with the castle and the Hayashibara Museum of Art (1,280 yen). They are not available on the other facility's closing days or during special exhibitions.

### Getting there

From Okayama Station the garden gives three options:

- **On foot:** about 25 minutes, along Momotaro Avenue and across the Asahi River on Tsurumi Bridge.
- **Bus:** about 12 minutes on Okaden bus line 18 for Fujiwara Danchi, from platform 1 of the station bus terminal, to the Korakuen-mae stop.
- **Streetcar:** about 15 minutes on the Okaden streetcar for Higashiyama, from Okayama-eki-mae to Shiroshita.

## Okayama Castle

According to the castle's official site, Ukita Hideie placed the main enclosure and keep on the hill of Okayama, and the keep (tenshu) was completed in 1597. Its outer walls are covered in black clapboards, which gave the castle the nickname Ujo, "crow castle". Gilded roof tiles from Hideie's time have been excavated, which led to a second name, Kin-ujo, "golden crow castle". The keep was destroyed by fire during the Second World War and rebuilt in 1966. Because the current keep is a reconstruction, Okayama is not among the castles with original keeps listed in [which Japanese castles are original](/questions/which-japanese-castles-are-original).

The official user guide gives these details:

- **Hours:** 9:00 to 17:30, last admission 17:00. Hours may be extended for events.
- **Closed:** 29 to 31 December. The keep may also close in bad weather or for inspections.
- **Admission:** adults (high school students and older) 500 yen; 400 yen each in groups of 20 or more. Junior high school students and younger enter free.
- **Combined tickets:** with Korakuen 800 yen; with Korakuen and the Hayashibara Museum of Art 1,280 yen; with Korakuen and the Yumeji Art Museum 1,440 yen.
- **Access:** the Higashiyama streetcar from Okayama Station to Shiroshita (about 5 minutes), then about 10 minutes on foot. Alternatively, a bus via Higashiyama to Kencho-mae, then about 5 minutes on foot.

## Kurashiki Bikan Historical Quarter

JR West describes Kurashiki as a commercial city that flourished during the Edo (1603 to 1868) and Meiji (1868 to 1912) periods. Kurashiki City's tourism site adds that the town became tenryo, territory under the direct control of the shogunate, in 1642. After land reclamation, cotton and igusa rush were widely grown, and cotton merchants and wholesalers gathered along the Kurashiki River.

The banks of the Kurashiki River are the centre of the quarter, with white-walled buildings and willows. The city's tourism site lists these features:

- **Kurashiki River boats.** Small boats recreate the craft that carried goods in the Edo period. Tickets are sold on the day, and some can be booked online. The site recommends buying same-day tickets at the Kurashikikan tourist information office soon after you arrive.
- **Night lighting.** After sunset the quarter is lit, and the white walls are reflected in the river.
- **Japan Heritage.** The story of the area is certified as Japan Heritage.

JR West gives the walk from Kurashiki Station to the quarter as about 15 minutes.

## Ohara Museum of Art

The Ohara Museum of Art stands in the Bikan quarter. Its history page says it was founded in 1930 by Ohara Magosaburo, a Kurashiki businessman, in memory of the painter Kojima Torajiro, who had died the year before. The museum describes itself as Japan's first private museum centred on Western art. It names works by El Greco, Gauguin, Monet and Matisse among the core of the collection.

| Period | Hours | Last entry |
|---|---|---|
| March to November | 9:00 to 17:00 | 16:30 |
| December to February | 9:00 to 15:00 | 14:30 |

The Kojima Torajiro Memorial Hall keeps shorter hours. The museum is closed on Mondays, but opens when Monday is a national or substitute holiday, and it does not close in August. Temporary closures also occur, and the museum publishes an opening calendar.

One ticket covers the main building, the Kogeikan and Toyokan and the Kojima Torajiro Memorial Hall: 2,000 yen for adults and 500 yen for under-18s, with preschool children free. There are no group rates. Each building stamps the ticket, and a building not yet visited can be seen on another day. The annex is closed for a long period.

## Getting there by rail

Okayama Station is on the Sanyo Shinkansen. The Ohara Museum's access page gives the onward journey from Okayama to Kurashiki on the JR Sanyo Line as about 18 minutes. From the Hiroshima direction, it suggests the Shinkansen to Shin-Kurashiki, then about 10 minutes on the JR Sanyo Line to Kurashiki. The museum is about 15 minutes on foot from Kurashiki Station.

The Sunrise Izumo sleeper train from Tokyo stops at both Okayama and Kurashiki (see [the Sunrise Izumo and Sunrise Seto](/articles/sunrise-izumo-seto-night-train)). Okayama is also the starting point for the Limited Express Yakumo to Matsue and Izumo (see [Izumo Taisha and Matsue](/articles/izumo-and-matsue)) and for the ferries to Naoshima via Uno (see [Naoshima and the art islands](/articles/naoshima-art-islands)).

- Korakuen opens earlier in summer (7:30) than in winter (8:00). Buy the 800 yen combined ticket if you plan to visit the castle too.
- The Ohara Museum closes on Mondays except on holidays and in August.
