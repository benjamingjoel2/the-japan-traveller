Visitors can drive in Japan, but only with the right document, and only for a limited time after arrival. This article covers which licences are accepted, the main road rules, expressway tolls and visitor passes, and official advice for snow and ice.

## Which licences are valid

The National Police Agency (NPA) says you must hold one of the following to drive in Japan:

- A Japanese driving licence.
- An International Driving Permit (IDP) issued under the Geneva Convention. The NPA gives drivers from the US, Italy, the UK, Canada, South Korea, Thailand and the Philippines as examples.
- A driving licence issued in Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco or Taiwan, with a Japanese translation attached.

**IDPs.** JAF (the Japan Automobile Federation) says only IDPs "issued under the September 19, 1949 Geneva Convention on Road Traffic" are valid. Permits issued under other treaties, such as the 1968 Vienna Convention, are not valid in Japan. The NPA says the IDP must be the original paper document; a digital version shown on a phone cannot be used. JNTO adds that card-type and smartphone IDPs are not valid, and that you must obtain your IDP before you leave home. The NPA also says you can only drive the vehicle types stamped in the permit's vehicle category columns.

**Licence plus translation.** For the six places listed above, you carry your original licence and a Japanese translation. The NPA says the translation must be prepared by the consulate of the country that issued the licence, or by a body designated by Japan's National Public Safety Commission. JAF prepares these translations. It accepts applications online only, and says processing takes about 2 to 3 business days for these six countries and regions.

## The one-year limit

The NPA says you may drive for up to one year from your date of arrival in Japan, or until your licence expires, whichever is sooner. An IDP itself is valid for one year from its issue date. JAF says a foreign licence is no longer valid for driving in Japan one year after entry, even with a Japanese translation.

Police may ask for your passport to check how long you have been in Japan, according to JAF. JNTO notes that your arrival date is confirmed by the entry stamp in your passport. Automated gates do not give a stamp, so it advises renters to use a staffed gate or ask at the inspection desk.

## Road rules

- **Drive on the left.** JNTO says "cars drive on the left side of the road". JAF says drivers and cyclists must keep to the left.
- **Alcohol.** The NPA's message is "If you drink, do not drive." It warns that drink drivers face heavy penalties and can have their licence revoked. Providing a vehicle to someone at risk of drink driving, or asking to ride with a driver who has been drinking, also carries heavy penalties. The NPA notes that driving while hungover is also illegal.
- **Seatbelts.** JAF says the driver and all passengers must wear seatbelts.
- **Child seats.** JAF says a child younger than six must be placed securely in a child seat.
- **Phones.** JAF says you must not use a mobile phone, or be distracted by the navigation system, while driving.
- **Red lights.** The NPA says you must stop at a red light and cannot go straight or turn. A green arrow allows you to go in the direction shown.
- **Stop signs.** The NPA says that at a stop sign you must come to a full stop and check both sides.
- **Pedestrians.** The NPA says to always yield to pedestrians at intersections, and to stop fully when pedestrians are crossing or about to cross.
- **Railway crossings.** JNTO says to stop before the line and check both directions for trains.
- **Accidents.** The NPA says to report every accident to the police, even if no one is hurt, and to give first aid and call an ambulance if someone is injured.

Driving without a valid licence carries heavy penalties, says the NPA. The same applies to anyone who knowingly lends a vehicle to an unlicensed driver, and to passengers.

## Expressway tolls, ETC and visitor passes

JNTO says expressway tolls are paid either at manual gates for cash or credit card (green sign) or at ETC gates (purple sign). ETC is electronic toll collection. JNTO says most rental companies can provide an ETC card, and you settle toll charges when you return the car. It gives a Tokyo to Kyoto toll of approximately 10,070 yen at the time of its writing.

**Visitor passes.** JNTO says the Japan Expressway Pass has not been sold since December 2023. It lists these regional passes for foreign tourists as still available: Hokkaido Expressway Pass, Tohoku Expressway Pass, San'in-Setouchi-Shikoku Expressway Pass, Kyusyu Expressway Pass, and Central Nippon Expressway Pass. It notes that new bookings for the Central Nippon Expressway Pass closed on 1 October 2024.

**Hokkaido Expressway Pass.** NEXCO East's terms of use, in force from 1 October 2024, say:

- You apply at the counter of a designated rental car company and pay the pass fee there.
- It covers ordinary cars and light vehicles rented from a designated company, used with a designated ETC card.
- You choose a usage period of 4 to 8 days, and can travel the covered sections an unlimited number of times in that period.
- You must carry a passport (or permanent residence permit) and an international driving licence.
- It covers named sections of the Hokkaido, Sasson, Shiribeshi, Doto, Hidaka and Fukagawa-Rumoi expressways. Tolls outside these sections are charged separately.

The terms do not give a price, so this article does not.

## Winter driving

JNTO says to fit winter tyres or tyre chains when driving in snowy areas. JAF's advice for snow and ice includes:

- Driving on snowy roads with regular tyres is extremely dangerous. Use snow tyres or chains.
- Carry chains and a jack even with studless tyres, and do not drive fast with chains except on snow-covered roads.
- Avoid sudden braking, lane changes and sharp turns, and keep a long following distance.
- Watch for black ice, especially on bridges, overpasses and near tunnel entrances and exits.
- In a whiteout, turn on hazard lights and stop somewhere safe. If snow blocks the exhaust, clear it, as there is a risk of carbon monoxide poisoning.
- Stick to main roads, allow extra time and check forecasts and road conditions before you go.

For winter travel in the north, see also /articles/hokkaido-in-winter.

## Before you go

- Get the right document before you travel: a 1949 Geneva Convention IDP in paper form, or, for the six listed countries and regions, a Japanese translation of your licence.
- Carry your original licence, IDP or translation, and your passport, whenever you drive.
- Ask the rental company about an ETC card, and about any regional expressway pass when you book.
- JNTO says most major destinations on main roads are signposted in English, but this may not be true in rural areas.
