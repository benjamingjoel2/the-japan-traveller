Ise Jingu is a Shinto shrine complex in the city of Ise, Mie Prefecture. This article covers its two main shrines, Naiku and Geku, the order in which the shrine asks visitors to see them, the Shikinen Sengu rebuilding cycle, the Okage Yokocho area, and getting there by rail.

## Naiku and Geku

The shrine's official name is simply Jingu. It is centred on two precincts, Kotaijingu (Naiku, the Inner Shrine) and Toyo'uke-daijingu (Geku, the Outer Shrine). The shrine says Jingu includes 125 jinja in total and that about 1,500 rituals are held each year.

**Naiku** is dedicated to Amaterasu-Omikami, described by the shrine as the ancestral kami of the Imperial family. The shrine says she was enshrined here about 2,000 years ago. The innermost sanctuary is enclosed by four rows of wooden fences. Visitors usually pray in front of the third-row gate and may not enter the inner courtyard. The entrance is over the Ujibashi Bridge across the Isuzugawa River. The bridge is over 100 m long and is itself rebuilt every twenty years.

**Geku** is dedicated to Toyo'uke-no-Omikami, who the shrine says was enshrined about 1,500 years ago. She is said to provide sacred food to Amaterasu-Omikami. The main sanctuary is architecturally almost the same as Naiku's, but Geku has a hall called Mikeden that Naiku does not.

The two shrines are in different parts of the city. Ise Jingu gives their addresses as 1 Ujitachi-cho (Naiku) and 279 Toyokawa-cho (Geku).

## Order of visiting and etiquette

Ise Jingu's access page asks visitors: "Please visit Geku at first, and then visit Naiku."

The shrine's guidance on praying:

- At the water basin (temizu), rinse the left hand, then the right, then rinse the mouth from water poured into the cupped left hand. Do not let the dipper touch your lips.
- To pray, bow deeply twice, clap twice, then bow deeply once more.
- Photography is not allowed in the praying area.
- Eating, drinking and smoking are not allowed in the precincts except in designated areas.

Opening hours, as published by the shrine:

| Months | Hours |
|---|---|
| January to April, and September | 05:00 to 18:00 |
| May to August | 05:00 to 19:00 |
| October to December | 05:00 to 17:00 |

## The Shikinen Sengu

Ise Jingu says its main sanctuary is rebuilt every twenty years. A new sanctuary of the same dimensions is built on an alternate site next to the current one. The sacred apparel, furnishings and treasures are also remade. The cycle involves about 30 rituals and ceremonies, starting with the cutting of the first trees, and the shrine says it takes about eight years to carry them all out. At the end, the priests move the Holy Mirror, the symbol of Amaterasu-Omikami, to the new sanctuary. The same rites are carried out at Geku and other shrines of Jingu.

The shrine says the first Shikinen Sengu of Naiku was held in 690 and that the one held in 2013 was the 62nd.

The 63rd Shikinen Sengu is under way. The shrine's published schedule says it began in 2025. The central rite, Sengyo no gi, when the deity moves to the new sanctuary, is planned for October 2033. Milestones on the schedule include:

- Timber-hauling events (okibiki) from May to July 2026 and from May to July 2027.
- The ceremony for the first crossing of the new Ujibashi Bridge in November 2029.
- The transfer at Naiku and Geku in October 2033, with the shrine's other betsugu shrines following in 2034.

## Oharai-machi and Okage Yokocho

Oharai-machi is the old approach street in front of Naiku. The Ise City Tourism Association describes it as about 800 m of cobbled street running from Ujibashi Bridge towards Sarutahiko Shrine. Okage Yokocho sits in the middle of the street. Its buildings are relocated and recreated examples of buildings from the Iseji route from the Edo to Meiji periods.

The association lists Okage Yokocho as open from 9:30 to 17:00, varying by season, with earlier opening or later closing possible during long holidays such as Golden Week and Obon. Admission is free. The nearest bus stop is Jingu Kaikan Mae; Naiku Mae is nearest to Ujibashi Bridge.

## Getting there

Ise has three stations near the shrines, according to the Ise City Tourism Association:

- **Iseshi (JR and Kintetsu).** About a 5-minute walk to Geku.
- **Ujiyamada (Kintetsu).** About a 10-minute walk to Geku.
- **Isuzugawa (Kintetsu).** Closest to Naiku, but about 2.3 km from it, roughly 30 minutes on foot.

Between the shrines, the association says the bus from the "Outer Shrine No. 3" stop to the Inner Shrine usually takes 15 to 20 minutes and costs 520 yen one way. Cash, IC cards and bus tickets are accepted. Ise Jingu names this the Geku-Naiku Loop Line (Route 51 or 55).

**From Nagoya.** JNTO's national parks site says Kintetsu limited express trains and JR Rapid Mie trains both run from Nagoya to Iseshi in 80 to 90 minutes. As an example of a timetabled Kintetsu service, the Shimakaze sightseeing express leaves Kintetsu-Nagoya at 10:25 and reaches Iseshi at 11:40 (Kintetsu timetable as of 14 March 2026; not in service on Thursdays as a rule).

**From Osaka.** The Ise City Tourism Association says Kintetsu limited express trains from Osaka-Uehommachi, Tsuruhashi or Osaka-Namba take 1 hour 40 minutes to 2 hours without changing. The Shimakaze from Osaka-Namba leaves at 10:40 and reaches Iseshi at 12:16 (not in service on Tuesdays as a rule).

**From Kyoto.** JNTO's Naiku page gives about 2 hours 30 minutes from Kyoto to Isuzugawa by Kintetsu. The Shimakaze from Kyoto leaves at 10:00 and reaches Iseshi at 12:03 on weekdays (not in service on Wednesdays as a rule).

**Tickets.** Kintetsu says a limited express ticket must be bought in addition to the basic fare ticket. Kintetsu is a private railway, and the official Japan Rail Pass site states that the fare and fee for private railway sections must be paid. JR's Rapid Mie, listed by the JR Pass site for Nagoya to Toba, does not have a private railway section named on that page. See [Rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

## Planning notes

- Visit Geku first, then Naiku, as the shrine asks.
- Shrine gates close at 17:00 from October to December.
- Okage Yokocho shops generally close in the early evening, so plan to visit before 17:00.
- During the 63rd Shikinen Sengu, check the shrine's schedule for ceremonies and timber-hauling events.
