Beppu, Yufuin and Kurokawa are the three onsen towns most often grouped together in Kyushu. Beppu is a coastal city with a very large number of hot-spring sources, Yufuin is an inland town below Mt Yufu, and Kurokawa is a small riverside village of inns in the mountains of Kumamoto. This article covers what each official tourism body says the towns are known for and how to reach them. For bathing etiquette and tattoo policies, see [ryokan and onsen](/guide/ryokan-and-onsen).

## Beppu: hot-spring sources and the hells

JNTO says there are close to 3,000 hot-spring sources in Beppu City, representing more than 10% of all vents in Japan. The city's hot springs are grouped into eight onsen areas known together as Beppu Hatto. JNTO says all eight can be reached from Beppu Station by public transport, and the closest, Ekimae-koto Onsen, is a two-minute walk from the station. JNTO also lists the Beppu Beach Sand Bath, for bathing in naturally hot sand.

**The hells (jigoku).** Beppu's best-known sight is the Seven Hells of Beppu. The Beppu tourism pamphlet describes them as places people once feared to approach because of the steam, hot mud and boiling water that gush out at around 100°C. JNTO notes that the hells are for viewing, not bathing. The seven are:

- Umi Jigoku, which the pamphlet describes as cobalt-blue
- Chinoike Jigoku, known for its blood-red colour
- Tatsumaki Jigoku
- Shiraike Jigoku
- Oniishi Bozu Jigoku
- Oniyama Jigoku, where crocodiles are bred using hot-spring heat
- Kamado Jigoku

According to the Beppu Jigoku Association, Umi, Chinoike, Tatsumaki and Shiraike Jigoku are designated national places of scenic beauty because of their varied colours and forms. The hells are open 8:00 to 17:00 every day of the year. JNTO says five are in the Kannawa district and two in the Shibaseki district, and that a tour of all seven takes around 2 to 2.5 hours. From Beppu Station, JNTO gives a 20-minute ride on bus No. 2, 5, 24 or 41 to the Kannawa or Umi-Jigoku-mae stop.

Above the city, Mt Tsurumi (1,375 m) is described in the pamphlet as one of the sources of Beppu's hot springs. The Beppu Ropeway takes 10 minutes to reach the station just below the summit.

## Yufuin: a basin town below Mt Yufu

The Yufuin tourism association describes the town as hot-spring inns in a basin surrounded by mountains, including Mt Yufu. JNTO says the main route between Yufuin Station and Kinrin Lake is 1.5 km long and takes about 20 to 30 minutes to explore on foot. Kinrin Lake has two hot springs below its surface, and in the autumn months JNTO says these create a morning mist over the water.

Yufu City also includes other hot-spring areas. The tourism association describes Tsukahara Onsen as strongly acidic and Yunohira as a hot spring long known as a place for therapeutic stays.

## Kurokawa: a village of inns

JNTO describes Kurokawa Onsen as a picturesque riverside town in the mountains with around thirty onsen ryokan. It is in Minamioguni, in the Aso district of Kumamoto Prefecture.

JNTO's advice is to buy the Nyuto Tegata, an onsen-hopping pass, on arrival. It lets you visit up to three participating bathhouses and is sold at the information centre, which also has an English-language pamphlet. Check the current price at the information centre.

## Getting there by train and bus

**YUFUIN NO MORI (JR Kyushu).** JR Kyushu's sightseeing train runs from Hakata (Fukuoka) to Yufuin, and some services continue to Beppu. As of January 2026, JR Kyushu lists 2 round trips a day between Hakata and Yufuin and 1 round trip a day between Hakata and Beppu, by way of Oita. Stops include Tosu, Kurume, Hita, Amagase and Bungo-Mori. On the timetable published on JR Kyushu's page, Hakata to Yufuin takes between about 2 hours 10 minutes and 2 hours 20 minutes, and Hakata to Beppu about 3 hours 20 minutes. JR Kyushu notes that timetables may only be valid for a few months.

All seats are reserved. JR Kyushu says seats on YUFUIN NO MORI and the YUFU limited express are consistently fully booked, and it suggests travelling to Yufuin via Kokura and Oita instead. On days YUFUIN NO MORI does not run, the YUFU runs on the same timetable. There is a footbath on platform 1 at Yufuin Station. JR Kyushu's page does not say whether the Japan Rail Pass or JR Kyushu Rail Pass covers this train, so check before you travel; see the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

**To Beppu.** JNTO says Beppu Station is reached by limited express train from Hakata, Oita, Miyazaki and Hitoyoshi.

**Between Beppu and Yufuin.** In autumn 2026 the Yufuin tourism association lists an open-top double-decker bus, Yunokaze, running daily between Beppu Station and Yufuin from 7 October to 13 December 2026. It takes about 65 minutes one way. Departures from Beppu Station are at 9:00, 12:15 and 15:45, and from Yufuin at 10:40, 14:20 and 17:05. Advance reservation is required. The one-way fare is ¥2,200 for adults and ¥1,100 for children (¥2,000 and ¥1,000 when booked online by credit card).

**To Kurokawa.** JNTO says Kurokawa is not on any rail line. Direct highway buses run from Fukuoka Airport and Hakata Station. From the Kumamoto side, Aso Station is the nearest station, and you continue by taxi or bus.

## Before you go

- Seats on YUFUIN NO MORI sell out. Book early or plan the Kokura and Oita route as a fallback.
- JNTO puts a tour of all seven Beppu hells at around 2 to 2.5 hours. Two of them are in Shibaseki, away from the Kannawa group.
- Kurokawa has no railway. Check highway bus times from Fukuoka before you set out.
- Tattoo policies differ between baths. Check with each inn or bath; see [ryokan and onsen](/guide/ryokan-and-onsen). For where Kyushu fits in a wider trip, see [the regions guide](/guide/regions).
