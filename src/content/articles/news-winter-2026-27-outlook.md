The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) issued its cold-season outlook for December 2026 to February 2027 on 18 September 2026. When it published its next three-month outlook on 20 October 2026, it said it had reviewed the winter forecast against new data and was making no change. The outlook leans towards a warmer winter than normal across most of the country and less snow than usual on much of the Sea of Japan side.

## What the outlook says

JMA's seasonal forecasts give the chance that a season will be below normal, near normal or above normal. For December to February the figures are:

| Area | Temperature: below / near / above normal |
|---|---|
| Northern Japan | 20% / 40% / 40% |
| Eastern Japan | 10% / 30% / 60% |
| Western Japan | 10% / 30% / 60% |
| Okinawa and Amami | 10% / 20% / 70% |

| Area | Snowfall: below / near / above normal |
|---|---|
| Northern Japan, Sea of Japan side | 40% / 40% / 20% |
| Eastern Japan, Sea of Japan side | 50% / 30% / 20% |
| Western Japan, Sea of Japan side (excluding northern Kyushu) | 50% / 30% / 20% |

For precipitation, JMA gives a 50% chance of above-normal totals on the Pacific side of eastern Japan, across western Japan and in Okinawa and Amami.

The agency's description of the expected weather is:

- **Sea of Japan side, northern and eastern Japan:** cloudy days with snow, or rain in eastern Japan, as in a normal winter.
- **Sea of Japan side, western Japan:** more cloudy days with snow or rain than normal.
- **Pacific side, northern, eastern and western Japan:** fewer sunny days than normal.
- **Okinawa and Amami:** more cloudy and rainy days than normal.

## The El Nino background

On 9 October 2026 JMA reported that the El Nino event under way since spring 2026 had reached its greatest strength on record. In September the sea surface in its monitoring area was 4.0 degrees Celsius above the reference value. JMA said this beat the previous highest figure since 1949, which was +3.6 degrees in December 1997. It put the chance that a record-strength El Nino continues into winter at 100%.

JMA's El Nino report does not itself forecast Japan's weather; it refers readers to the seasonal forecasts. The Japan Weather Association, a private forecaster, cited the El Nino in its own outlook of 8 October 2026. It expects a warm winter across Japan, with near-normal or below-normal snowfall on the Sea of Japan side. It also warned that short spells of strong cold and heavy snow are still possible in a warm winter.

## What this means for travellers

- **Probabilities, not promises.** A 60% chance of a warm season still leaves a real chance of a normal or cold one. Cold snaps and heavy snow can occur in a mild winter.
- **Ski trips.** The snowfall outlook covers the Sea of Japan side. Our [seasons guide](/guide/seasons) notes that Hokkaido, the Sea of Japan side of Tohoku and the Northern Alps get heavy snow from December to March. The forecast favours near-normal or below-normal snow there, not a lack of snow. It does not say when individual resorts will open. Check each resort's own announcements.
- **Clear-sky winter on the Pacific side.** Winter on the Pacific side (Tokyo, Osaka) is usually dry and clear, as the seasons guide says. This year JMA expects fewer sunny days than normal there and more precipitation in eastern and western Japan. Allow some flexibility if a clear view, such as Mount Fuji, matters to your plans.
- **Snow events.** Dates for winter events are covered in [Hokkaido in winter](/articles/hokkaido-in-winter) and [Shirakawa-go in winter](/articles/shirakawa-go-in-winter). Month-by-month notes are under [Seasons](/seasons).

## What we don't know yet

JMA updates its outlook every month. The next three-month outlook, covering December to February, is due at 14:00 Japan time on 24 November 2026. The next El Nino report is due on 10 November 2026. Shorter-range one-month forecasts are issued every Thursday. The seasonal outlook cannot predict individual storms, cold waves or snowfall at a particular place on a particular day.
