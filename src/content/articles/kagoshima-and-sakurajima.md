Kagoshima sits at the southern end of the Kyushu Shinkansen, across the bay from the active volcano Sakurajima. This article covers the city ferry to Sakurajima, what the Japan Meteorological Agency and Kagoshima City say about the volcano and its ash, Sengan-en garden, and getting to Kagoshima by train.

## Getting to Kagoshima

JNTO says the Kyushu Shinkansen connects Kagoshima with Kumamoto in 42 minutes, Fukuoka in 1 hour 16 minutes and Shin-Osaka in 3 hours 41 minutes. For the Hakata to Kagoshima-Chuo service and pass coverage, see the [rail-route table](/rail-routes) and the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

From Kagoshima Airport, the Sakurajima Visitor Center lists the airport bus to Kagoshima-Chuo Station at 38 minutes direct, with slower runs via Ishiki (40 minutes) and Yoshino (55 minutes).

## The Sakurajima ferry

The ferry is run by Kagoshima City. It links Kagoshima Port and Sakurajima Port in about 15 minutes, or around 20 minutes depending on weather and sea conditions. It runs every day of the year.

**It no longer runs 24 hours.** The city says it revised its 24-hour operation from 1 October 2025. Sailings now run from 4:00 to 23:30. Older guides that describe a round-the-clock service are out of date.

Passenger fares were revised on 1 July 2024. The adult fare rose from ¥200 to ¥250, and the child fare from ¥100 to ¥130. Fares for passengers and vehicles are paid at Sakurajima Port, not at Kagoshima.

From Kagoshima-Chuo Station, the Sakurajima Visitor Center gives about 14 minutes by bus to the Kagoshima Suizokukan-mae stop, then a 1-minute walk to the ferry terminal. On the island, the Sakurajima Island View Bus takes about 6 minutes from the Sakurajima Port stop to the Visitor Center stop.

## Sakurajima

The Kagoshima prefectural tourism site describes Sakurajima as one of Japan's most active volcanoes, releasing smoke or ash almost daily. It gives the height as 1,117 m and the circumference as about 50 km, and puts the island about 4 km from the city by ferry. Places it lists include:

- **Sakurajima Visitor Center**, with exhibits on the volcano's history and geology and on life beside it.
- **Yunohira Observatory**, at 373 m the highest point open to visitors.
- **Arimura Lava Observatory**, on the lava field from the 1946 eruption.
- **Kurokami observation point**, with views of the volcano and the buried torii gate of Kurokami Shrine.

## Volcanic activity and ash

The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) has Sakurajima at **Volcanic Alert Level 3, with entry to the mountain restricted**. JMA lowered it from Level 5 to Level 3 in a warning issued on 27 July 2022. Its explanatory statement of 6 November 2026 says Level 3 continues. JMA's advice:

- Within about 2 km of the Minamidake summit crater and the Showa crater, watch for large volcanic bombs and pyroclastic flows.
- Downwind, ash and small volcanic rocks can be carried a long way.
- Strong air shocks from explosions can break windows.
- If ashfall continues, debris flows may occur during rain.

Kagoshima City says that by law the area within a 2 km radius of the crater is off-limits, and that residents on the city side often have ashfall. The city's volcano pages link to JMA's ashfall forecast for Sakurajima.

The Kagoshima Convention and Visitors Bureau's safety page for visitors says:

- Ash usually lands within 2 km of the crater, where entry is prohibited.
- If ash falls, take cover under a roof. It usually stops within about 10 minutes.
- A mask helps you avoid breathing it in. Brush ash off clothing by hand.

Check the alert level and JMA's ashfall forecast on the day you plan to cross. For general disaster preparation, see [earthquakes and safety](/articles/earthquakes-and-safety).

## Sengan-en

JNTO says Sengan-en was built in 1658 as one of the residences of the Shimazu clan, which ruled the area. Its main feature is borrowed scenery: the garden takes in the view of Sakurajima and Kagoshima Bay behind it. The house blends Japanese, Chinese and Western styles. JNTO says Sengan-en and the Shoko Shuseikan Museum were recognised in 2015 as part of the UNESCO World Heritage site related to Japan's Meiji Industrial Revolution. A hiking trail to the top of the grounds takes about 30 minutes one way.

The garden's own site describes it as a nationally designated place of scenic beauty and the villa of the Shimazu family of the Satsuma domain. It is open 9:00 to 17:00 every day of the year. The site does not list a standard admission fee on its home page, so check before you go.

JNTO gives about 30 minutes from Kagoshima-Chuo Station by the Kagoshima City View Bus or the Machi Meguri Bus.

## Before you go

- The Sakurajima ferry runs 4:00 to 23:30, not 24 hours. Pay on arrival at Sakurajima Port.
- Check JMA's alert level and ashfall forecast before you cross, and keep out of the 2 km restricted zone.
- Carry a mask in case of ashfall.
- For onward travel to Yakushima, see [Yakushima: cedar forests and the Jomon Sugi trail](/articles/yakushima). For other Kyushu bases, see [the regions guide](/guide/regions).
