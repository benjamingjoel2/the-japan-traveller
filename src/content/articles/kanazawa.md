Kanazawa is the capital of Ishikawa Prefecture on the Japan Sea coast. This article covers its main sights (Kenroku-en, Kanazawa Castle Park, the Higashi Chaya district, Omicho Market and the 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art), the crafts the city is known for, and how to get there by rail. Opening hours and fees are given only as the operators publish them; check before you go.

## Kenroku-en and Kanazawa Castle Park

Ishikawa Prefecture, which manages the garden, describes Kenroku-en as a representative Edo-period daimyo garden shaped over a long period by successive lords of the Kaga domain. It is designated a Special Place of Scenic Beauty and is counted as one of Japan's three great gardens, with Kairakuen in Mito and Koraku-en in Okayama.

Hours and fees as published by the prefecture's management office:

| | Details |
|---|---|
| 1 March to 15 October | 7:00 to 18:00 (last entry 17:30) |
| 16 October to end of February | 8:00 to 17:00 (last entry 16:30) |
| Adults (18 and over) | 320 yen; 250 yen each in groups of 30 or more |
| Children (6 to under 18) | 100 yen; 80 yen each in groups |
| Free entry | Visitors aged 65 and over with official proof of age, among others |
| Free opening days | About 7 days in the cherry blossom season (late March to early April), 3 days at the Hyakumangoku Festival (early June), 14 to 16 August, 3 November, and 31 December to 3 January |

The garden also has free early-morning entry before paid hours, through the Hasuike Gate or Zuijinzaka entrance only. The start time varies by season (for example, from 4:00 between 1 April and 31 August, and from 6:00 between 1 November and the end of February), and visitors must leave 15 minutes before paid hours begin. Evening openings with illumination are held in the cherry blossom and autumn leaf seasons.

Kanazawa Castle Park is next to the garden. The prefecture describes the castle as the residence of the Maeda family of the Kaga domain, which ruled the three provinces of Kaga, Noto and Etchu. Its Ishikawa Gate is a National Important Cultural Property, and the Hishi yagura turret, the Gojikken nagaya and the Hashizume Gate have been reconstructed. Entry to the park is free. It is open from 7:00 to 18:00 between 1 March and 15 October, and from 8:00 to 17:00 from 16 October to the end of February, with earlier gate opening for morning walks.

The reconstructed buildings (Hishi yagura, Gojikken nagaya, Hashizume-mon tsuzuki yagura and Hashizume-mon) open from 9:00 to 16:30 (last entry 16:00) every day. Admission is 320 yen for adults and 100 yen for children aged 6 to under 18. A combined ticket for Kenroku-en and one castle facility costs 500 yen.

## Higashi Chaya district

The official Kanazawa tourism site says the city's geisha houses were moved in 1820 from central Kanazawa to three districts, and that Higashi Chaya is the largest. The houses are recognisable by the fine lattice on the ground floor, with guest rooms on the upper floor. Many old buildings now house restaurants, cafes and souvenir shops, and one facility shows the interior of a geisha house built almost 200 years ago. The tourism site suggests allowing about 60 minutes.

The district is about a 5-minute walk from the Hashiba-cho bus stop, served by the Kanazawa Loop Bus, Hokutetsu local buses and West JR Bus.

## Omicho Market

Omicho Market was established in the Edo period and, according to the official tourism site, has been part of Kanazawa's food culture for more than 300 years. It has more than 170 stores, including many fishmongers selling seafood from the Sea of Japan. Hours are given as 9:00 to 16:00, varying from store to store, and the market closes for the New Year holidays. The Musashigatsuji Omicho Market bus stop is a minute's walk away.

## 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art

The museum says it was established "with the mission of generating new culture and revitalizing its community". The official Kanazawa tourism site says it opened in 2004, describes its disc-shaped building, and highlights Leandro Erlich's *The Swimming Pool*.

Hours, as stated by the museum:

- Exhibition Zone: 10:00 to 18:00, until 20:00 on Fridays and Saturdays. Closed on Mondays (or the following weekday when Monday is a national holiday) and over the New Year holidays.
- Public Zone: 9:00 to 22:00, closed over the New Year holidays.

The museum will close for renovation from 6 May 2027 to March 2028 (planned). If your trip falls in that period, check the museum's site.

## Gold leaf, Kutani ware and Kaga yuzen

The official Kanazawa tourism site describes these crafts as follows:

- **Gold leaf (Kanazawa haku):** "Kanazawa produces 99% of domestic gold leaf and 100% of domestic silver leaf and platinum leaf." Gold leaf workshops are listed on the tourism site.
- **Kutani ware:** porcelain whose signature technique is thickly applied paint in five colours: red, yellow, green, purple and dark blue.
- **Kaga yuzen:** silk dyeing with realistic natural designs in five vivid tones called "Kaga gosai", using a gradation technique called "bokashi".

The same page lists other local crafts, including Ohi ware, Kanazawa lacquerware and Kaga embroidery.

## Getting there

**From Tokyo.** The Hokuriku Shinkansen runs directly from Tokyo Station to Kanazawa. The official tourism site gives the journey as 2 hours 30 minutes.

**From Kyoto and Osaka.** The Hokuriku Shinkansen opened between Kanazawa and Tsuruga on 16 March 2024. JR West states that since then the Limited Express Thunderbird runs between Osaka/Kyoto and Tsuruga, and passengers change at Tsuruga to the Hokuriku Shinkansen for Fukui, Kanazawa and Toyama. The official tourism site gives Osaka to Tsuruga as 1 hour 20 minutes on the Thunderbird, then 40 minutes on the Shinkansen to Kanazawa. See the [rail-route table](/rail-routes) and [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass) for coverage.

**From Takayama and Shirakawa-go.** Highway buses link Kanazawa with Shirakawa-go and Takayama; see [Shirakawa-go in winter](/articles/shirakawa-go-in-winter) and [Takayama](/articles/takayama).

## Before you go: the Noto Peninsula

The Noto region north of Kanazawa was heavily affected by the Noto Peninsula Earthquake in January 2024. The official Kanazawa tourism site, in an article based on information as of August 2026, says that central Noto sustained minimal damage, that the Nanao area is easily reached from Kanazawa by car or train, and that Oku-Noto (Wajima, Suzu, Noto Town and Anamizu) is slowly opening to visitors again, with some sections of road still closed or requiring detours. Its FAQ says rail services including the JR Nanao Line and the Noto Railway have resumed. Check current operating information before travelling into Noto.
