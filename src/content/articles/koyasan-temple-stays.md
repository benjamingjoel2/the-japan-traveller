Koyasan is a mountain-top temple town in Wakayama Prefecture and the centre of Shingon Buddhism. This article covers its two main sites, Kongobu-ji and Okunoin, its World Heritage status, what an overnight stay in a temple (shukubo) involves, and how to get there from Osaka.

## Koyasan and Kongobu-ji

Koyasan was opened as a monastery by the monk Kobo Daishi at the beginning of the Heian period, according to Kongobu-ji. Kobo Daishi is the posthumous name of Kukai, given by Emperor Daigo in 921. JNTO gives Kukai's dates as 774 to 835 and says he founded Kongobu-ji in 816.

Kongobu-ji is the head temple of the Koyasan Shingon Sect. Its own site describes the whole mountain as the temple's grounds, made up of 117 temples. JNTO describes Koyasan as having more than 100 temples and over 1,200 years of history. At Kongobu-ji itself, JNTO notes that visitors remove their shoes to walk through the halls.

Other sites on the mountain, as described by Kongobu-ji:

- **Danjo Garan.** The first site built when Kobo Daishi opened the monastery. Most of the complex burned in a fire in 1843. The present Kondo hall was completed in 1932 and stands 23.73 m tall.
- **Daimon.** The great gate. It was rebuilt in its current location in 1705 and is 25.1 m tall.
- **Reihokan Museum.** Built in 1921 to store Kongobu-ji's sacred relics.

Kongobu-ji also notes that the ban on women entering the precinct was lifted in 1872.

## Okunoin

Okunoin is the mausoleum of Kobo Daishi. Kongobu-ji says it is where he is believed to remain in eternal meditation, praying for people's salvation, and describes it as one of the central pillars of faith on Koyasan. JNTO says the cobbled path to Okunoin passes over 200,000 gravestones and memorials under old cedar trees.

The Torodo, or lantern hall, stands in front of the mausoleum. Kongobu-ji says it was enlarged to roughly its present size in 1023 and that memorial sutra-chanting services are held there six times a day, between 9:00 and 14:40. Kongobu-ji's English site also mentions the Okunoin Mandoe lantern service, held each year on 1 to 3 October in the Torodo.

JNTO lists an Okunoin night tour that leaves each evening from one of the shukubo temples, except on the 20th of the month.

## The UNESCO listing

Koyasan is one of three sacred sites in the World Heritage property that JNTO names "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range". The other two are Yoshino and Omine, and Kumano Sanzan. JNTO says the property spans Nara, Wakayama and Mie prefectures. The Kumano sites and the Kumano Kodo pilgrimage routes are covered in [Walking the Kumano Kodo Nakahechi route](/articles/kumano-kodo-nakahechi).

## Staying in a shukubo

A shukubo is temple lodging. JNTO says shukubo were originally monastic lodgings for travellers and now serve modern pilgrims. Kongobu-ji's site says 52 of Koyasan's temples offer lodging; JNTO's shukubo page says 51, and its Koyasan page says over 50. The Koyasan Shukubo Temple Lodging Association's own English list, checked on 7 November 2026, showed 31 temples. Figures differ between sources, so treat any count as approximate.

What a stay involves, as described by official sources:

- **Shojin ryori.** JNTO describes this as vegetarian Buddhist cuisine. It is served to overnight guests and excludes meat, fish, green onion, garlic and other stimulants. JNTO says vegan-friendly versions are available and that the menu changes with the seasons.
- **Morning service (otsutome).** The shukubo association says the chief priest and monks chant sutras in the main hall early each morning. Depending on the temple, it may start at around 6:00. Overnight guests may take part or watch.
- **Other practices.** JNTO says guests are encouraged, but not required, to join practices such as meditation and sutra copying. The association notes that only a few shukubo offer ajikan meditation to private visitors, so check first. For sutra copying (shakyo), it directs visitors to the Daishi Kyokai Center.

Bookings can be made through the association's reservation site. The association notes that credit cards are not accepted at most shops and lodgings on Koyasan, so carry cash. For booking timing in general, see [How far ahead should I book a ryokan?](/questions/how-far-ahead-should-i-book-a-ryokan) and [Ryokan and onsen](/guide/ryokan-and-onsen).

## Getting there from Osaka

Koyasan is reached on the Nankai Koya Line to Gokurakubashi Station, then the Koyasan Cable Car up the mountain. Kongobu-ji's access page describes the change at Gokurakubashi. From Shin-Osaka, take the Midosuji subway line to Namba and change to the Nankai Koya Line. From Kansai International Airport, take the Nankai Airport Line and change to the Koya Line at Tengachaya.

- **Journey time.** Nankai gives about 90 minutes from Namba to Koyasan on the Limited Express Koya, including the transfer to the cable car. JNTO says the cable car ride takes five minutes.
- **Limited Express Koya.** Nankai describes it as a sightseeing limited express between Namba and Gokurakubashi, with all seats reserved. Tickets go on sale one month in advance. Nankai adds a 300 yen charge for tickets bought on board.
- **Koyasan World Heritage Digital Ticket.** Nankai sells this as a round-trip discount train ticket between the issuing station and Koyasan Station, valid for 2 days from the day selected. It includes a 2-day bus pass for Nankai Rinkan Bus routes on Koyasan (excluding the Tateri, Koya Ryujin and Koya Niutsuhime lines) and discount coupons for the Kondo, the Konpon Daito pagoda and the Reihokan Museum. Getting off along the way invalidates the rest of the trip. A Limited Express Koya option is valid for the outward journey only. It is bought online through Nankai's digital ticket site.
- **Japan Rail Pass.** Nankai is a private railway, not part of JR. The official Japan Rail Pass site states that when using a private railway, the fare and fee for the section used must be paid. See [Rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

## Planning notes

- The Koyasan Shukubo Association describes a Common Ticket for Visiting Temples covering six temples, including Kongobu-ji, available at its information centres.
- Bring cash for lodging and shops.
- Check the morning service time with your temple, as it varies.
- Fares for the World Heritage ticket are published by Nankai in a separate price list for 2026; check it before buying.
