Hokkaido has no formal rainy season, and its summer is short. This article covers lavender in Furano, the Blue Pond and farm hills of Biei, summer in Daisetsuzan, and the Shiretoko Five Lakes, using the local tourism bodies, JR Hokkaido and the park authorities. For winter, see [Hokkaido in winter](/articles/hokkaido-in-winter).

## Lavender in Furano

The Furano Tourism Association says lavender flowering depends on the variety:

- Early varieties begin to flower in late June.
- Okamurasaki, the variety with the largest planted area in Furano, peaks in mid-July.
- Later varieties can be seen into early August.
- Peak season usually runs from mid-July to early August, depending on the weather.
- Some farms begin cutting back the flowers in August to protect the roots.

The Hokkaido Tourism Organization gives the best season for lavender in Hokkaido as mid to late July. It describes Farm Tomita in Nakafurano as Hokkaido's best-known lavender farm. The tourism association adds that Farm Tomita grows lavender in a greenhouse, so it can be seen there before the outdoor fields flower.

**By train.** JR Hokkaido runs the Furano Lavender Express, a limited express from Sapporo to Furano in about two hours without changing trains. In 2026 it ran from June to September. All seats are reserved. You need a reserved-seat limited express ticket as well as a basic fare ticket. JR Hokkaido notes that IC cards cannot be used between Takikawa and Furano, and that fares adjusted on board are cash only.

**The Norokko train has ended.** JR Hokkaido said the Furano Biei Norokko Train would end in 2026. The Hokkaido Tourism Organization listed its final season as 77 days between 6 June and 23 September 2026.

## Biei: the Blue Pond and the hills

The Biei Tourism Association describes the town's hills as covered in rural scenery, with colours at their best from summer to autumn. In summer, Kashiwa Park has views of potato flowers and wheat fields.

**Shirogane Blue Pond.** The association says the water collected behind a dam built during disaster prevention work on Tokachi-dake, and took on an unusually blue colour. It attributes the colour to rivers of different mineral content mixing. After the spring snowmelt or heavy rain, the water can look cloudy or not blue at all. According to the association:

- The pond is about 20 minutes by car or bus from Biei or Asahikawa station.
- Dohoku Bus route buses run from JR Asahikawa and JR Biei stations.
- Traffic peaks from about 1pm to 3pm on sunny days, so it advises visiting in the morning.

**Requests to visitors.** The fields in Biei are private farmland. The association asks visitors:

- Never enter farmland. Shoes can carry soil and disease that harm crops.
- Stay on paved roads. Do not enter gravel or farm roads, and take photos from the paved road.
- Do not obstruct traffic.
- Do not fly drones. Drone flights and aerial photography are prohibited in principle in Biei Town without prior permission.
- Do not throw rocks or rubbish into the Blue Pond, and do not dump waste.

## Daisetsuzan National Park

JNTO's national parks site gives Daisetsuzan an area of 226,764 hectares. The park was established in 1934, and its mountains average about 2,000 metres, earning it the name "the roof of Hokkaido". JNTO gives the journey from Asahikawa as about 1.5 to 2 hours.

**Summer is short.** JNTO describes it as roughly July to August. Alpine plants then cover the treeless plateaus, and the area around Sugatamiike Pond has rare alpine plant communities. Snow and ice dominate most of the year.

**Two ropeways** lead into the high ground:

- **Asahidake Ropeway.** The Hokkaido Tourism Organization says the gondola climbs about 500 metres in 10 minutes, to Sugatami Station at around 1,600 metres. A 1.7 km trail loops past Sugatami Pond in about 90 minutes. Hours are 6.30am to 5.30pm, varying by season. JNTO gives about 2 hours 30 minutes from the top station to the summit of Asahidake.
- **Kurodake Ropeway** from Sounkyo Onsen. JNTO gives 60 to 90 minutes from the top station to the summit.

**Autumn comes early.** JNTO says the colours peak in mid-September and first snow usually arrives in October. The Hokkaido Tourism Organization says autumn colours begin at Asahidake from late August.

**Bears.** JNTO recommends visiting the Brown Bear Information Center at Daisetsu Kogen Onsen before hiking.

## Shiretoko Five Lakes

The Shiretoko Goko (Five Lakes) are on the Shiretoko Peninsula, about 14 km (about 20 minutes by car) from Utoro. There are two ways to see them, both run from the Shiretoko Goko Lakes Field House.

**The elevated wooden path** is 800 m long (about 1.6 km and 40 minutes there and back). It leads towards the first lake. It is free, level and suitable for wheelchairs, which can be borrowed free at the field house. An electric fence makes it a safe viewing route in bear country.

**The ground pathways** have a long loop (about 3 hours) and a short loop (1 hour 45 minutes). JNTO gives their lengths as 3 km and 1.6 km. Access depends on the season:

| Period | Access | Adult fee |
|---|---|---|
| Opening to 9 May | After a lecture at the field house | 450 yen |
| 10 May to 31 July (bears active) | Registered-guide tours only | Short loop 4,000 yen; long loop from 6,000 yen |
| 1 August to closing | After a lecture at the field house | 450 yen |

Guided tours must be booked on the official site. Same-day places, when available, cost 4,000 yen (short) or 7,000 yen (long). Tours run at 10-minute intervals for up to 10 people. Lectures take up to 50 people. The ground pathways close if bears appear, and they have no toilets or shops.

**Rules.** The field house asks visitors not to feed animals, litter, cook or eat, smoke, leave the paths, take plants or animals, or fly drones. The Ministry of the Environment says that since 1 April 2022, approaching, following or feeding brown bears has been illegal in the special zones of national and quasi-national parks. A person who continues after being ordered to stop by ministry staff can be fined up to 300,000 yen.

**Opening and access.** The field house's access page lists opening dates from 20 April to 8 November, with hours changing through the season. JNTO says it opens from the end of April to the end of November. The car park has paid spaces for 100 cars. In season, Shari Bus runs to Utoro from Memanbetsu Airport and Shiretoko-Shari Station.

## Before you go

- Lavender peaks from mid-July. Some fields are cut back in August.
- The Furano Biei Norokko Train ended in 2026. Check JR Hokkaido for 2027 services such as the Furano Lavender Express, which ran from June to September in 2026.
- Book Shiretoko Five Lakes guided tours ahead for 10 May to 31 July.
- See [Rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass), [regional rail passes](/articles/regional-rail-passes), the [Hokkaido section of the regions guide](/guide/regions), and the [July](/seasons/july) and [August](/seasons/august) pages.
