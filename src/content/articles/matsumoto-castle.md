Matsumoto Castle, in Matsumoto in Nagano Prefecture, is one of the twelve castles whose original keeps survive and one of the five whose keeps are National Treasures. This article covers what is original, how tickets and entry work, the Former Kaichi School nearby, and the limited express trains from Shinjuku and Nagoya. Details come from the castle's official site, run by Matsumoto City, and the city's tourism site, Visit Matsumoto.

## The keep and its five structures

The official site describes the castle as five connected structures:

- the Great Keep (dai-tenshu)
- the small Inui (north-west) Keep
- the Watari Tower, which links the Great Keep and the Inui Keep
- the Tatsumi Tsukeyagura (Tatsumitsuke Tower)
- the Tsukimi Yagura (Tsukimi Tower), joined to the Tatsumi Tsukeyagura

The site's heading calls it the oldest surviving castle of this five-structure, six-storey type. The castle's 1936 designation document described it as a rare "connected towers" castle older than Nagoya Castle.

## What is original, and when it was built

According to the official site, the Great Keep, the Inui Keep and the Watari Tower date from the end of the Warring States period. Matsumoto City's view is that Ishikawa Kazumasa and Ishikawa Yasunaga built them in 1593 and 1594. The Tatsumi Tsukeyagura and the Tsukimi Yagura are thought to have been added about 40 years later, early in the Edo period. The official site adds that the stone walls under the three older structures have been repaired but never rebuilt since they were first laid.

The building date is not settled. The castle's "construction age" page sets out several dates. A 1958 catalog gives 1594 to 1596. A 2012 Agency for Cultural Affairs catalog dates the Inui Keep to 1592, and the Great Keep and Watari Tower to 1615. Other theories put completion at various dates between 1596 and 1601. The city describes its own dating as a strong presumption, not proof, and plans tree-ring dating.

### National Treasure status

The official site says the castle structures were designated National Treasures on 20 April 1936, under the National Treasures Preservation Law of 1929. They were designated again after the Second World War under the Cultural Properties Protection Law. A large dismantling repair ran from 1950 to 1955. JNTO also notes that the Agency for Cultural Affairs recognises the castle as a National Treasure.

## Tickets, hours and entry rules

The official site gives these hours:

- Regular hours: 8:30 to 17:00, last entry 16:30
- Golden Week: 8:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:30
- Extended hours also apply in mid-August
- Open every day except 29 to 31 December

Admission for individuals, as listed on the official site (no effective date is given there):

| Ticket | Adult | Child (6 to 15) |
|---|---|---|
| E-ticket (timed entry) | 1,200 yen | 400 yen |
| Paper ticket (on site) | 1,300 yen | 400 yen |
| Group of 20 or more, e-ticket | 1,080 yen | 360 yen |

Children aged 5 and under enter free. Combination tickets are sold online only. The castle and Former Kaichi School ticket costs 1,600 yen for adults and 600 yen for children and is valid for two days. A four-site ticket costs 2,600 yen for adults and is valid for three days.

Points the official site makes about entry:

- Castle tickets are for a set time, are valid for two days and cannot be cancelled after purchase.
- You must take your shoes off inside. Bags are provided at the entrance so you can carry them.
- The keep has six storeys, steep narrow stairs and no lift. It has not been adapted for accessibility.
- It is about 140 steps to the sixth floor, and the steepest stairs have a 61-degree incline.
- A visit inside usually takes 45 to 60 minutes.
- During Golden Week (29 April to 5 May) and Obon (mid-August), the number of people inside at one time may be limited. Queues form along a route through the Honmaru Garden.
- Visitors with a disability certificate, plus one carer, enter free. So do visitors in yukata or other traditional dress during the mid-August extended hours.

## The city

The castle's access page puts it about 15 minutes' walk from Matsumoto Station, on the JR Shinonoi Line. The bus from the station's Oshiroguchi stop to Matsumotojo-Shiyakushomae takes about 10 minutes.

Visit Matsumoto treats the castle and the Former Kaichi School as the city's two National Treasures. The school is a building in pseudo-Western (giyofu) style designed by a local master carpenter in 1876. Its listing gives these details:

- Hours: 9:00 to 17:00, last admission 16:30
- Closed: the third Tuesday of each month from March to November, every Tuesday from December to February, and 29 December to 3 January
- Admission: 600 yen general (digital), 700 yen general (printed), 300 yen for elementary and junior high school students

Visit Matsumoto's city walking route is about 9.2 km with 56 m of climbing. It links the two National Treasures with the city's natural springs and a large outdoor sculpture by the Matsumoto-born artist Yayoi Kusama.

## Getting there by train

Visit Matsumoto gives these limited express services:

| Train | From | Operator | Time | Seats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Azusa | JR Shinjuku, direct | JR East | about 2 hours 30 minutes | All reserved |
| Shinano | JR Nagoya, direct | JR Central | about 2 hours | Unreserved seats available, but reserving is recommended |

The city's page states that Japan Rail Pass holders can reserve Azusa seats at no extra cost and can use the Shinano. For whether a pass is worth buying, see [Is the JR Pass still worth it?](/questions/is-the-jr-pass-still-worth-it) and [rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

## Planning notes

- Buy a timed e-ticket online; it is 100 yen cheaper than the paper ticket.
- Expect a cap on numbers inside during Golden Week and mid-August.
- Wear shoes that are easy to remove and expect very steep stairs.
- Matsumoto is also the base for trips to Kamikochi; see the [regions guide](/guide/regions).
- For other castles with surviving keeps, see [Which Japanese castles are original?](/questions/which-japanese-castles-are-original) and the [Himeji Castle](/articles/himeji-castle) article.
