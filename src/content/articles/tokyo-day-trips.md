Several places with their own character lie within about two hours of central Tokyo by train or bus. This page is a quick comparison of the day trips the site covers, with routes and times as the operators and tourism bodies state them. It ends with short notes on Kawagoe and Yokohama, which are closer still.

## At a glance

Times are one way and approximate. They are the operator's or tourism body's own figures and do not include getting to the departure station.

## Notes on each route

**Nikko.** Tobu also runs through limited express SPACIA trains from JR Shinjuku (about 2 hours, direct) and JR Ikebukuro (about 1 hour 55 minutes, direct). There is also a JR route via Utsunomiya; the [Nikko article](/articles/nikko-day-trip) compares the two and the Tobu NIKKO PASS.

**Kamakura.** Visit Kamakura gives about 50 minutes from Shinagawa and about 25 minutes from Yokohama on the same JR Yokosuka Line. The [Kamakura article](/articles/kamakura-day-trip) covers the Great Buddha, the Enoden line and the city's visitor guidelines.

**Hakone.** Every Romancecar seat is reserved. The [Hakone article](/articles/hakone-loop) explains the loop by train, cable car, ropeway and boat, and the Hakone Freepass.

**Fuji Five Lakes.** Fuji Kyuko Railway says the Fuji Excursion runs four round trips a day, with one more depending on the season, and that it has no unreserved seats. Fuji Kyuko Bus says highway bus reservations open one month before travel and close 10 minutes before departure. See the [Fuji Five Lakes article](/articles/fuji-five-lakes) for Kawaguchiko and the Chureito Pagoda.

## Kawagoe (Koedo)

Kawagoe is a city in Saitama Prefecture. JNTO says it is called "Koedo Kawagoe", or "Little Edo", for its Edo-period culture and buildings. The Koedo Kawagoe Tourism Association names three main sights: the Kurazukuri townscape, Toki no Kane and Kashiya Yokocho.

- **Kurazukuri.** JNTO describes these as old clay warehouses and merchant homes. Most on Kurazukuri Street now house shops and restaurants.
- **Toki no Kane.** The tourism association calls the bell tower Kawagoe's symbol and says the present building is the fourth generation, after fires destroyed earlier ones. JNTO says it chimes four times a day.
- **Kashiya Yokocho.** JNTO describes a "Candy Alley" of about 20 old shops selling traditional Japanese sweets.
- **History.** The tourism association says Kawagoe Castle was built in 1457 and the town grew as a castle town.

Routes into Kawagoe, as given by the tourism association:

| From | Route | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ikebukuro | Tobu Tojo Line, Kawagoe Limited Express, to Kawagoe Station | 26 minutes at the fastest |
| Shinjuku | JR Saikyo Line rapid, to Kawagoe Station | About 50 minutes |
| Shinjuku (Seibu) | Seibu Shinjuku Line, Limited Express Red Arrow "Koedo", to Hon-Kawagoe Station | As little as 44 minutes |
| Shibuya | Tokyo Metro Fukutoshin Line, to Kawagoe Station | About 45 minutes |

Tobu's own access page gives approximately 30 minutes from Ikebukuro to Kawagoe on the Tobu Tojo Line. JNTO says Tobu sells a Kawagoe Discount Pass for foreign visitors. It covers unlimited rides between three stations and Tobu buses in the city for one day, with discounts at some facilities.

## Yokohama

The Yokohama Convention and Visitors Bureau's official guide describes Yokohama as the first harbour city introduced to the world as the entrance to Japan. Its port opened in 1859. The guide gives the population as 3.7 million, second after Tokyo's 23 wards. JNTO says JR and several private railways run from downtown Tokyo to Yokohama Station in an hour or less.

The official guide's main sightseeing areas include:

- **Minato Mirai 21.** Yokohama Landmark Tower (296 metres), the Red Brick Warehouse and amusement facilities, all within easy walking distance of each other.
- **Yamashita Park and Kannai.** Western-style historic buildings, long-established restaurants and hotels.
- **Yokohama Chinatown.** About 500 Chinese restaurants, grocery stores and other shops, according to the guide.
- **Motomachi and Yamate.** A shopping street, and the Western residential area where foreigners first settled when the port opened.

The Red Brick Warehouse was the customs inspection house in the early 1920s and now holds shops and restaurants. The guide lists opening hours of 10:00 to 19:00 for Building 1 and 11:00 to 20:00 for Building 2; cafes and restaurants vary. It is a 6-minute walk from Bashamichi or Nihon-Odori stations on the Minatomirai Line. The 2026 Christmas Market there runs from 20 November to 25 December 2026, with a regular admission ticket of 500 yen.
