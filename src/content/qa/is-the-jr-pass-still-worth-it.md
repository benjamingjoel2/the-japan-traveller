## What changed

In October 2023 the price of the nationwide Japan Rail Pass rose sharply. Bought online from the official site, a 7-day ordinary pass now costs ¥50,000, a 14-day pass ¥80,000 and a 21-day pass ¥100,000 for adults. At the same time, the fastest trains on the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen (Nozomi and Mizuho) became usable with the pass, but only with a separate special ticket for each ride. Full prices are in the [rail guide](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).

## When it is not worth it

For a first trip that goes Tokyo to Kyoto and back, or Tokyo to Kyoto and flies home from Osaka, the individual Shinkansen tickets cost much less than a 7-day pass. The same is usually true of a 10-day route that adds Hakone (which is on the private Odakyu line, not JR) and Hiroshima.

## When it can be

The pass becomes good value when you cover long distances in a short time: for example Tokyo to Hiroshima and back with Kanazawa and Sendai in the same week, or a route that combines Tohoku, Tokyo and Kyushu. Use the journey times and coverage in the [rail-route table](/rail-routes) to plan the legs.

## How to check

1. List every JR journey you will make in the days the pass would cover.
2. Look up each fare (SmartEX shows Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen fares; the JR company websites show others).
3. Add them up and compare with the pass price. Add the Nozomi special ticket if you want the fastest trains.
4. Compare with the regional JR passes. For a trip that stays in Kansai, Hokuriku or Kyushu, a regional pass is often cheaper.
