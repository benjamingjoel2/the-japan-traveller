Tap water in Japan is safe to drink. This is the position of the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), stated on both its FAQ and its trip planning pages.

## What JNTO says

JNTO's FAQ answers the question directly: "Yes. Tap water in Japan is safe to drink."

Its planning page says that while bottled water is available all over Japan, "the tap water is perfectly safe to drink."

JNTO's drinking water page adds that tap water is "a source of pride" for many of Japan's mountainous regions, where the water comes from the surrounding peaks. It encourages visitors to try it in those areas.

## Water in restaurants

JNTO says it is common for restaurants to serve complimentary water. Its drinking water page says "most places offer tap water for no extra charge. Just ask the staff." For other questions about paying in restaurants, see [Is tipping expected in Japan?](/questions/is-tipping-expected-in-japan) and [Do I need cash in Japan?](/questions/do-i-need-cash-in-japan).

## Bottled water

If you prefer bottled water, JNTO says there are numerous vending machines, and that all convenience stores stock bottled water in various sizes.

## Staying hydrated

JNTO advises keeping hydrated while travelling, "especially in the summer". It warns that if you are not used to the humidity, and even if you are, "it can be very easy to suffer from dehydration." It suggests sports drinks as one way to stay hydrated, alongside plenty of water, and advises keeping some water with you at all times.

For conditions by month, see [Seasons](/guide/seasons) and [Is Japan good in the rainy season?](/questions/is-japan-good-in-the-rainy-season).

## Outdoors

JNTO's pages above cover tap water. They do not cover water from streams, springs or mountain sources. If you are walking or climbing, plan your water from official trail or park information rather than assuming natural water is safe to drink.
