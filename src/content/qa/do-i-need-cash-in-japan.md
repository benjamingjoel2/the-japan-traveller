Cards are widely accepted in Japan, but not everywhere. Official guidance is to carry some cash as well.

## Cards

JNTO says credit, debit and prepaid cards of international brands are "generally accepted throughout the country". Its planning page says cards are usually accepted in most shops, restaurants and taxis in major cities. Some shops do not display the logos of the cards they take, so JNTO suggests asking staff before you pay.

On the railways, JNTO says cards can be used for Narita Express and Shinkansen fares, but "you can't always use them for short distance train/subway fares".

## Where cash still matters

JNTO's currency page describes Japan as "very much a cash-based society" and recommends that you keep some cash on you during your stay. Yen comes in 1,000, 2,000, 5,000 and 10,000 yen notes and 1, 5, 10, 50, 100 and 500 yen coins.

JNTO also notes that ATM fees may be charged depending on the hour or day, and that many banks close at 3pm.

## ATMs that take overseas cards

**Seven Bank.** Seven Bank says you can withdraw yen "day or night" at "over 28,000 ATMs at 7-Eleven stores all across Japan". It also has ATMs at airports including New Chitose, Narita, Haneda, Chubu Centrair, Kansai International, Fukuoka and Naha. Accepted brands include Visa, Plus, Mastercard, Maestro, Cirrus, UnionPay, American Express, JCB, Discover and Diners Club, although Seven Bank says some cards with these marks may not be accepted.

**Japan Post Bank.** Japan Post Bank ATMs accept cards bearing the Visa, Plus, Mastercard, Maestro, Cirrus, JCB, China UnionPay and Discover marks. The bank states:

- an ATM fee of 220 yen (tax included) per use may apply to some overseas cards;
- the single withdrawal limit is 50,000 yen;
- balance enquiries are not available;
- ATMs at branches and post offices open 7am to 11pm Monday to Saturday, and 7am to 9pm on Sundays, holidays, 31 December and 1 to 3 January, with different hours possible over the New Year and Golden Week.

JNTO also lists Mitsubishi UFJ Bank and SMBC as accepting Visa, Mastercard, Maestro or Cirrus cards. Your own card issuer may charge its own fees.

## IC cards as an alternative

JNTO suggests a Suica or PASMO card for travellers who do not want to carry much cash. As well as trains, these cards work on many other kinds of public transport, at convenience stores and at a growing number of shops and restaurants.
