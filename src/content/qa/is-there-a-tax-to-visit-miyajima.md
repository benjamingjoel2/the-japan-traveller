Yes. Miyajima is part of Hatsukaichi City, and the city charges a visit tax on people who enter the island by boat. If you arrive by ferry, it is collected with your fare. The rules below are from the city's own overview page, which is in Japanese.

## How much and since when

- The tax started on 1 October 2023.
- It applies to anyone entering Miyajima by boat.
- The rate is ¥100 per person for each visit.
- Alternatively, you can pay ¥500 per person for one year in advance.

The city describes the tax as a way to cover the costs that large numbers of visitors place on the city, with the aim of tourism that is good both for residents and for visitors.

## How it is collected

- **Passenger ferries and charter boats:** the tax is added to the fare and collected by the boat operator.
- **Private boats using a pier:** the pier manager collects it with the pier fee.
- **Private boats landing elsewhere on the shore:** pay within 10 days of the visit at Hatsukaichi City Hall or its Miyajima or Ono branch offices.
- **The one-year option:** pay at City Hall or the Miyajima or Ono branch offices by the 10th of the month before your first planned visit.

Two ferry companies sail from Miyajimaguchi: the JR ferry and Miyajima Matsudai Kisen. The JR ferry's fare page says its fares do not include the visit tax, which is charged separately when you enter the island. The Japan Rail Pass is accepted on the JR ferry, but the ferry does not say the pass covers the tax, so expect to pay it.

## Who does not pay

The city lists two groups.

Not subject to the tax:

- residents of Miyajima
- people who commute to work in Miyajima for at least 48 hours a month
- students attending schools in Miyajima for at least 48 hours of classes a month

These people show a certificate issued by the city.

Exempt from the tax:

- preschool children
- students (not university students) on school trips or other school events, and their teachers and attendants, with a certificate from the school given to the boat operator
- holders of a physical disability certificate, a rehabilitation (ryoiku) handbook or a mental health and welfare handbook, who must show it. How to buy a ticket without the tax differs by ferry company.
