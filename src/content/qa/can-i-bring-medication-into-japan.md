Most travellers can bring their usual medicines into Japan without paperwork, as long as the amount is small and the medicine is not controlled. The rules are set by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW).

## Amounts allowed without procedures

The MHLW lists these limits for personal use. Within them, you do not need any special procedure:

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Prescription drugs (and poisons and deadly poisons) | up to a 1-month supply |
| External-use drugs (other than the above) | up to 24 per item |
| Injectable drugs and syringes | up to a 1-month supply; pre-filled syringes or self-injection kits only |
| Other drugs and quasi-drugs | up to a 2-month supply |
| Disposable contact lenses | up to a 2-month supply |
| Household medical devices | 1 set |

The MHLW's Q&A says self-injected medicine such as insulin, brought with its syringes, needs no certificate if it is under a 1-month supply. Syringes brought on their own, without medicine, need an Import Confirmation even for one piece.

## The Yunyu Kakunin-sho (Import Confirmation)

If you bring more than these amounts, you need an Import Confirmation, known as the Yunyu Kakunin-sho. The MHLW says you must apply before leaving home and show the certificate to customs if asked.

- **How to apply.** The MHLW's Q&A says applications are made online, and the certificate is sent as a PDF to your registered email address.
- **Documents.** You need a valid prescription or doctor's note showing the patient, the medicine and the doctor's name, for every medicine you apply for. You also need a document from the airline or cruise line showing your arrival date, arrival airport or port, flight or cruise number and name, such as your itinerary.
- **Timing.** The MHLW says the certificate is normally issued "in a few business days", not counting weekends and national holidays, if the form is complete.
- **Do not alter it.** The MHLW warns that a corrected certificate becomes invalid.

## Narcotics, psychotropics and stimulants

Some medicines need separate permission, and some cannot be brought in at all.

- **Prohibited.** The MHLW Narcotics Control Department says no individual traveller may bring in medicines containing methamphetamine or amphetamine, including dexamphetamine and levamfetamine. The MHLW Q&A also names methamphetamine as a prohibited drug.
- **Narcotics and stimulant raw materials.** Examples given by the MHLW include morphine, fentanyl and oxycodone (narcotics), and lisdexamfetamine and pseudoephedrine (stimulant raw materials). These need permission from the Japanese government before you leave home. The Narcotics Control Department says you should apply at least 14 days before you travel.
- **Psychotropics.** Examples listed include methylphenidate, zolpidem and alprazolam. Up to the amounts in the department's table, you need neither a doctor's certificate nor permission.
- **Carry it yourself.** The department says you cannot send these medicines to Japan or have someone else carry them for you.

JNTO adds that cannabis is treated like other drugs. Do not bring it into Japan, even if it is prescribed in your home country.
