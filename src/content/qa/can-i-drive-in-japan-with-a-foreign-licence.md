Most visitors cannot drive in Japan on their home licence alone. What you need depends on where your licence was issued.

## What the National Police Agency accepts

The National Police Agency (NPA) lists three documents that allow you to drive in Japan:

- A Japanese driving licence.
- An International Driving Permit (IDP) governed by the Geneva Convention. The NPA gives drivers from the US, Italy, the UK, Canada, South Korea, Thailand and the Philippines as examples.
- A licence issued in Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco or Taiwan, with a Japanese translation attached.

## International Driving Permits

The NPA says only IDPs issued under the Geneva Convention, by an authorised body of a country that is party to it, are valid in Japan. JAF specifies the 1949 Geneva Convention, and says permits issued under other treaties, such as the 1968 Vienna Convention, are not valid in Japan.

The NPA also says:

- You must carry the original paper permit. A digital version shown on a phone cannot be used.
- You can drive only the vehicle types stamped in the permit's vehicle category columns.

JNTO says you must get your IDP before you leave home, and that card-type and smartphone IDPs are not valid in Japan.

## The translation route

If your licence is from Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco or Taiwan, you can drive with the original licence plus a Japanese translation. The NPA says the translation must be prepared by the consulate of the country that issued the licence, or by a body designated by Japan's National Public Safety Commission. JAF, the Japan Automobile Federation, prepares these translations. It takes applications online only and says processing takes about 2 to 3 business days for these six countries and regions.

## How long you can drive

The NPA says you may drive for up to one year from your date of arrival in Japan, or until your licence expires, whichever is sooner. An IDP is valid for one year from its issue date. JAF says the one-year limit applies even with a Japanese translation, and that police may ask to see your passport to check how many days you have been in Japan.

JNTO says your arrival date is confirmed by the entry stamp in your passport. Automated gates do not give a stamp, so it advises renters to use a staffed gate or ask at the inspection desk.
