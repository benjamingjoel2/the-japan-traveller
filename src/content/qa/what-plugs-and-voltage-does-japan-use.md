Japan's electricity supply is the same voltage everywhere, but the frequency differs between east and west. This answer sets out what the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) says about voltage, frequency, plugs and adapters.

## Voltage and frequency

JNTO says "the voltage used throughout Japan is uniformly 100 volts, A.C."

The frequency is not the same across the country. JNTO says there are two frequencies in use: 50 Hertz in eastern Japan and 60 Hertz in western Japan. It places Nagoya, Kyoto and Osaka in the 60 Hz area. JNTO's FAQ puts it more briefly: the standard frequency is "50Hz or 60Hz, depending on the region."

JNTO does not say exactly where the boundary between the two areas runs, so this answer does not give one. If you have a device that depends on mains frequency, check its label for the range it accepts.

## Plug type

JNTO's FAQ says "power outlets use Type A plugs." Its planning page says "Japan uses two flat parallel prong plugs" and advises buying a plug adapter before you travel. The FAQ adds that if you are travelling from overseas, you may need one.

## Adapters and transformers

Whether your device works on 100 volts is a separate question from the plug shape. JNTO says dual-voltage appliances, such as hair dryers, travel irons and shavers, "should work wherever you are in Japan." For other appliances, "a step-down transformer is required to convert the voltage."

Check the label on your charger or appliance for the voltage it accepts before you plug it in.

JNTO says conversion plugs and transformers are relatively easy to find in urban areas. It names electronics districts such as Akihabara in Tokyo and Nipponbashi in Osaka.

## Charging on the move

JNTO says newer Shinkansen models have power outlets next to the window seats. For rail travel more generally, see [Rail and the JR Pass](/guide/rail-and-the-jr-pass).
